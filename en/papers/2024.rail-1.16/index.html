Abstract

This paper introduces a novel approach to spell checking and correction for low-resource and under-represented languages, with a specific focus on an African language, Wolof. By leveraging the capabilities of transformer models and neural networks, we propose an efficient and practical system capable of correcting typos and improving text quality. Our proposed technique involves training a transformer model on a parallel corpus consisting of misspelled sentences and their correctly spelled counterparts, generated using a semi-automatic method. As we fine tune the model to transform misspelled text into accurate sentences, we demonstrate the immense potential of this approach to overcome the challenges faced by resource-scarce and under-represented languages in the realm of spell checking and correction. Our experimental results and evaluations exhibit promising outcomes, offering valuable insights that contribute to the ongoing endeavors aimed at enriching linguistic diversity and inclusion and thus improving digital communication accessibility for languages grappling with scarcity of resources and under-representation in the digital landscape.

1. Introduction

In recent years, Natural Language Processing (NLP) has made impressive progress in understanding, analyzing and generating human language. Yet, most of this progress is focused on high-resource languages like English, French, and Spanish, leaving low-resource and under-represented languages with limited tools and resources for effective NLP applications. This paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing a novel approach for spell checking and correction in resource-scarce languages. Specifically, we focus on Wolof, an African spoken language that has recently sparked interest in NLP research. We also present a new dataset that can be used for word correction in Wolof. This study contributes to the overarching objective of developing inclusive and effective natural language processing (NLP) tools and resources, in alignment with the ethos of "no language left behind".

Wolof, a Senegambian language primarily spoken in Senegal, Gambia and Mauritania (Diouf et al., 2017), serves as an example of a low-resource language that could greatly benefit from NLP advancements. Despite having over 10 million native speakers (Eberhard et al., 2019), there is a significant lack of digital resources and computational tools for most of (if not all) African languages, among them the Wolof language. As the world increasingly connects through digital platforms, it is vital to ensure robust NLP tools are available for low-resource languages like Wolof. Providing speakers of the language with accurate and effective spell checking and correction systems can enhance linguistic accessibility and promote digital communication across diverse linguistic communities.

Developing spell checkers and correction systems for low-resource languages is difficult due to the limited availability of annotated data, morphological complexity, and the absence of well-established computational resources. Traditional methods like rule-based or dictionary-based systems may not adequately address these challenges, requiring alternative approaches. Deep learning techniques, particularly transformer models, have demonstrated immense potential in various NLP tasks lately. These techniques can learn complex language patterns and generate context-sensitive representations, making them ideal for tackling challenges associated with low-resource language spell checking and correction.

This paper presents a transformer-based model for word correction and spelling in Wolof. Our model is trained on a parallel corpus consisting of misspelled sentences and their error-free counterparts, optimizing the model to translate error-prone text into accurate sentences. Furthermore, we contribute to the advancement of NLP for the Wolof language by creating a new corpus of misspelled sentences and their error-free counterparts. This corpus serves as a benchmark and state-of-the-art in word correction and spelling for Wolof, providing a valuable resource for future research. This resource will facilitate the development of more advanced NLP tools and applications for Wolof.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 reviews related work on the Wolof language and offers an overview of low-resource language spell checking and correction, as well as neural networks and transformer models in NLP. Section 3 details the methodology employed in developing our transformer-based spell checking and correction system. Section 4 presents our evaluation results, including a discussion of the system's performance. In Section 5, we examine the limitations of our system and discuss potential areas for improvement. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper, underlines the implications of our findings and suggests future research directions.

2. Background

2.1. Wolof Language

Wolof is a language belonging to the Senegambian group within the Northern branch of the Atlantic language family, which is part of the broader Niger-Congo language family. It shares strong linguistic connections with Pulaar and Serer languages (Sapir, 1971; Doneux, 1978; Wilson, 1989). The Atlantic language family includes approximately 40 languages, with Pulaar (a dialect of Fula) being the exception, and most are spoken in regions near of the Atlantic coast of Africa. Although Wolof is fundamentally an oral language, its orthography was standardized in 1972 (Robert, 2011).

Descriptive linguistic studies of Wolof can be traced back to the colonial period (Boilat, 1858), while other researches on Wolof morphology and syntax have been conducted by Diagne (1971), Mangold (1977), Church (1981), Dialo (1981), and Ka (1981). In-depth analytical studies of Wolof syntax can be primarily found in the works of Njie (1982) and Dunigan (1994).

Wolof is mainly an aspectual language, focusing on the aspect of an action rather than its tense. This characteristic allows the imperfective marker to combine with various tense markers. The language features a rich verb system, which includes a wide array of basic verbal forms and paradigms. Notably, Mangold (1977) and Church (1981) provide systematic presentations of Wolof verbal paradigms.

In terms of literature and resources, Wolof appears in various forms, such as novels, short story collections, and poetry. However, even in Senegal, it is challenging to find materials written in Wolof. Recent efforts have been made to improve the availability of resources for Wolof speakers. In a study by Gauthier et al. (2016), researchers gathered an Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) dataset for four African languages, including Wolof. This dataset was then used to create the first ASR system for Wolof. Another initiative was proposed by Nguer et al. (2015), who outlined the creation process for the first collaborative online Wolof dictionary. This project was part of the larger Dictionnaires Langues Africaines - Français (DiLAF) project, which has produced dictionaries for seven African languages, including Wolof. However, at the time of writing, all dictionaries are accessible online except for the Wolof one. More recently, Cissé and Sadat (2023) have presented a range of resources for the Wolof language, including a spell checking tool mainly grounded in the language's writing rules.

2.2. Low-Resource Language Spell Checking and Correction

Spell checking and correction for low-resource languages have been of great interest to many researchers. Early approaches often depended on rule-based systems (Armstrong et al., 1995) or statistical methods, such as noisy channel models (Kernighan et al., 1990), n-gram models (Stolcke, 2000), and hidden Markov models (Viterbi, 1967). However, these methods often require substantial linguistic knowledge and annotated data, which may be scarce or non-existent for low-resource languages.

In recent years, researchers have investigated data-driven approaches for low-resource languages, such as unsupervised learning (Soricut and Och, 2015) and bootstrapping techniques (Yarowsky et al., 2001). Some studies have also explored the use of cross-lingual transfer learning (Täckström et al., 2012) or leveraging comparable corpora (Madnani et al., 2012) to enhance spell checking and correction performance in low-resource languages. Nevertheless, these approaches may still be constrained by the availability and quality of parallel and comparable corpora.

2.3. Neural Networks in Spell Checking and Correction

The emergence of deep learning techniques, in particular transformer models (Vaswani et al., 2017) and neural networks, has had a significant impact on the NLP field. These techniques have shown immense potential in a wide range of tasks, including machine translation (Bahdanau et al., 2015), information retrieval, conversational agents, sentiment analysis (Socher et al., 2013), and text summarization (See et al., 2017).

In the context of spell checking and correction, sequence-to-sequence models (Sutskever et al., 2014) have been employed with promising results, using an encoder-decoder architecture to map misspelled sequences to their correct counterparts (Hládek et al., 2019). Attention mechanisms (Bahdanau et al., 2015) have also been integrated into these models to enhance the alignment between input and output sequences (Garg et al., 2019).

The development of transformer models has further advanced the capabilities of neural networks in spell checking and correction. Transformer models, which rely on self-attention mechanisms, have proven effective in capturing long-range dependencies and providing more accurate context-sensitive representations (Devlin et al., 2019). Recent studies have applied transformer models, such as BERT and GPT (Radford and Narasimhan, 2018), to spelling error detection and correction (Sorokin et al., 2016), or fine-tuned them for specific low-resource languages (Al-Ghamdi et al., 2023).

3. Methodology

Our approach consists of three main steps, namely data preparation, model architecture building, and model training configuration.

Initially, we discuss the process of data acquisition and corpus annotation, which is crucial for training an effective model, especially in the context of low-resource languages. Subsequently, we delve into the architecture of the transformer model, detailing its components and design choices. Finally, we describe the training configurations, including the parameters and settings used to train the model.

3.1. Data selection and annotation process

The data acquisition and corpus annotation process encompasses two principal phases. Initially, we identified suitable sources for the corpus data, which were available in various formats (e.g., PDF, text, HTML), and subsequently carried out the extraction of content. Following this, we employed a hybrid approach, incorporating both manual and automatic annotation techniques, and conducted thorough proofreading to generate a corpus of accurately corrected sentences.

3.1.1. Data Selection

The data collection process for our Wolof spell correction study involved gathering data from various sources such as news websites, social media platforms 3 4, religious websites, religious PDF files (Diagne, 1997), bilingual Wolof-French dictionaries (Diouf and Kenkyūjo, 2001; Cissé, 2004) and bilingual Wolof-French corpora released (Adelani et al., 2022; Costa-jussà et al., 2022).

In total, we collected 78,384 sentences for our corpus. During the collection process, we emphasized the quality and diversity of the content, ensuring that our corpus included sentences from various domains and genres.

3.1.2. Corpus annotation

First, we used Python scripts to scrape data from news websites, social media platforms, and religious websites. This process yielded 25,860 sentences from religious websites, 21,341 sentences from social media platforms, and 13,245 sentences from news websites. Next, we extracted 10,087 sentences from religious PDF files and Wolof-French bilingual dictionaries. Additionally, we used the Wolof data from the bilingual Wolof-French corpora released by Masakhane (Adelani et al., 2022) and Facebook (Costa-jussà et al., 2022; Goyal et al., 2022). A substantial number of NLP models have traditionally used tokens as their smallest unit. However, an emerging trend has been noted towards the use of subword units (Sennrich et al., 2016b) as the fundamental building blocks.

The notion of using words as inputs to our model initially appears to be a logical default strategy, mirroring the approach observed in numerous NLP models. However, when applied to spell correction, the token approach can become overly complicated, owing to potential inaccuracies stemming from punctuation use. Additionally, the necessity for NLP models to function on a fixed vocabulary implies that our spell correction tool's vocabulary would need to be comprehensive enough to include every single possible misspelling of every single word encountered during the training process. The implications of this requirement would result in a costly model, both in terms of training and maintenance.

In consideration of these factors, we have decided to use the character as the fundamental building block for our spell checker. This approach has proven to be very effective in translation tasks by Lee et al. (2017). The adoption of character-level segmentation also allows us to preserve a manageable vocabulary size.

For experimental purposes, the overall dataset is divided into three subsets: a training set, a validation set and a test set. We randomly selected 10% of the generated corpus to form the validation and test sets. This was done to make sure that these sets accurately represent the entire dataset. The leftover 90% of the data was then used to create our training set.

3.2. Model architecture

In this study, we employed a customized Transformer model architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) for the task of Wolof spell correction. The Transformer model has demonstrated remarkable success in various natural language processing tasks by leveraging self-attention mechanisms, which allow it to efficiently process input sequences without the need for recurrent or convolutional layers.

Our model consists of two components: an encoder and a decoder, each comprising five identical layers (Biljon et al., 2020). The encoder's primary task is to manage the input sequences containing misspellings, while the decoder focuses on producing output sequences without misspellings, as illustrated in Figure 1.

Figure 1: Transformer model(Vaswani et al., 2017)

During the encoding phase, each input word is converted into a vector representation using an embedding layer. To incorporate positional information into the input embeddings, positional encoding is applied. In both the encoder and decoder components of the model, each layer comprises a multi-head self-attention mechanism with two attention heads. This is followed by position-wise feed-forward networks (FFNs) with a hidden size of 256 and a feed-forward size of 1024.

The self-attention process involves generating query (Q), key (K), and value (V) vectors from the input. These vectors are then used to compute a score matrix by performing matrix multiplication between the query and the key vector. The resulting matrix is scaled by the square root of the key vector dimension (dk). To obtain attention weights, the score matrix is normalized using softmax, representing the importance assigned to different parts of the input sequence. These attention weights are utilized to derive an output vector, as demonstrated in Eq. 1 (Vaswani et al., 2017). To enable efficient training and stable gradients, residual connections and layer normalization are implemented throughout the network.

The decoder includes two multi-headed attention blocks within a single layer: one for the target sequences and another for the encoder's output. The former multi-head attention is masked to prevent computing attention scores for subsequent words. The latter multi-head attention layer employs the encoder's outputs as queries and keys, while the outputs of the first multi-headed attention layer serve as values. This mechanism empowers the decoder to determine the encoder inputs that are most relevant to its generation process, thereby producing an output sequence without any misspellings. The output from the final pointwise feed-forward layer is then forwarded to a linear layer, serving as a classifier, followed by a softmax layer to generate the corrected text.

For initialization, we employ Xavier initialization with a gain of 1.0 (Glorot and Bengio, 2010) for all trainable weights, while the bias terms are initialized with zeros. The embeddings undergo Xavier initialization with a distinct gain of 1.0. To minimize the number of trainable parameters, a common practice is to tie the source and target embeddings, as well as the softmax layer (Press and Wolf, 2017). Since our model operates at the character level, the default vocabulary size is relatively small. We set the embedding dimension to 256 in both the encoder and decoder, which corresponds to the hidden size of the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) for compatibility. Furthermore, we scale the embeddings by the square root of their size.

To address the issue of overfitting, we employ dropout techniques on various Transformer components. Initially, we apply an embedding dropout rate of 10^-1 to the encoder and decoder, which helps in dropping words from the embedding matrix (Gal and Ghahramani, 2016). Furthermore, we apply dropout only within the decoder layers at a rate of 3 × 10^-1 (Srivastava et al., 2014).

3.3. Model Training

The model training procedure was carefully designed, considering various parameters to ensure rigorous and repeatable results.

We employed deterministic training by using a fixed random seed of 42. To optimize the model, we chose the widely used Adam optimizer (Kingma and Ba, 2015), which features adaptive learning rates and momentum-based parameter updates. For the first and second-order moments, we assigned beta values of [9×10^-1, 999×10^-3 ], respectively. The learning rate was initialized at 10^-4, and a minimum threshold of 10^-8 was set to terminate training upon convergence or near-convergence.

To optimize the learning process, we adopted a plateau-based scheduling strategy (Smith, 2017). With a patience value of 5, the learning rate was reduced by a factor of 7 × 10^-1 if the validation score did not improve over five consecutive validation rounds. This dynamic adaptation of the learning rate, based on performance feedback, led to enhanced convergence and optimization.

We facilitated efficient parallel computation during training by using a batch size of 4096 tokens (Ott et al., 2018). The token-based batching approach optimized computational resources by forming batches based on the total number of tokens instead of the number of sentences.

Throughout the entire training process, we placed significant emphasis on the model's ability to generalize and perform well by conducting regular evaluations. To ensure a thorough assessment, we established validation intervals of 2000 updates, covering 50 epochs. We carefully selected this interval, considering that setting a validation frequency that is too high might not provide ample opportunities for the model to learn and improve during validation. Furthermore, excessively frequent validation could lead to extended training times and potentially prematurely terminate the training if the validation patience value is not set high enough. Thus, we decided on the mentioned interval to strike a balance.

Moreover, to enhance our ability to closely track the training process and gain comprehensive insights into the model's development, we implemented a logging frequency of 200 updates.

We implemented early stopping by minimizing our cross-entropy loss function, which is a common approach in model training. Continuously monitoring the loss function allowed us to terminate the training when a new low score was reached, effectively preventing overfitting.

To promote diverse predictions and mitigate overfitting, we incorporated two regularization techniques: label smoothing and weight decay. Specifically, we employed label smoothing with a coefficient of 10^-1 (Szegedy et al., 2016), and weight decay at a rate of 10^-4 (Srivastava et al., 2014). Label smoothing is a regularization method that redistributes the probability weight from reference tokens to other vocabulary tokens. By reducing the overemphasis on specific reference tokens, label smoothing fosters diversity in the model's output and helps prevent overconfidence in predictions. Weight decay, also known as L2 regularization, is a technique used to control the complexity of the model. During training, it reduces the magnitude of the model's weights by adding a penalty term proportional to the weight values to the loss function. This regularization term encourages smaller weight values, preventing the model from overfitting the training data and improving generalization performance.

During training, our primary evaluation metric was the well-established BLEU score (Papineni et al., 2002), which measures the similarity between predicted and reference sequences. For efficient evaluation, we used a token-based batching strategy with a batch size of 1024 tokens.

To manage the length of generated sequences during decoding, we set a maximum output length of 175 tokens. Furthermore, we maintained progress monitoring and validation integrity by consistently printing three validation sentences during each validation run.

4. Evaluations

Evaluating spell-checking and correction systems is a crucial task that will help understand their effectiveness and general applicability. While there is no universally accepted standard for evaluating spell-checking and correction systems, three main methodologies have emerged. These methodologies involve classification metrics, machine translation metrics, and information retrieval metrics.

Classification metrics, such as precision, recall, and F-score, are used to assess the performance of automatic spelling correction systems (Starlander and Popescu-Belis, 2002). Machine Translation metrics, including BLEU score (Papineni et al., 2002), CER or WER (Popović and Ney, 2007), and ChrF++ (Popović, 2015, 2017), are also employed in the evaluation. Additionally, information retrieval metrics like MRR (Mangu et al., 2000) can be used.

Considering that our spell checker operates by translating a source text with errors into its most likely correct form, machine translation metrics are the most suitable for measuring our system's performance. For example, the BLEU metric has been widely used to evaluate spell-checking tools in various studies, including those conducted by researchers like Gerdjikov et al. (2013); Mitankin et al. (2014); Sariev et al. (2014). The WER metric was also used in a study by Evershed and Fitch (2014).

In addition to the performance metrics mentioned above, it is crucial to conduct a comprehensive error analysis to gain deeper insights into the behavior of our spell checker. An examination of errors on a subset of the test data has revealed three primary categories of recurring errors produced by our model.

The first group of errors revolves around the correction of long vowels in words. In the Wolof language, distinguishing between long and short vowels significantly impacts word meanings. However, our model consistently struggles to accurately determine when to substitute a short vowel with a long one, resulting in incorrect corrections.

The second group of errors is related to named entities. Named entities, which often deviate from standard Wolof writing conventions, introduce considerable confusion for the model. In some instances, the model incorrectly assumes that these named entities are erroneous and attempts to rectify them. In other cases, when specific named entities are indeed misspelled and not part of the vocabulary, the model suggests incorrect corrections.

The third group of errors is associated with accent management. Accents play a crucial role in distinguishing and pronouncing words in Wolof. Our model consistently faces challenges when accurately identifying and reinstating missing accents in words.

These findings underscore the need for further refinement of our spell checker, particularly in addressing the complexities of vowel length, handling named entities, and preserving accents within the Wolof language. Moreover, it is essential to explore potential solutions for mitigating these recurring errors, such as incorporating contextual language comprehension and enhancing the model's ability to discern linguistic nuances.

4.2. Test of significance

To establish the statistical significance of the results derived from our evaluation of the spell checker, we conducted a significance test, comparing our model against the sole existing Wolof spell checker accessible online. The objective of this test is to determine the robustness of the observed performance metrics, ensuring that they are not merely a product of random chance.

Our initial step involved the random selection of 100 Wolof sentences from our constructed corpus. Following this preliminary stage, each chosen sentence was input into both correction systems to observe and analyze the proposed corrections.

Subsequently, the correction proposals generated by both systems underwent evaluation by a native Wolof speaker. The evaluator was kept unaware of the source of each correction to maintain impartiality. The applied grading system was as follows: a score of "3" was assigned to sentences that were perfectly corrected and aligned with the reference sentence. A score of "2" was reserved for corrections that, despite minor errors, preserved the original sentence's intended meaning. Lastly, a score of "1" was given to corrections that were entirely incorrect or inadequate.

The Wilcoxon test, initially introduced by Wilcoxon (1945), represents a non-parametric approach widely employed for comparing two paired samples. This method is particularly useful when assumptions regarding data distribution are not met or when dealing with ordinal data. The p-value reflects the likelihood of obtaining such a pronounced difference between the two systems purely by chance, assuming the null hypothesis to be valid. 5. Limitations

Our spell checking system has demonstrated good performance, as indicated by its high BLEU and ChrF++ scores, as well as the relatively low WER and CER scores. However, there are still limitations that require further investigations and improvements.

Firstly, character-level models, such as the one used in this study, are inherently complex and can be time consuming to train. This is due to the larger sequence of data they need to learn from, compared to word-level models. The computational cost of training such models can be particularly high when working with large datasets or languages with extensive character sets.

Secondly, our model may struggle with capturing long-range dependencies within the text. The dependencies between words in a sentence, which often span across several characters, can be difficult for character-level models to understand. This could potentially affect the model's performance in cases that require a deep understanding of sentence-level semantics.

Thirdly, our model lacks the advantage of leveraging pre-trained word embeddings, which capture semantic and syntactic relationships between words. As a result, the model's semantic understanding may be less nuanced compared to models that operate at the word level.

Fourthly, character-level models can be more sensitive to noise in the input data. Spelling errors, inconsistent punctuation usage, and other forms of noise can have a more significant impact on these models, which could lead to lower performance in certain situations.

Additionally, while our model is designed to handle any language that utilizes an alphabet similar to that of the Wolof language, it may struggle with languages that rely heavily on word order. This is due to the model's lack of word-level understanding, which could help in these situations.

Lastly, our model may face difficulties with disambiguation. For instance, words spelled the same but with different meanings can pose a challenge for character-level models, as these models lack access to word-level semantic information.

Given these considerations, there are several areas that could be targeted for improvement. Firstly, the model could be further trained on a wider variety of textual data in order to improve its capacity to handle of less common or more complex errors. Given our current focus on a language with limited available resources, the use of the back-translation technique emerges as a promising strategy. This approach has consistently demonstrated its effectiveness in various domains, such as Statistical Machine Translation (SMT) (Bojar and Tamchyna, 2011), supervised Neural Machine Translation (Sennrich et al., 2016a), and unsupervised Machine Translation (Lample et al., 2017).

In the context of spell-checking and correction, adopting this approach would involve training a model to intentionally introduce a substantial number of realistic spelling errors within clean text. Subsequently, the resulting corpus of corrupted text can be employed to refine our spell checking model.

Furthermore, we suggest further exploration of hybrid models that combine the benefits of both character-level and word-level processing. Such models could potentially leverage the granularity of character-level models while still maintaining a higher-level understanding of word and sentence semantics.

Lastly, considering the computational expenses associated with character-level models, it would be beneficial to conduct research on more efficient training methods. By doing so, we can mitigate the computational burden and improve the overall efficiency of the training process.

6. Conclusion

The present study represents significant progress in the field of automatic spelling correction, particularly for under-resourced and under-represented spoken languages. Our model, which utilizes a transformer-based architecture has produced encouraging results across several evaluation metrics, including BLEU, WER, CER and ChrF++. These outcomes highlight the potential of advanced deep learning techniques to overcome the challenge of spelling errors, even in languages with limited available data.

Despite these promising results, our work has also highlighted certain areas of improvement that could further refine the performance of the proposed system. Our model, being character-level, exhibits certain limitations such as computational complexity, difficulty in capturing long-range dependencies, and sensitivity to noise in the input data. Moreover, the lack of word-level understanding could lead to potential difficulties with languages that heavily rely on word order or face challenges with disambiguation. Furthermore, the investigation of hybrid models, combining the benefits of both character-level and word-level processing, could be a promising direction for future work.

We hope that our findings will encourage further research in this direction, ultimately contributing to the broader goal of building inclusive and effective natural language processing tools for all languages.

Abstract

By leveraging the capabilities of transformer models and neural networks, we propose an efficient and practical system capable of correcting typos and improving text quality. Our experimental results and evaluations exhibit promising outcomes, offering valuable insights that contribute to the ongoing endeavors aimed at enriching linguistic diversity and inclusion and thus improving digital communication accessibility for languages grappling with scarcity of resources and under-representation in the digital landscape.

2. Background

2. Contexte

2.1. Wolof Language

2.1. Langue wolof

Wolof is a language belonging to the Senegambian group within the Northern branch of the Atlantic language family, which is part of the broader Niger-Congo language family. It shares strong linguistic connections with Pulaar and Serer languages (Sapir, 1971; Doneux, 1978; Wilson, 1989). The Atlantic language family includes approximately 40 languages, with Pulaar (a dialect of Fula) being the exception, and most are spoken in regions near of the Atlantic coast of Africa. Although Wolof is fundamentally an oral language, its orthography was standardized in 1972 (Robert, 2011).

Le wolof est une langue appartenant au groupe sénégambien de la branche nord de la famille des langues atlantiques, qui fait partie de la famille plus large des langues nigéro-congolaises. Il partage de fortes connexions linguistiques avec les langues Pulaar et Serer (Sapir, 1971; Doneux, 1978; Wilson, 1989). La famille des langues de l'Atlantique comprend environ 40 langues, le pulaar (un dialecte de Fula) étant l'exception, et la plupart sont parlées dans les régions proches de la côte atlantique de l'Afrique. Bien que le wolof soit fondamentalement une langue orale, son orthographe a été standardisée en 1972 (Robert, 2011).

Descriptive linguistic studies of Wolof can be traced back to the colonial period (Boilat, 1858), while other researches on Wolof morphology and syntax have been conducted by Diagne (1971), Mangold (1977), Church (1981), Dialo (1981), and Ka (1981). In-depth analytical studies of Wolof syntax can be primarily found in the works of Njie (1982) and Dunigan (1994).

Les études linguistiques descriptives du wolof remontent à la période coloniale (Boilat, 1858), tandis que d'autres recherches sur la morphologie et la syntaxe du wolof ont été menées par Diagne (1971), Mangold (1977), Church (1981), Dialo (1981), et Ka (1981). Les études analytiques approfondies de la syntaxe wolof se trouvent principalement dans les travaux de Njie (1982) et Dunigan (1994).

Wolof is mainly an aspectual language, focusing on the aspect of an action rather than its tense. This characteristic allows the imperfective marker to combine with various tense markers. The language features a rich verb system, which includes a wide array of basic verbal forms and paradigms. Notably, Mangold (1977) and Church (1981) provide systematic presentations of Wolof verbal paradigms.

Le wolof est principalement une langue aspectuelle, se concentrant sur l'aspect d'une action plutôt que sur son temps. Cette caractéristique permet au marqueur imperfectif de se combiner avec divers marqueurs de temps. La langue dispose d'un système de verbes riches, qui comprend un large éventail de formes et de paradigmes verbaux de base. Notamment, Mangold (1977) et Church (1981) fournissent des présentations systématiques des paradigmes verbaux wolofs.

In terms of literature and resources, Wolof appears in various forms, such as novels, short story collections, and poetry. However, even in Senegal, it is challenging to find materials written in Wolof. Recent efforts have been made to improve the availability of resources for Wolof speakers. In a study by Gauthier et al. (2016), researchers gathered an Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) dataset for four African languages, including Wolof. This dataset was then used to create the first ASR system for Wolof. Another initiative was proposed by Nguer et al. Spell checking and correction for low-resource languages have been of great interest to many researchers. La vérification et la correction orthographiques pour les langues à faibles ressources ont été d'un grand intérêt pour de nombreux chercheurs. Cependant, ces méthodes nécessitent souvent des connaissances linguistiques substantielles et des données annotées, qui peuvent être rares ou inexistantes pour les langues à faibles ressources.

In recent years, researchers have investigated data-driven approaches for low-resource languages, such as unsupervised learning (Soricut and Och, 2015) and bootstrapping techniques (Yarowsky et al., 2001). 3.2. Model architecture

3.2. Architecture du modèle

In this study, we employed a customized Transformer model architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) for the task of Wolof spell correction. The Transformer model has demonstrated remarkable success in various natural language processing tasks by leveraging self-attention mechanisms, which allow it to efficiently process input sequences without the need for recurrent or convolutional layers.

Dans cette étude, nous avons utilisé une architecture de modèle Transformer personnalisée (Vaswani et al., 2017) pour la tâche de correction orthographique du wolof. Le modèle Transformer a démontré un succès remarquable dans diverses tâches de traitement du langage naturel en tirant parti des mécanismes d'auto-attention, qui lui permettent de traiter efficacement les séquences d'entrée sans avoir besoin de couches récurrentes ou convolutionnelles.

Our model consists of two components: an encoder and a decoder, each comprising five identical layers (Biljon et al., 2020). The encoder's primary task is to manage the input sequences containing misspellings, while the decoder focuses on producing output sequences without misspellings, as illustrated in Figure 1.

Notre modèle se compose de deux composantes : un encodeur et un décodeur, chacun comprenant cinq couches identiques (Biljon et al., 2020). La tâche principale de l'encodeur est de gérer les séquences d'entrée contenant des fautes d'orthographe, tandis que le décodeur se concentre sur la production de séquences de sortie sans fautes d'orthographe, comme illustré à la figure 1.

During the encoding phase, each input word is converted into a vector representation using an embedding layer. To incorporate positional information into the input embeddings, positional encoding is applied. In both the encoder and decoder components of the model, each layer comprises a multi-head self-attention mechanism with two attention heads. This is followed by position-wise feed-forward networks (FFNs) with a hidden size of 256 and a feed-forward size of 1024.

Au cours de la phase d'encodage, chaque mot d'entrée est converti en une représentation vectorielle à l'aide d'une couche de plongement. Pour incorporer l'information positionnelle dans les plongements d'entrée, un encodage positionnel est appliqué. Dans les composants encodeur et décodeur du modèle, chaque couche comprend un mécanisme d'auto-attention multi-têtes avec deux têtes d'attention. Ceci est suivi de réseaux à propagation avant positionnels (FFN) avec une taille cachée de 256 et une taille de propagation avant de 1024.

The self-attention process involves generating query (Q), key (K), and value (V) vectors from the input. These vectors are then used to compute a score matrix by performing matrix multiplication between the query and the key vector. The resulting matrix is scaled by the square root of the key vector dimension (dk). To obtain attention weights, the score matrix is normalized using softmax, representing the importance assigned to different parts of the input sequence. These attention weights are utilized to derive an output vector, as demonstrated in Eq. 1 (Vaswani et al., 2017). To enable efficient training and stable gradients, residual connections and layer normalization are implemented throughout the network.

Le processus d'auto-attention implique la génération de vecteurs de requête (Q), de clé (K) et de valeur (V) à partir de l'entrée. Ces vecteurs sont ensuite utilisés pour calculer une matrice de scores en effectuant une multiplication matricielle entre la requête et le vecteur clé. La matrice résultante est mise à l'échelle par la racine carrée de la dimension du vecteur clé (dk). Pour obtenir des pondérations d'attention, la matrice de scores est normalisée en utilisant softmax, représentant l'importance attribuée aux différentes parties de la séquence d'entrée. Ces poids d'attention sont utilisés pour dériver un vecteur de sortie, comme démontré dans l'équation 1 (Vaswani et al., 2017). Pour permettre un entraînement efficace et des gradients stables, des connexions résiduelles et une normalisation de couche sont mises en œuvre dans tout le réseau.

The decoder includes two multi-headed attention blocks within a single layer: one for the target sequences and another for the encoder's output. The former multi-head attention is masked to prevent computing attention scores for subsequent words. The latter multi-head attention layer employs the encoder's outputs as queries and keys, while the outputs of the first multi-headed attention layer serve as values. This mechanism empowers the decoder to determine the encoder inputs that are most relevant to its generation process, thereby producing an output sequence without any misspellings. The output from the final pointwise feed-forward layer is then forwarded to a linear layer, serving as a classifier, followed by a softmax layer to generate the corrected text.

Le décodeur comprend deux blocs d'attention multi-têtes au sein d'une seule couche : un pour les séquences cibles et un autre pour la sortie de l'encodeur. L'attention multi-tête précédente est masquée pour empêcher le calcul des scores d'attention pour les mots suivants. Cette dernière couche d'attention multi-têtes utilise les sorties de l'encodeur comme requêtes et clés, tandis que les sorties de la première couche d'attention multi-têtes servent de valeurs. Ce mécanisme permet au décodeur de déterminer les entrées de l'encodeur qui sont les plus pertinentes pour son processus de génération, produisant ainsi une séquence de sortie sans fautes d'orthographe. La sortie de la couche de feed-forward pointwise finale est ensuite transmise à une couche linéaire, servant de classificateur, suivie d'une couche softmax pour générer le texte corrigé.

For initialization, we employ Xavier initialization with a gain of 1.0 (Glorot and Bengio, 2010) for all trainable weights, while the bias terms are initialized with zeros. The embeddings undergo Xavier initialization with a distinct gain of 1.0. To minimize the number of trainable parameters, a common practice is to tie the source and target embeddings, as well as the softmax layer (Press and Wolf, 2017). Since our model operates at the character level, the default vocabulary size is relatively small. We set the embedding dimension to 256 in both the encoder and decoder, which corresponds to the hidden size of the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) for compatibility. Furthermore, we scale the embeddings by the square root of their size.

Pour l'initialisation, nous employons l'initialisation Xavier avec un gain de 1.0 (Glorot et Bengio, 2010) pour tous les poids entraînables, tandis que les termes de biais sont initialisés avec des zéros. Les plongements subissent une initialisation Xavier avec un gain distinct de 1,0. Pour minimiser le nombre de paramètres entraînables, une pratique courante consiste à lier les plongements source et cible, ainsi que la couche softmax (Press et Wolf, 2017). Puisque notre modèle fonctionne au niveau des caractères, la taille du vocabulaire par défaut est relativement petite. Nous fixons la dimension de plongement à 256 à la fois dans l'encodeur et le décodeur, ce qui correspond à la taille cachée du Réseau de Transfert Direct (FFN) pour compatibilité. De plus, nous mettons à l'échelle les plongements par la racine carrée de leur taille.

To address the issue of overfitting, we employ dropout techniques on various Transformer components. Initially, we apply an embedding dropout rate of 10^-1 to the encoder and decoder, which helps in dropping words from the embedding matrix (Gal and Ghahramani, 2016). Furthermore, we apply dropout only within the decoder layers at a rate of 3 × 10^-1 (Srivastava et al., 2014).

Pour remédier au problème de surapprentissage, nous utilisons des techniques de dropout sur divers composants Transformer. Initialement, nous appliquons un taux de dropout de plongement de 10-1 à l'encodeur et au décodeur, ce qui aide à éliminer des mots de la matrice de plongement (Gal et Ghahramani, 2016). De plus, nous appliquons le dropout uniquement au sein des couches du décodeur à un taux de 3 × 10^-1 (Srivastava et al., 2014).

3.3. Entraînement du modèle

The model training procedure was carefully designed, considering various parameters to ensure rigorous and repeatable results.

La procédure d'entraînement du modèle a été soigneusement conçue, en tenant compte de divers paramètres pour garantir des résultats rigoureux et reproductibles.

We employed deterministic training by using a fixed random seed of 42. To optimize the model, we chose the widely used Adam optimizer (Kingma and Ba, 2015), which features adaptive learning rates and momentum-based parameter updates. For the first and second-order moments, we assigned beta values of [9×10^-1, 999×10^-3 ], respectively. The learning rate was initialized at 10^-4, and a minimum threshold of 10^-8 was set to terminate training upon convergence or near-convergence.

Nous avons utilisé un entraînement déterministe en utilisant une graine aléatoire fixe de 42. Pour optimiser le modèle, nous avons choisi l'optimiseur largement utilisé Adam (Kingma et Ba, 2015), qui comporte des taux d'apprentissage adaptatifs et des mises à jour de paramètres basées sur le momentum. Pour les moments du premier et du second ordre, nous avons attribué des valeurs bêta de [9×10^-1, 999×10^-3], respectivement. Le taux d'apprentissage a été initialisé à 10^-4, et un seuil minimum de 10^-8 a été défini pour terminer l'entraînement en cas de convergence ou de quasi-convergence.

To optimize the learning process, we adopted a plateau-based scheduling strategy (Smith, 2017). With a patience value of 5, the learning rate was reduced by a factor of 7 × 10^-1 if the validation score did not improve over five consecutive validation rounds. This dynamic adaptation of the learning rate, based on performance feedback, led to enhanced convergence and optimization.

Pour optimiser le processus d'apprentissage, nous avons adopté une stratégie de planification basée sur les plateaux (Smith, 2017). Avec une valeur de patience de 5, le taux d'apprentissage était réduit d'un facteur de 7 × 10^-1 si le score de validation ne s'améliorait pas au cours de cinq cycles de validation consécutifs. Cette adaptation dynamique du taux d'apprentissage, basée sur le retour de performance, a conduit à une convergence et une optimisation améliorées.

We facilitated efficient parallel computation during training by using a batch size of 4096 tokens (Ott et al., 2018). The token-based batching approach optimized computational resources by forming batches based on the total number of tokens instead of the number of sentences.

Nous avons facilité un calcul parallèle efficace pendant l'entraînement en utilisant une taille de lot de 4096 tokens (Ott et al., 2018). L'approche de traitement par lots basée sur les jetons a optimisé les ressources de calcul en formant des lots basés sur le nombre total de jetons plutôt que sur le nombre de phrases.

Throughout the entire training process, we placed significant emphasis on the model's ability to generalize and perform well by conducting regular evaluations. To ensure a thorough assessment, we established validation intervals of 2000 updates, covering 50 epochs. We carefully selected this interval, considering that setting a validation frequency that is too high might not provide ample opportunities for the model to learn and improve during validation. Furthermore, excessively frequent validation could lead to extended training times and potentially prematurely terminate the training if the validation patience value is not set high enough. Thus, we decided on the mentioned interval to strike a balance.

Tout au long du processus d'entraînement, nous avons accordé une importance significative à la capacité du modèle à généraliser et à bien performer en effectuant des évaluations régulières. Pour assurer une évaluation approfondie, nous avons établi des intervalles de validation de 2000 mises à jour, couvrant 50 époques. Nous avons soigneusement sélectionné cet intervalle, en tenant compte du fait qu'une fréquence de validation trop élevée pourrait ne pas offrir suffisamment d'opportunités au modèle d'apprendre et de s'améliorer pendant la validation. En outre, une validation trop fréquente pourrait entraîner des temps d'entraînement prolongés et potentiellement mettre fin prématurément à l'entraînement si la valeur de patience de validation n'est pas fixée suffisamment haut. Ainsi, nous avons décidé sur l'intervalle mentionné pour trouver un équilibre.

Moreover, to enhance our ability to closely track the training process and gain comprehensive insights into the model's development, we implemented a logging frequency of 200 updates.

De plus, afin d'améliorer notre capacité à suivre de près le processus d'entraînement et à obtenir des informations complètes sur le développement du modèle, nous avons mis en œuvre une fréquence de journalisation de 200 mises à jour.

We implemented early stopping by minimizing our cross-entropy loss function, which is a common approach in model training. Continuously monitoring the loss function allowed us to terminate the training when a new low score was reached, effectively preventing overfitting.

Nous avons mis en œuvre l'arrêt précoce en minimisant notre fonction de perte d'entropie croisée, une approche courante dans l'entraînement des modèles. La surveillance continue de la fonction de perte nous a permis de mettre fin à l'entraînement lorsqu'un nouveau score bas était atteint, empêchant ainsi efficacement le surapprentissage.

To promote diverse predictions and mitigate overfitting, we incorporated two regularization techniques: label smoothing and weight decay. Specifically, we employed label smoothing with a coefficient of 10^-1 (Szegedy et al., 2016), and weight decay at a rate of 10^-4 (Srivastava et al., 2014). Label smoothing is a regularization method that redistributes the probability weight from reference tokens to other vocabulary tokens. By reducing the overemphasis on specific reference tokens, label smoothing fosters diversity in the model's output and helps prevent overconfidence in predictions. Weight decay, also known as L2 regularization, is a technique used to control the complexity of the model. During training, it reduces the magnitude of the model's weights by adding a penalty term proportional to the weight values to the loss function. This regularization term encourages smaller weight values, preventing the model from overfitting the training data and improving generalization performance.

Pour favoriser des prédictions diverses et atténuer le surapprentissage, nous avons intégré deux techniques de régularisation : le lissage des étiquettes et la décroissance des poids. Plus précisément, nous avons utilisé le lissage d'étiquettes avec un coefficient de 10-1 (Szegedy et al., 2016), et la décroissance des poids à un taux de 10-4 (Srivastava et al., 2014). Le lissage d'étiquettes est une méthode de régularisation qui redistribue le poids de probabilité des tokens de référence vers d'autres tokens du vocabulaire. En réduisant l'accent excessif sur les jetons de référence spécifiques, le lissage des étiquettes favorise la diversité dans la production du modèle et aide à prévenir l'excès de confiance dans les prédictions. La décroissance des poids, également connue sous le nom de régularisation L2, est une technique utilisée pour contrôler la complexité du modèle. Pendant l'entraînement, elle réduit l'ampleur des poids du modèle en ajoutant à la fonction de perte un terme de pénalité proportionnel aux valeurs des poids. Ce terme de régularisation encourage des valeurs de poids plus petites, empêchant le modèle de surapprendre les données d'entraînement et améliorant les performances de généralisation.

During training, our primary evaluation metric was the well-established BLEU score (Papineni et al., 2002), which measures the similarity between predicted and reference sequences. For efficient evaluation, we used a token-based batching strategy with a batch size of 1024 tokens.

Pendant l'entraînement, notre métrique d'évaluation principale était le score BLEU bien établi (Papineni et al., 2002), qui mesure la similarité entre les séquences prédites et de référence. Pour une évaluation efficace, nous avons utilisé une stratégie de regroupement basée sur les tokens avec une taille de lot de 1024 tokens.

To manage the length of generated sequences during decoding, we set a maximum output length of 175 tokens. Furthermore, we maintained progress monitoring and validation integrity by consistently printing three validation sentences during each validation run.

Pour gérer la longueur des séquences générées lors du décodage, nous avons fixé une longueur maximale de sortie de 175 jetons. 5. Limitations

5. Limites

Our spell checking system has demonstrated good performance, as indicated by its high BLEU and ChrF++ scores, as well as the relatively low WER and CER scores. However, there are still limitations that require further investigations and improvements.

Notre système de correction orthographique a démontré de bonnes performances, comme l'indiquent ses scores élevés BLEU et ChrF++, ainsi que les scores relativement faibles WER et CER. Cependant, il existe encore des limitations qui nécessitent des investigations et des améliorations supplémentaires.

Firstly, character-level models, such as the one used in this study, are inherently complex and can be time consuming to train. This is due to the larger sequence of data they need to learn from, compared to word-level models. The computational cost of training such models can be particularly high when working with large datasets or languages with extensive character sets.

Tout d'abord, les modèles de niveau de caractère, tels que celui utilisé dans cette étude, sont intrinsèquement complexes et peuvent être longs à entraîner. Cela est dû à la plus grande séquence de données dont ils ont besoin pour apprendre, comparé aux modèles de niveau de mot. Le coût de calcul de l'entraînement de tels modèles peut être particulièrement élevé lorsqu'on travaille avec de grands jeux de données ou des langues ayant des ensembles de caractères étendus.

Secondly, our model may struggle with capturing long-range dependencies within the text. The dependencies between words in a sentence, which often span across several characters, can be difficult for character-level models to understand. This could potentially affect the model's performance in cases that require a deep understanding of sentence-level semantics.

Deuxièmement, notre modèle peut avoir du mal à capturer les dépendances à longue portée dans le texte. Les dépendances entre les mots d'une phrase, qui s'étendent souvent sur plusieurs caractères, peuvent être difficiles à comprendre pour les modèles de niveau caractère. Cela pourrait potentiellement affecter les performances du modèle dans les cas qui nécessitent une compréhension approfondie de la sémantique au niveau de la phrase.

Thirdly, our model lacks the advantage of leveraging pre-trained word embeddings, which capture semantic and syntactic relationships between words. As a result, the model's semantic understanding may be less nuanced compared to models that operate at the word level.

Troisièmement, notre modèle ne bénéficie pas de l'avantage de tirer parti des plongements lexicaux pré-entraînés, qui capturent les relations sémantiques et syntaxiques entre les mots. En conséquence, la compréhension sémantique du modèle peut être moins nuancée par rapport aux modèles qui fonctionnent au niveau du mot.

Fourthly, character-level models can be more sensitive to noise in the input data. Spelling errors, inconsistent punctuation usage, and other forms of noise can have a more significant impact on these models, which could lead to lower performance in certain situations.

Quatrièmement, les modèles au niveau des caractères peuvent être plus sensibles au bruit dans les données d'entrée. Les fautes d'orthographe, l'utilisation incohérente de la ponctuation et d'autres formes de bruit peuvent avoir un impact plus important sur ces modèles, ce qui pourrait entraîner une baisse des performances dans certaines situations.

Additionally, while our model is designed to handle any language that utilizes an alphabet similar to that of the Wolof language, it may struggle with languages that rely heavily on word order. This is due to the model's lack of word-level understanding, which could help in these situations.

De plus, bien que notre modèle soit conçu pour gérer toute langue utilisant un alphabet similaire à celui de la langue wolofe, il pourrait avoir des difficultés avec les langues qui dépendent fortement de l'ordre des mots. Cela est dû au manque de compréhension du modèle au niveau des mots, ce qui pourrait aider dans ces situations.

Lastly, our model may face difficulties with disambiguation. For instance, words spelled the same but with different meanings can pose a challenge for character-level models, as these models lack access to word-level semantic information.

Enfin, notre modèle peut rencontrer des difficultés avec la désambiguïsation. Par exemple, les mots orthographiés de la même manière mais ayant des significations différentes peuvent poser un défi pour les modèles de niveau caractère, car ces modèles n'ont pas accès aux informations sémantiques de niveau mot.

Given these considerations, there are several areas that could be targeted for improvement. Firstly, the model could be further trained on a wider variety of textual data in order to improve its capacity to handle of less common or more complex errors. Given our current focus on a language with limited available resources, the use of the back-translation technique emerges as a promising strategy. This approach has consistently demonstrated its effectiveness in various domains, such as Statistical Machine Translation (SMT) (Bojar and Tamchyna, 2011), supervised Neural Machine Translation (Sennrich et al., 2016a), and unsupervised Machine Translation (Lample et al., 2017).

Compte tenu de ces considérations, il existe plusieurs domaines qui pourraient être ciblés pour une amélioration. Tout d'abord, le modèle pourrait être davantage entraîné sur une plus grande variété de données textuelles afin d'améliorer sa capacité à gérer des erreurs moins courantes ou plus complexes. Étant donné que nous nous concentrons actuellement sur une langue dont les ressources disponibles sont limitées, l’utilisation de la technique de rétrotraduction apparaît comme une stratégie prometteuse. Cette approche a constamment démontré son efficacité dans divers domaines, tels que la traduction automatique statistique (SMT) (Bojar et Tamchyna, 2011), la traduction automatique neuronale supervisée (Sennrich et al., 2016a) et la traduction automatique non supervisée (Lample et al., 2017).

In the context of spell-checking and correction, adopting this approach would involve training a model to intentionally introduce a substantial number of realistic spelling errors within clean text. Subsequently, the resulting corpus of corrupted text can be employed to refine our spell checking model.

Dans le contexte de la vérification orthographique et de la correction, l'adoption de cette approche impliquerait l'entraînement d'un modèle pour introduire intentionnellement un nombre important d'erreurs d'orthographe réalistes dans le texte propre. Par la suite, le corpus résultant de texte corrompu peut être utilisé pour affiner notre modèle de correction orthographique.

Furthermore, we suggest further exploration of hybrid models that combine the benefits of both character-level and word-level processing. Such models could potentially leverage the granularity of character-level models while still maintaining a higher-level understanding of word and sentence semantics.

De plus, nous suggérons d'explorer davantage les modèles hybrides qui combinent les avantages du traitement au niveau des caractères et au niveau des mots. De tels modèles pourraient potentiellement tirer parti de la granularité des modèles au niveau des caractères tout en maintenant une compréhension de plus haut niveau de la sémantique des mots et des phrases.

Lastly, considering the computational expenses associated with character-level models, it would be beneficial to conduct research on more efficient training methods. By doing so, we can mitigate the computational burden and improve the overall efficiency of the training process.

Enfin, en tenant compte des coûts computationnels associés aux modèles de niveau caractère, il serait bénéfique de mener des recherches sur des méthodes d'entraînement plus efficaces. En procédant ainsi, nous pouvons atténuer la charge computationnelle et améliorer l'efficacité globale du processus d'entraînement.

The present study represents significant progress in the field of automatic spelling correction, particularly for under-resourced and under-represented spoken languages. Our model, which utilizes a transformer-based architecture has produced encouraging results across several evaluation metrics, including BLEU, WER, CER and ChrF++. These outcomes highlight the potential of advanced deep learning techniques to overcome the challenge of spelling errors, even in languages with limited available data.

La présente étude représente une avancée significative dans le domaine de la correction automatique de l'orthographe, en particulier pour les langues parlées sous-dotées et sous-représentées. Notre modèle, qui utilise une architecture basée sur les transformateurs, a produit des résultats encourageants selon plusieurs métriques d'évaluation, y compris BLEU, WER, CER et ChrF++. Despite these promising results, our work has also highlighted certain areas of improvement that could further refine the performance of the proposed system. Our model, being character-level, exhibits certain limitations such as computational complexity, difficulty in capturing long-range dependencies, and sensitivity to noise in the input data. Moreover, the lack of word-level understanding could lead to potential difficulties with languages that heavily rely on word order or face challenges with disambiguation. Furthermore, the investigation of hybrid models, combining the benefits of both character-level and word-level processing, could be a promising direction for future work.

Malgré ces résultats prometteurs, notre travail a également mis en évidence certains domaines d'amélioration qui pourraient affiner davantage la performance du système proposé. Notre modèle, étant au niveau des caractères, présente certaines limites telles que la complexité computationnelle, la difficulté à capturer les dépendances à long terme et la sensibilité au bruit dans les données d'entrée. De plus, l'absence de compréhension au niveau du mot pourrait entraîner des difficultés potentielles avec les langues qui dépendent fortement de l'ordre des mots ou rencontrent des défis de désambiguïsation. En outre, l'investigation de modèles hybrides, combinant les avantages du traitement de niveau caractère et de niveau mot, pourrait être une direction prometteuse pour les travaux futurs.

We hope that our findings will encourage further research in this direction, ultimately contributing to the broader goal of building inclusive and effective natural language processing tools for all languages.

Nous espérons que nos résultats encourageront des recherches supplémentaires dans cette direction, contribuant ainsi, en fin de compte, à l'objectif plus large de construction d'outils de traitement automatique des langues naturelles inclusifs et efficaces pour toutes les langues.

Abstract

Muhtasari

This paper introduces a novel approach to spell checking and correction for low-resource and under-represented languages, with a specific focus on an African language, Wolof. By leveraging the capabilities of transformer models and neural networks, we propose an efficient and practical system capable of correcting typos and improving text quality. Our proposed technique involves training a transformer model on a parallel corpus consisting of misspelled sentences and their correctly spelled counterparts, generated using a semi-automatic method. As we fine tune the model to transform misspelled text into accurate sentences, we demonstrate the immense potential of this approach to overcome the challenges faced by resource-scarce and under-represented languages in the realm of spell checking and correction. Our experimental results and evaluations exhibit promising outcomes, offering valuable insights that contribute to the ongoing endeavors aimed at enriching linguistic diversity and inclusion and thus improving digital communication accessibility for languages grappling with scarcity of resources and under-representation in the digital landscape.

1. Introduction

1. Utangulizi

In recent years, Natural Language Processing (NLP) has made impressive progress in understanding, analyzing and generating human language. Yet, most of this progress is focused on high-resource languages like English, French, and Spanish, leaving low-resource and under-represented languages with limited tools and resources for effective NLP applications. This paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing a novel approach for spell checking and correction in resource-scarce languages. Specifically, we focus on Wolof, an African spoken language that has recently sparked interest in NLP research. We also present a new dataset that can be used for word correction in Wolof. This study contributes to the overarching objective of developing inclusive and effective natural language processing (NLP) tools and resources, in alignment with the ethos of "no language left behind".

Katika miaka ya hivi karibuni, Uchakataji wa Lugha Asilia (NLP) umepiga hatua kubwa katika kuelewa, kuchambua na kuzalisha lugha ya binadamu. Hata hivyo, maendeleo haya mengi yanalenga lugha zenye rasilimali nyingi kama Kiingereza, Kifaransa, na Kihispania, na kuziacha lugha zenye rasilimali chache na zisizowakilishwa vyema zikiwa na zana na rasilimali chache kwa ajili ya matumizi bora ya NLP. Karatasi hii inalenga kuziba pengo hili kwa kuanzisha mbinu mpya ya kukagua tahajia na kusahihisha katika lugha zenye uhaba wa rasilimali. Hasa, tunazingatia Kiwolofu, lugha ya Kiafrika inayozungumzwa ambayo hivi karibuni imeamsha shauku katika utafiti wa NLP. Pia tunawasilisha seti mpya ya data inayoweza kutumika kwa usahihishaji wa maneno katika Kiwolofu. Utafiti huu unachangia lengo kuu la kuunda zana na rasilimali jumuishi na bora za uchakataji wa lugha asilia (NLP), kwa kuzingatia kauli mbiu ya "hakuna lugha inayoachwa nyuma".

Wolof, a Senegambian language primarily spoken in Senegal, Gambia and Mauritania (Diouf et al., 2017), serves as an example of a low-resource language that could greatly benefit from NLP advancements. Despite having over 10 million native speakers (Eberhard et al., 2019), there is a significant lack of digital resources and computational tools for most of (if not all) African languages, among them the Wolof language. As the world increasingly connects through digital platforms, it is vital to ensure robust NLP tools are available for low-resource languages like Wolof. Providing speakers of the language with accurate and effective spell checking and correction systems can enhance linguistic accessibility and promote digital communication across diverse linguistic communities.

Kiwolofu, lugha ya Kisenegambia inayozungumzwa hasa nchini Senegal, Gambia na Mauritania (Diouf et al., 2017), kinatumika kama mfano wa lugha yenye rasilimali chache ambayo inaweza kufaidika sana na maendeleo ya NLP. Licha ya kuwa na zaidi ya wazungumzaji asilia milioni 10 (Eberhard et al., 2019), kuna upungufu mkubwa wa rasilimali za kidijitali na zana za kikompyuta kwa Lugha nyingi za Afrika (kama si zote), miongoni mwazo lugha ya Kiwolofu. Kadiri dunia inavyozidi kuunganishwa kupitia majukwaa ya kidijitali, ni muhimu kuhakikisha kuwa zana imara za NLP zinapatikana kwa lugha zenye rasilimali chache kama Kiwolofu. Kuwapa wazungumzaji wa lugha mifumo sahihi na yenye ufanisi ya kikagua tahajia na usahihishaji kunaweza kuongeza ufikiaji wa lugha na kukuza mawasiliano ya kidijitali katika jamii mbalimbali za lugha.

Developing spell checkers and correction systems for low-resource languages is difficult due to the limited availability of annotated data, morphological complexity, and the absence of well-established computational resources. Traditional methods like rule-based or dictionary-based systems may not adequately address these challenges, requiring alternative approaches. Deep learning techniques, particularly transformer models, have demonstrated immense potential in various NLP tasks lately. These techniques can learn complex language patterns and generate context-sensitive representations, making them ideal for tackling challenges associated with low-resource language spell checking and correction.

Kutengeneza vikagua tahajia na mifumo ya usahihishaji kwa lugha yenye rasilimali chache ni vigumu kutokana na upatikanaji mdogo wa data iliyofafanuliwa, uchangamano wa kimofolojia, na ukosefu wa rasilimali za kikompyuta zilizoimarika. Mbinu za kijadi kama mifumo inayotegemea kanuni au kamusi huenda zisishughulikie changamoto hizi kikamilifu, hivyo kuhitaji mbinu mbadala. Mbinu za Ujifunzajikina, hususan mifano ya Transformer, zimeonyesha uwezo mkubwa katika kazi mbalimbali za NLP hivi karibuni. Mbinu hizi zinaweza kujifunza mifumo changamano ya lugha na kuzalisha uwakilishi unaozingatia muktadha, na kuzifanya kuwa bora kwa kushughulikia changamoto zinazohusiana na kikagua tahajia na usahihishaji wa lugha yenye rasilimali chache.

This paper presents a transformer-based model for word correction and spelling in Wolof. Our model is trained on a parallel corpus consisting of misspelled sentences and their error-free counterparts, optimizing the model to translate error-prone text into accurate sentences. Furthermore, we contribute to the advancement of NLP for the Wolof language by creating a new corpus of misspelled sentences and their error-free counterparts. This corpus serves as a benchmark and state-of-the-art in word correction and spelling for Wolof, providing a valuable resource for future research. This resource will facilitate the development of more advanced NLP tools and applications for Wolof.

Karatasi hii inawasilisha mfano unaotegemea Transformer kwa ajili ya usahihishaji wa neno na tahajia katika Wolof. Mfano wetu umefunzwa kwenye korasi sambamba inayojumuisha sentensi zenye makosa ya tahajia na sentensi zisizo na makosa, ukiboresha mfano ili kutafsiri maandishi yanayokabiliwa na makosa kuwa sentensi sahihi. Zaidi ya hayo, tunachangia katika maendeleo ya NLP kwa lugha ya Wolof kwa kuunda korasi mpya ya sentensi zenye makosa ya tahajia na sentensi zisizo na makosa. Korasi hii inatumika kama kigezo na hali ya kisasa katika usahihishaji wa neno na tahajia kwa Wolof, ikitoa rasilimali muhimu kwa utafiti wa baadaye. Rasilimali hii itawezesha uundaji wa zana na matumizi ya hali ya juu zaidi ya NLP kwa Wolof.

2. Background

2. Usuli

2.1. Wolof Language

2.1. Lugha ya Wolof

Wolof is a language belonging to the Senegambian group within the Northern branch of the Atlantic language family, which is part of the broader Niger-Congo language family. It shares strong linguistic connections with Pulaar and Serer languages (Sapir, 1971; Doneux, 1978; Wilson, 1989). The Atlantic language family includes approximately 40 languages, with Pulaar (a dialect of Fula) being the exception, and most are spoken in regions near of the Atlantic coast of Africa. Although Wolof is fundamentally an oral language, its orthography was standardized in 1972 (Robert, 2011).

Wolof ni lugha inayomilikiwa na kundi la Kisenegambia ndani ya tawi la Kaskazini la familia ya lugha ya Kiatlantiki, ambayo ni sehemu ya familia pana ya lugha za Kiniger-Kongo. Inashirikiana kwa uhusiano imara wa kiisimu na lugha za Kipulaar na Kiserer (Sapir, 1971; Doneux, 1978; Wilson, 1989). Familia ya lugha ya Kiatlantiki inajumuisha takriban lugha 40, huku Kipulaar (lahaja ya Kifulani) kikiwa kipekee, na nyingi zinazungumzwa katika maeneo karibu na pwani ya Atlantiki ya Afrika. Ingawa Wolof kimsingi ni lugha ya mazungumzo, tahajia yake ilisanifishwa mwaka 1972 (Robert, 2011).

Descriptive linguistic studies of Wolof can be traced back to the colonial period (Boilat, 1858), while other researches on Wolof morphology and syntax have been conducted by Diagne (1971), Mangold (1977), Church (1981), Dialo (1981), and Ka (1981). In-depth analytical studies of Wolof syntax can be primarily found in the works of Njie (1982) and Dunigan (1994).

Tafiti za kiisimu za kifafanuzi kuhusu Wolof zinaweza kufuatiliwa hadi kipindi cha ukoloni (Boilat, 1858), huku tafiti nyingine kuhusu mofolojia na sintaksi ya Wolof zimefanywa na Diagne (1971), Mangold (1977), Church (1981), Dialo (1981), na Ka (1981). Tafiti za kina za kiuchambuzi za sintaksi ya Wolof zinaweza kupatikana hasa katika kazi za Njie (1982) na Dunigan (1994).

Wolof is mainly an aspectual language, focusing on the aspect of an action rather than its tense. This characteristic allows the imperfective marker to combine with various tense markers. The language features a rich verb system, which includes a wide array of basic verbal forms and paradigms. Notably, Mangold (1977) and Church (1981) provide systematic presentations of Wolof verbal paradigms.

Wolof hasa ni lugha ya kiaspekti, inayozingatia kipengele cha kitendo badala ya wakati wake. Sifa hii inaruhusu kialamishi cha hali isiyokamilika kuungana na vialamishi mbalimbali vya wakati. Lugha hiyo ina mfumo tajiri wa vitenzi, unaojumuisha safu pana ya maumbo msingi ya vitenzi na mifano ya unyambulishaji. Hasa, Mangold (1977) na Church (1981) wanatoa mawasilisho ya kimfumo ya mifano ya unyambulishaji wa vitenzi vya Wolof.

In terms of literature and resources, Wolof appears in various forms, such as novels, short story collections, and poetry. However, even in Senegal, it is challenging to find materials written in Wolof. Recent efforts have been made to improve the availability of resources for Wolof speakers. In a study by Gauthier et al. (2016), researchers gathered an Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) dataset for four African languages, including Wolof. This dataset was then used to create the first ASR system for Wolof. Another initiative was proposed by Nguer et al. (2015), who outlined the creation process for the first collaborative online Wolof dictionary. This project was part of the larger Dictionnaires Langues Africaines - Français (DiLAF) project, which has produced dictionaries for seven African languages, including Wolof. However, at the time of writing, all dictionaries are accessible online except for the Wolof one. More recently, Cissé and Sadat (2023) have presented a range of resources for the Wolof language, including a spell checking tool mainly grounded in the language's writing rules.

Kwa upande wa fasihi na rasilimali, Wolof inaonekana katika aina mbalimbali, kama vile riwaya, mkusanyiko wa hadithi fupi, na mashairi. Hata hivyo, hata nchini Senegal, ni vigumu kupata nyenzo zilizoandikwa kwa Wolof. Juhudi za hivi karibuni zimefanywa ili kuboresha upatikanaji wa rasilimali kwa wazungumzaji wa Wolof. Katika utafiti wa Gauthier et al. (2016), watafiti walikusanya seti ya data ya utambuzi wa usemi wa kiotomatiki (ASR) kwa Lugha nne za Afrika, zikiwemo Wolof. Seti hii ya data ilitumika baadaye kuunda mfumo wa kwanza wa ASR kwa Wolof. Mpango mwingine ulipendekezwa na Nguer et al. (2015), ambao walielezea mchakato wa uundaji wa kamusi ya kwanza shirikishi ya mtandaoni ya Wolof. Mradi huu ulikuwa sehemu ya mradi mkubwa wa Dictionnaires Langues Africaines - Français (DiLAF), ambao umetoa kamusi za Lugha za Afrika saba, ikiwemo Wolof. Hata hivyo, wakati wa kuandika, kamusi zote zinapatikana mtandaoni isipokuwa ile ya Wolof. Hivi karibuni zaidi, Cissé na Sadat (2023) wamewasilisha rasilimali mbalimbali za lugha ya Wolof, ikiwemo zana ya kikagua tahajia inayotegemea hasa kanuni za uandishi za lugha hiyo.

2.2. Low-Resource Language Spell Checking and Correction

2.2. Kikagua Tahajia na Usahihishaji kwa Lugha yenye Rasilimali Chache

Spell checking and correction for low-resource languages have been of great interest to many researchers. Early approaches often depended on rule-based systems (Armstrong et al., 1995) or statistical methods, such as noisy channel models (Kernighan et al., 1990), n-gram models (Stolcke, 2000), and hidden Markov models (Viterbi, 1967). However, these methods often require substantial linguistic knowledge and annotated data, which may be scarce or non-existent for low-resource languages.

Kikagua tahajia na usahihishaji kwa lugha yenye rasilimali chache vimekuwa vya kuvutia sana kwa watafiti wengi. Mbinu za awali mara nyingi zilitegemea mifumo inayotegemea kanuni (Armstrong et al., 1995) au mbinu za kitakwimu, kama vile miundo ya mifereji yenye kelele (Kernighan et al., 1990), miundo ya n-gramu (Stolcke, 2000), na miundo fiche ya Markov (Viterbi, 1967). Hata hivyo, mbinu hizi mara nyingi zinahitaji ujuzi mkubwa wa kiisimu na data iliyofafanuliwa, ambayo inaweza kuwa haba au kutokuwepo kabisa kwa lugha yenye rasilimali chache.

In recent years, researchers have investigated data-driven approaches for low-resource languages, such as unsupervised learning (Soricut and Och, 2015) and bootstrapping techniques (Yarowsky et al., 2001). Some studies have also explored the use of cross-lingual transfer learning (Täckström et al., 2012) or leveraging comparable corpora (Madnani et al., 2012) to enhance spell checking and correction performance in low-resource languages. Nevertheless, these approaches may still be constrained by the availability and quality of parallel and comparable corpora.

Katika miaka ya hivi karibuni, watafiti wamechunguza mbinu zinazoendeshwa na data kwa lugha yenye rasilimali chache, kama vile ujifunzaji bila usimamizi (Soricut na Och, 2015) na mbinu za kujianzisha (Yarowsky et al., 2001). Baadhi ya tafiti pia zimechunguza matumizi ya Ujifunzaji hamishi kati ya lugha (Täckström et al., 2012) au kutumia korasi linganifu (Madnani et al., 2012) ili kuimarisha utendaji wa kikagua tahajia na usahihishaji katika lugha zenye rasilimali chache. Hata hivyo, mbinu hizi bado zinaweza kuwa na vikwazo kutokana na upatikanaji na ubora wa korasi sambamba na zinazolinganishwa.

2.3. Neural Networks in Spell Checking and Correction

2.3. Mitandao ya Neva katika Kikagua Tahajia na Usahihishaji

The emergence of deep learning techniques, in particular transformer models (Vaswani et al., 2017) and neural networks, has had a significant impact on the NLP field. These techniques have shown immense potential in a wide range of tasks, including machine translation (Bahdanau et al., 2015), information retrieval, conversational agents, sentiment analysis (Socher et al., 2013), and text summarization (See et al., 2017).

Kuibuka kwa mbinu za Ujifunzajikina, hasa miundo ya Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) na mitandao ya neva, kumekuwa na athari kubwa katika uwanja wa NLP. Mbinu hizi zimeonyesha uwezo mkubwa katika kazi mbalimbali, ikiwemo tafsiri ya mashine (Bahdanau et al., 2015), urejeshaji wa taarifa, mawakala wa mazungumzo, uchambuzi wa hisia (Socher et al., 2013), na ufupishaji wa maandishi (See et al., 2017).

In the context of spell checking and correction, sequence-to-sequence models (Sutskever et al., 2014) have been employed with promising results, using an encoder-decoder architecture to map misspelled sequences to their correct counterparts (Hládek et al., 2019). Attention mechanisms (Bahdanau et al., 2015) have also been integrated into these models to enhance the alignment between input and output sequences (Garg et al., 2019).

Katika muktadha wa kikagua tahajia na usahihishaji, miundo ya mfuatano-hadi-mfuatano (Sutskever et al., 2014) imetumika kwa matokeo ya kuahidi, kwa kutumia usanifu wa kisimbaji-kifumbuzi kuoanisha mifuatano yenye makosa ya tahajia na mifuatano yake sahihi (Hládek et al., 2019). Mbinu za umakini (Bahdanau et al., 2015) pia zimeunganishwa katika miundo hii ili kuimarisha ulinganifu kati ya mifuatano ya pembejeo na matokeo (Garg et al., 2019).

The development of transformer models has further advanced the capabilities of neural networks in spell checking and correction. Transformer models, which rely on self-attention mechanisms, have proven effective in capturing long-range dependencies and providing more accurate context-sensitive representations (Devlin et al., 2019). Recent studies have applied transformer models, such as BERT and GPT (Radford and Narasimhan, 2018), to spelling error detection and correction (Sorokin et al., 2016), or fine-tuned them for specific low-resource languages (Al-Ghamdi et al., 2023).

Uendelezaji wa miundo ya Transformer umeendeleza zaidi uwezo wa mitandao ya neva katika kikagua tahajia na usahihishaji. Miundo ya Transformer, ambayo hutegemea mbinu za kujizingatia, imethibitisha ufanisi katika kunasa tegemezi za masafa marefu na kutoa uwakilishi sahihi zaidi unaozingatia muktadha (Devlin et al., 2019). Tafiti za hivi karibuni zimetumia miundo ya Transformer, kama vile BERT na GPT (Radford na Narasimhan, 2018), kwa utambuzi na usahihishaji wa makosa ya tahajia (Sorokin et al., 2016), au kuzirekebisha kwa lugha mahususi zenye rasilimali chache (Al-Ghamdi et al., 2023).

3.2. Model architecture

3.2. Usanifu wa modeli

In this study, we employed a customized Transformer model architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) for the task of Wolof spell correction. The Transformer model has demonstrated remarkable success in various natural language processing tasks by leveraging self-attention mechanisms, which allow it to efficiently process input sequences without the need for recurrent or convolutional layers.

Katika utafiti huu, tulitumia usanifu wa modeli ya Transformer uliobinafsishwa (Vaswani et al., 2017) kwa kazi ya kusahihisha tahajia ya Kiwolofu. Modeli ya Transformer imeonyesha mafanikio ya ajabu katika kazi mbalimbali za uchakataji wa lugha asilia kwa kutumia mbinu za kujizingatia, ambazo huiruhusu kuchakata mifuatano ya pembejeo kwa ufanisi bila hitaji la tabaka rejezi au konvolisheni.

Our model consists of two components: an encoder and a decoder, each comprising five identical layers (Biljon et al., 2020). The encoder's primary task is to manage the input sequences containing misspellings, while the decoder focuses on producing output sequences without misspellings, as illustrated in Figure 1.

Modeli yetu ina vipengele viwili: kisimbuzi na kifumbuzi, kila kimoja kikijumuisha tabaka tano zinazofanana (Biljon et al., 2020). Kazi kuu ya kisimbuzi ni kudhibiti mifuatano ya pembejeo iliyo na makosa ya tahajia, huku kifumbuzi kikilenga kutoa mifuatano ya matokeo bila makosa ya tahajia, kama inavyoonyeshwa katika Kielelezo 1.

During the encoding phase, each input word is converted into a vector representation using an embedding layer. To incorporate positional information into the input embeddings, positional encoding is applied. In both the encoder and decoder components of the model, each layer comprises a multi-head self-attention mechanism with two attention heads. This is followed by position-wise feed-forward networks (FFNs) with a hidden size of 256 and a feed-forward size of 1024.

Wakati wa awamu ya usimbaji, kila neno la pembejeo hubadilishwa kuwa uwakilishi wa vekta kwa kutumia safu ya upachikaji. Ili kujumuisha taarifa za nafasi katika upachikaji wa pembejeo, usimbaji wa nafasi unatumika. Katika sehemu zote mbili za kisimbuzi na kifumbuzi cha mfano, kila tabaka linajumuisha utaratibu wa kujitazamia wenye vichwa vingi wenye vichwa viwili vya utazamaji. Hii inafuatwa na mitandao ya kusambaza mbele kwa nafasi (FFNs) yenye saizi ya fiche ya 256 na saizi ya kusambaza mbele ya 1024.

The self-attention process involves generating query (Q), key (K), and value (V) vectors from the input. These vectors are then used to compute a score matrix by performing matrix multiplication between the query and the key vector. The resulting matrix is scaled by the square root of the key vector dimension (dk). To obtain attention weights, the score matrix is normalized using softmax, representing the importance assigned to different parts of the input sequence. These attention weights are utilized to derive an output vector, as demonstrated in Eq. 1 (Vaswani et al., 2017). To enable efficient training and stable gradients, residual connections and layer normalization are implemented throughout the network.

Mchakato wa kujizingatia unahusisha kuzalisha vekta za swali (Q), ufunguo (K), na thamani (V) kutoka kwa pembejeo. Vekta hizi kisha hutumika kuhesabu matriksi ya alama kwa kufanya zidisho la matriksi kati ya vekta ya hoja na vekta ya ufunguo. Matriki inayotokana hupunguzwa kwa kipimo cha mzizi wa mraba wa mwelekeo wa vekta ya ufunguo (dk). Ili kupata uzani wa umakini, matriki ya alama hurekebishwa kwa kutumia softmax, ikiwakilisha umuhimu uliogawiwa kwa sehemu tofauti za mfuatano wa pembejeo. Uzani huu wa umakini hutumika kupata vekta ya pato, kama inavyoonyeshwa katika Eq. 1 (Vaswani et al., 2017). Ili kuwezesha mafunzo bora na mielekeo thabiti, miunganisho ya mabaki na urekebishaji wa tabaka hutekelezwa kote katika mtandao.

The decoder includes two multi-headed attention blocks within a single layer: one for the target sequences and another for the encoder's output. The former multi-head attention is masked to prevent computing attention scores for subsequent words. The latter multi-head attention layer employs the encoder's outputs as queries and keys, while the outputs of the first multi-headed attention layer serve as values. This mechanism empowers the decoder to determine the encoder inputs that are most relevant to its generation process, thereby producing an output sequence without any misspellings. The output from the final pointwise feed-forward layer is then forwarded to a linear layer, serving as a classifier, followed by a softmax layer to generate the corrected text.

Kifumbuzi kinajumuisha vijisehemu viwili vya umakini wa vichwa vingi ndani ya tabaka moja: kimoja kwa ajili ya mfuatano lengwa na kingine kwa ajili ya matokeo ya kisimbuzi. Umakini wa kichwa vingi wa kwanza umefichwa ili kuzuia kukokotoa alama za umakini kwa maneno yanayofuata. Tabaka la umakini wa vichwa vingi la pili linatumia matokeo ya enkoda kama maswali na funguo, huku matokeo ya tabaka la kwanza la umakini wa vichwa vingi yakitumika kama thamani. Utaratibu huu unaiwezesha dekoda kubainisha pembejeo za enkoda ambazo ni muhimu zaidi kwa mchakato wake wa uzalishaji, hivyo kutoa mfuatano wa pato bila makosa yoyote ya tahajia. Pato kutoka tabaka la mwisho la kusambaza mbele kwa nukta moja kisha hupelekwa kwa tabaka la mstari, likitumika kama kiainishi, likifuatiwa na tabaka la softmax ili kuzalisha maandishi yaliyosahihishwa.

For initialization, we employ Xavier initialization with a gain of 1.0 (Glorot and Bengio, 2010) for all trainable weights, while the bias terms are initialized with zeros. The embeddings undergo Xavier initialization with a distinct gain of 1.0. To minimize the number of trainable parameters, a common practice is to tie the source and target embeddings, as well as the softmax layer (Press and Wolf, 2017). Since our model operates at the character level, the default vocabulary size is relatively small. We set the embedding dimension to 256 in both the encoder and decoder, which corresponds to the hidden size of the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) for compatibility. Furthermore, we scale the embeddings by the square root of their size.

Kwa uanzishaji, tunatumia uanzishaji wa Xavier na faida ya 1.0 (Glorot na Bengio, 2010) kwa uzani wote unaoweza kufunzwa, huku vipengele vya upendeleo vikianzishwa na sufuri. Upachikaji hupitia uanzishaji wa Xavier na faida tofauti ya 1.0. Ili kupunguza idadi ya vigezo vinavyoweza kufunzwa, mazoezi ya kawaida ni kufunga upachikaji wa chanzo na lengwa, pamoja na safu ya softmax (Press na Wolf, 2017). Kwa kuwa mfano wetu hufanya kazi katika kiwango cha herufi, saizi ya msamiati chaguomsingi ni ndogo kiasi. Tunaweka mwelekeo wa upachikaji kuwa 256 katika kisimbuzi na kifumbuzi, ambayo inalingana na ukubwa wa siri wa Mtandao wa Kusambaza Mbele (FFN) kwa upatanifu. Zaidi ya hayo, tunapima upachikaji kwa mzizi wa mraba wa saizi yake.

To address the issue of overfitting, we employ dropout techniques on various Transformer components. Initially, we apply an embedding dropout rate of 10^-1 to the encoder and decoder, which helps in dropping words from the embedding matrix (Gal and Ghahramani, 2016). Furthermore, we apply dropout only within the decoder layers at a rate of 3 × 10^-1 (Srivastava et al., 2014).

Ili kushughulikia suala la ujifunzaji kupita kiasi, tunatumia mbinu za kufuta (dropout) kwenye vipengele mbalimbali vya Transformer. Awali, tunatumia kiwango cha kuacha upachikaji cha 10-1 kwa kisimbuzi na kifumbuzi, ambacho kinasaidia katika kuacha maneno kutoka kwa matriki ya upachikaji (Gal na Ghahramani, 2016). Zaidi ya hayo, tunatumia kuacha tu ndani ya tabaka za kifumbuzi kwa kiwango cha 3 × 10-1 (Srivastava et al., 2014).

3.3. Model Training

3.3. Mafunzo ya Mfano

The model training procedure was carefully designed, considering various parameters to ensure rigorous and repeatable results.

Utaratibu wa mafunzo ya mfano ulibuniwa kwa uangalifu, ukizingatia vigezo mbalimbali ili kuhakikisha matokeo madhubuti na yanayoweza kurudiwa.

We employed deterministic training by using a fixed random seed of 42. To optimize the model, we chose the widely used Adam optimizer (Kingma and Ba, 2015), which features adaptive learning rates and momentum-based parameter updates. For the first and second-order moments, we assigned beta values of [9×10^-1, 999×10^-3 ], respectively. The learning rate was initialized at 10^-4, and a minimum threshold of 10^-8 was set to terminate training upon convergence or near-convergence.

Tulitumia mafunzo ya kiamuzi kwa kutumia mbegu thabiti ya nasibu ya 42. Ili kuboresha mfano, tulichagua kiboreshaji cha Adam kinachotumika sana (Kingma na Ba, 2015), ambacho kina viwango vya ujifunzaji vinavyobadilika na masasisho ya vigezo yanayotegemea msukumo. Kwa momenti za mpangilio wa kwanza na wa pili, tuligawa thamani za beta za [9×10-1, 999×10-3], mtawalia. Kiwango cha ujifunzaji kilianzishwa katika 10^-4, na kizingiti cha chini cha 10^-8 kiliwekwa ili kusitisha mafunzo wakati wa muunganiko au karibu na muunganiko.

To optimize the learning process, we adopted a plateau-based scheduling strategy (Smith, 2017). With a patience value of 5, the learning rate was reduced by a factor of 7 × 10^-1 if the validation score did not improve over five consecutive validation rounds. This dynamic adaptation of the learning rate, based on performance feedback, led to enhanced convergence and optimization.

Ili kuboresha mchakato wa ujifunzaji, tulipitisha mkakati wa upangaji unaotegemea tambarare (Smith, 2017). Kwa thamani ya subira ya 5, kiwango cha ujifunzaji kilipunguzwa kwa kipengele cha 7 × 10-1 ikiwa alama ya uthibitishaji haikuboreshwa katika raundi tano mfululizo za uthibitishaji. Marekebisho haya mahiri ya kiwango cha ujifunzaji, kulingana na maoni ya utendaji, yalisababisha muunganiko na uboreshaji ulioimarishwa.

We facilitated efficient parallel computation during training by using a batch size of 4096 tokens (Ott et al., 2018). The token-based batching approach optimized computational resources by forming batches based on the total number of tokens instead of the number of sentences.

Tuliwezesha ukokotoaji sambamba wenye ufanisi wakati wa mafunzo kwa kutumia ukubwa wa kundi wa tokeni 4096 (Ott et al., 2018). Mbinu ya kupanga makundi kwa kuzingatia tokeni iliboresha rasilimali za kikokotozi kwa kuunda makundi kulingana na jumla ya idadi ya tokeni badala ya idadi ya sentensi.

Throughout the entire training process, we placed significant emphasis on the model's ability to generalize and perform well by conducting regular evaluations. To ensure a thorough assessment, we established validation intervals of 2000 updates, covering 50 epochs. We carefully selected this interval, considering that setting a validation frequency that is too high might not provide ample opportunities for the model to learn and improve during validation. Furthermore, excessively frequent validation could lead to extended training times and potentially prematurely terminate the training if the validation patience value is not set high enough. Thus, we decided on the mentioned interval to strike a balance.

Katika mchakato mzima wa mafunzo, tuliweka mkazo mkubwa kwenye uwezo wa modeli wa kujumlisha na kufanya vizuri kwa kufanya tathmini za mara kwa mara. Ili kuhakikisha tathmini kamili, tulianzisha vipindi vya uthibitishaji vya masasisho 2000, vikijumuisha epoka 50. Tulichagua kipindi hiki kwa uangalifu, tukizingatia kwamba kuweka marudio ya uthibitishaji ya juu sana kunaweza kutotoa fursa za kutosha kwa modeli kujifunza na kuboresha wakati wa uthibitishaji. Zaidi ya hayo, uthibitishaji wa mara kwa mara kupita kiasi unaweza kusababisha muda mrefu wa mafunzo na uwezekano wa kusitisha mafunzo mapema ikiwa thamani ya subira ya uthibitishaji haijawekwa juu vya kutosha. Kwa hivyo, tuliamua kutumia muda uliotajwa ili kuweka usawa.

Moreover, to enhance our ability to closely track the training process and gain comprehensive insights into the model's development, we implemented a logging frequency of 200 updates.

Zaidi ya hayo, ili kuimarisha uwezo wetu wa kufuatilia kwa karibu mchakato wa mafunzo na kupata ufahamu wa kina kuhusu maendeleo ya modeli, tulitekeleza marudio ya kuweka kumbukumbu ya masasisho 200.

We implemented early stopping by minimizing our cross-entropy loss function, which is a common approach in model training. Continuously monitoring the loss function allowed us to terminate the training when a new low score was reached, effectively preventing overfitting.

Tulitekeleza usimamishaji wa mapema kwa kupunguza kazi yetu ya hasara ya entropi mtambuka, ambayo ni mbinu ya kawaida katika mafunzo ya modeli. Kufuatilia kwa kuendelea kazi ya hasara kulituruhusu kusitisha mafunzo wakati alama mpya ya chini ilipofikiwa, jambo lililozuia kwa ufanisi Ujifunzaji kupita kiasi.

To promote diverse predictions and mitigate overfitting, we incorporated two regularization techniques: label smoothing and weight decay. Specifically, we employed label smoothing with a coefficient of 10^-1 (Szegedy et al., 2016), and weight decay at a rate of 10^-4 (Srivastava et al., 2014). Label smoothing is a regularization method that redistributes the probability weight from reference tokens to other vocabulary tokens. By reducing the overemphasis on specific reference tokens, label smoothing fosters diversity in the model's output and helps prevent overconfidence in predictions. Weight decay, also known as L2 regularization, is a technique used to control the complexity of the model. During training, it reduces the magnitude of the model's weights by adding a penalty term proportional to the weight values to the loss function. This regularization term encourages smaller weight values, preventing the model from overfitting the training data and improving generalization performance.

Ili kukuza utabiri mbalimbali na kupunguza ujifunzaji kupita kiasi, tulijumuisha mbinu mbili za udhibiti: ulainishaji wa lebo na upunguzaji wa uzani. Hasa, tulitumia ulainishaji wa lebo kwa mgawo wa 10^-1 (Szegedy et al., 2016), na upunguzaji wa uzani kwa kiwango cha 10^-4 (Srivastava et al., 2014). Ulainishaji wa lebo ni mbinu ya udhibiti inayogawa upya uzito wa uwezekano kutoka kwa tokeni za rejeleo hadi kwa tokeni nyingine za msamiati. Kwa kupunguza msisitizo wa kupita kiasi kwenye tokeni maalum za rejeleo, ulainishaji wa lebo unakuza utofauti katika matokeo ya modeli na kusaidia kuzuia kujiamini kupita kiasi katika utabiri. Uozo wa uzani, unaojulikana pia kama urekebishaji wa L2, ni mbinu inayotumika kudhibiti utata wa modeli. Wakati wa mafunzo, inapunguza ukubwa wa uzani wa modeli kwa kuongeza neno la adhabu linalolingana na thamani za uzani kwenye kazi ya hasara. Neno hili la urekebishaji linahimiza thamani ndogo za uzani, kuzuia modeli dhidi ya kuzoea kupita kiasi data ya mafunzo na kuboresha utendaji wa ujumlishaji.

During training, our primary evaluation metric was the well-established BLEU score (Papineni et al., 2002), which measures the similarity between predicted and reference sequences. For efficient evaluation, we used a token-based batching strategy with a batch size of 1024 tokens.

Wakati wa mafunzo, kipimo chetu kikuu cha tathmini kilikuwa alama ya BLEU iliyoimarika (Papineni et al., 2002), ambayo hupima ufanano kati ya mfuatano uliotabiriwa na wa marejeleo. Kwa tathmini bora, tulitumia mkakati wa kupanga makundi kulingana na tokeni, ukiwa na ukubwa wa kundi wa tokeni 1024.

To manage the length of generated sequences during decoding, we set a maximum output length of 175 tokens. Furthermore, we maintained progress monitoring and validation integrity by consistently printing three validation sentences during each validation run.

Ili kudhibiti urefu wa mfuatano unaozalishwa wakati wa usimbuaji, tuliweka urefu wa juu wa matokeo wa tokeni 175. Zaidi ya hayo, tulidumisha ufuatiliaji wa maendeleo na uadilifu wa uthibitishaji kwa kuchapisha sentensi tatu za uthibitishaji mara kwa mara wakati wa kila kukimbia kwa uthibitishaji.

The p-value reflects the likelihood of obtaining such a pronounced difference between the two systems purely by chance, assuming the null hypothesis to be valid. In the context of our Wilcoxon signed-rank test, the null hypothesis postulates that there is no significant difference in the performance of the two systems. An extremely low p-value, such as the one calculated (4.92 × 10^-17 ), provides compelling evidence against this null hypothesis (H0), thereby reinforcing the validity of our alternative hypothesis (Ha).

5. Limitations

5. Mapungufu

Our spell checking system has demonstrated good performance, as indicated by its high BLEU and ChrF++ scores, as well as the relatively low WER and CER scores. However, there are still limitations that require further investigations and improvements.

Mfumo wetu wa kikagua tahajia umeonyesha utendaji mzuri, kama inavyoonyeshwa na alama zake za juu za BLEU na ChrF++, pamoja na alama za chini kiasi za WER na CER. Hata hivyo, bado kuna vikwazo vinavyohitaji uchunguzi zaidi na maboresho.

Firstly, character-level models, such as the one used in this study, are inherently complex and can be time consuming to train. This is due to the larger sequence of data they need to learn from, compared to word-level models. The computational cost of training such models can be particularly high when working with large datasets or languages with extensive character sets.

Kwanza, mifano ya kiwango cha herufi, kama ile iliyotumika katika utafiti huu, kwa asili ni changamano na inaweza kuchukua muda mrefu kufunzwa. Hii inatokana na mfuatano mkubwa zaidi wa data wanaohitaji kujifunza kutoka kwake, ikilinganishwa na mifano ya kiwango cha neno. Gharama ya kikokotoo ya kufunza mifano kama hiyo inaweza kuwa juu hasa wakati wa kufanya kazi na seti kubwa za data au lugha zenye seti pana za herufi.

Secondly, our model may struggle with capturing long-range dependencies within the text. The dependencies between words in a sentence, which often span across several characters, can be difficult for character-level models to understand. This could potentially affect the model's performance in cases that require a deep understanding of sentence-level semantics.

Pili, mfano wetu unaweza kutatizika kunasa tegemezi za masafa marefu ndani ya maandishi. Tegemezi kati ya maneno katika sentensi, ambazo mara nyingi hujitokeza katika herufi kadhaa, zinaweza kuwa ngumu kwa mifano ya kiwango cha herufi kuelewa. Hili linaweza kuathiri utendaji wa mfano katika hali zinazohitaji uelewa wa kina wa semantiki ya kiwango cha sentensi.

Thirdly, our model lacks the advantage of leveraging pre-trained word embeddings, which capture semantic and syntactic relationships between words. As a result, the model's semantic understanding may be less nuanced compared to models that operate at the word level.

Tatu, mfano wetu hauna faida ya kutumia upachikaji wa maneno uliofunzwa awali, ambao hunasa uhusiano wa semantiki na kisintaksia kati ya maneno. Kwa sababu hiyo, uelewa wa semantiki wa mfano unaweza kuwa na upeo mdogo ikilinganishwa na mifano inayofanya kazi katika kiwango cha neno.

Fourthly, character-level models can be more sensitive to noise in the input data. Spelling errors, inconsistent punctuation usage, and other forms of noise can have a more significant impact on these models, which could lead to lower performance in certain situations.

Nne, mifano ya kiwango cha herufi inaweza kuwa nyeti zaidi kwa kelele katika data ya pembejeo. Makosa ya tahajia, matumizi yasiyolingana ya alama ya uakifishaji, na aina nyingine za kelele zinaweza kuwa na athari kubwa zaidi kwa mifano hii, jambo ambalo linaweza kusababisha utendaji duni katika hali fulani.

Additionally, while our model is designed to handle any language that utilizes an alphabet similar to that of the Wolof language, it may struggle with languages that rely heavily on word order. This is due to the model's lack of word-level understanding, which could help in these situations.

Zaidi ya hayo, ingawa mfano wetu umeundwa kushughulikia lugha yoyote inayotumia alfabeti sawa na ile ya lugha ya Wolof, unaweza kutatizika na lugha zinazotegemea sana mpangilio wa maneno. Hii inatokana na ukosefu wa uelewa wa kiwango cha neno wa modeli, ambao ungeweza kusaidia katika hali hizi.

Lastly, our model may face difficulties with disambiguation. For instance, words spelled the same but with different meanings can pose a challenge for character-level models, as these models lack access to word-level semantic information.

Mwishowe, modeli yetu inaweza kukumbwa na matatizo ya utatuzi wa utata. Kwa mfano, maneno yanayoandikwa sawa lakini yenye maana tofauti yanaweza kuleta changamoto kwa modeli za kiwango cha herufi, kwa kuwa modeli hizi hazina ufikiaji wa taarifa za semantiki za kiwango cha neno.

Given these considerations, there are several areas that could be targeted for improvement. Firstly, the model could be further trained on a wider variety of textual data in order to improve its capacity to handle of less common or more complex errors. Given our current focus on a language with limited available resources, the use of the back-translation technique emerges as a promising strategy. This approach has consistently demonstrated its effectiveness in various domains, such as Statistical Machine Translation (SMT) (Bojar and Tamchyna, 2011), supervised Neural Machine Translation (Sennrich et al., 2016a), and unsupervised Machine Translation (Lample et al., 2017).

Kwa kuzingatia mambo haya, kuna maeneo kadhaa ambayo yanaweza kulengwa kwa ajili ya uboreshaji. Kwanza, modeli inaweza kufunzwa zaidi kwa aina mbalimbali zaidi za data za kimaandishi ili kuboresha uwezo wake wa kushughulikia makosa yasiyo ya kawaida au changamano zaidi. Kwa kuzingatia mwelekeo wetu wa sasa kwenye lugha yenye rasilimali chache zinazopatikana, matumizi ya mbinu ya tafsiri-nyuma yanajitokeza kama mkakati wenye matumaini. Mbinu hii imeonyesha mara kwa mara ufanisi wake katika nyanja mbalimbali, kama vile Tafsiri ya Mashine ya Kitakwimu (SMT) (Bojar and Tamchyna, 2011), Tafsiri ya Mashine ya Neva inayosimamiwa (Sennrich et al., 2016a), na Tafsiri ya Mashine isiyosimamiwa (Lample et al., 2017).

In the context of spell-checking and correction, adopting this approach would involve training a model to intentionally introduce a substantial number of realistic spelling errors within clean text. Subsequently, the resulting corpus of corrupted text can be employed to refine our spell checking model.

Katika muktadha wa kukagua tahajia na kusahihisha, kutumia mbinu hii kungehusisha kufunza modeli ili kwa makusudi kuanzisha idadi kubwa ya makosa ya tahajia ya kweli ndani ya maandishi safi. Baadaye, korasi inayotokana ya maandishi yaliyoharibika inaweza kutumiwa kuboresha mfano wetu wa kikagua tahajia.

Furthermore, we suggest further exploration of hybrid models that combine the benefits of both character-level and word-level processing. Such models could potentially leverage the granularity of character-level models while still maintaining a higher-level understanding of word and sentence semantics.

Zaidi ya hayo, tunapendekeza uchunguzi zaidi wa miundo mseto inayochanganya faida za uchakataji wa kiwango cha herufi na kiwango cha neno. Miundo kama hiyo inaweza kutumia undani wa miundo ya kiwango cha herufi huku ikidumisha uelewa wa kiwango cha juu wa semantiki ya neno na sentensi.

Lastly, considering the computational expenses associated with character-level models, it would be beneficial to conduct research on more efficient training methods. By doing so, we can mitigate the computational burden and improve the overall efficiency of the training process.

Mwishowe, kwa kuzingatia gharama za kikokotozi zinazohusiana na miundo ya kiwango cha herufi, itakuwa na manufaa kufanya utafiti kuhusu mbinu bora zaidi za mafunzo. Kwa kufanya hivyo, tunaweza kupunguza mzigo wa kikokotozi na kuboresha ufanisi wa jumla wa mchakato wa mafunzo.

6. Conclusion

6. Hitimisho

The present study represents significant progress in the field of automatic spelling correction, particularly for under-resourced and under-represented spoken languages. Our model, which utilizes a transformer-based architecture has produced encouraging results across several evaluation metrics, including BLEU, WER, CER and ChrF++. These outcomes highlight the potential of advanced deep learning techniques to overcome the challenge of spelling errors, even in languages with limited available data.

Utafiti huu unawakilisha maendeleo makubwa katika uwanja wa usahihishaji tahajia otomatiki, hasa kwa lugha zinazozungumzwa zenye rasilimali chache na uwakilishi mdogo. Mfano wetu, unaotumia usanifu msingi wa transfoma umetoa matokeo ya kutia moyo katika vipimo kadhaa vya tathmini, vikiwemo BLEU, WER, CER na ChrF++. Matokeo haya yanaangazia uwezo wa mbinu za hali ya juu za Ujifunzajikina kushinda changamoto ya makosa ya tahajia, hata katika lugha zenye data ndogo inayopatikana.

Despite these promising results, our work has also highlighted certain areas of improvement that could further refine the performance of the proposed system. Our model, being character-level, exhibits certain limitations such as computational complexity, difficulty in capturing long-range dependencies, and sensitivity to noise in the input data. Moreover, the lack of word-level understanding could lead to potential difficulties with languages that heavily rely on word order or face challenges with disambiguation. Furthermore, the investigation of hybrid models, combining the benefits of both character-level and word-level processing, could be a promising direction for future work.

Licha ya matokeo haya ya kuahidi, kazi yetu pia imeangazia maeneo fulani ya kuboresha ambayo yanaweza kuboresha zaidi utendaji wa mfumo uliopendekezwa. Mfano wetu, kwa kuwa ni wa kiwango cha herufi, unaonyesha vikwazo fulani kama vile uchangamano wa kikokotoo, ugumu wa kunasa tegemezi za masafa marefu, na uelekevu kwa kelele katika data ya pembejeo. Zaidi ya hayo, ukosefu wa uelewa wa kiwango cha neno unaweza kusababisha ugumu unaowezekana kwa lugha ambazo hutegemea sana mpangilio wa maneno au zinazokabiliwa na changamoto za utatuzi wa utata. Zaidi ya hayo, uchunguzi wa miundo mseto, unaochanganya manufaa ya uchakataji wa kiwango cha herufi na kiwango cha neno, unaweza kuwa mwelekeo wa kuahidi kwa kazi ya baadaye.

We hope that our findings will encourage further research in this direction, ultimately contributing to the broader goal of building inclusive and effective natural language processing tools for all languages.

Tunatumaini kwamba matokeo yetu yatahimiza utafiti zaidi katika mwelekeo huu, hatimaye kuchangia lengo pana la kujenga zana jumuishi na zenye ufanisi za uchakataji wa lugha asilia kwa lugha zote.
