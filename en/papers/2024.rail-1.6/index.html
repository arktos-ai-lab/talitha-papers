Abstract

The South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) has proven to be a valuable tool for data analysis in the multilingual context of South Africa, particularly in governmental texts. However, its suitability for broader projects has yet to be determined. This paper aims to assess the performance of the SA-LID in identifying isiXhosa in YouTube comments as part of the methodology for research on the expression of cultural identity through linguistic strategies. We curated a selection of 10 videos which focused on the isiXhosa culture in terms of theatre, poetry, language learning, culture, or music. The videos were predominantly in English as were most of the comments but the latter were interspersed with elements of isiXhosa, identifying the commentators as speakers of isiXhosa. The SA-LID was used to identify all instances of the use of isiXhosa to facilitate the analysis of the relevant items. Following the application of the SA-LID to this data, a manual evaluation was conducted to gauge the effectiveness of this tool in selecting all isiXhosa items. Our findings reveal significant limitations in the use of the SA-LID, encompassing the oversight of unconventional spellings in indigenous languages and misclassification of closely related languages within the Nguni group. Although proficient in identifying the use of Nguni languages, differentiating within this language group proved challenging for the SA-LID. These results underscore the necessity for manual checks to complement the use of the SA-LID when other Nguni languages may be present in the comment texts.

1. Introduction

The global linguistic landscape, comprising approximately 7168 languages, is dynamic and demands continuous exploration (Aitchison, 2005; Trask, 2003). This is particularly true in light of the core role played by language in terms of the social, cultural, intellectual and political vitality in any society (Lo Bianco, 2010). As such, there is a need for continuing research in order to understand the characteristics of each language as well as the cultures and identities that are linked to the concerned linguistic communities.

Given the global linguistic diversity, an ability to distinguish between the languages being used in a particular context is understandably significant (Jaspers, 2015). Such an ability facilitates the decoding of the content of the message and thus fosters effective communication and comprehension (Hardan, 2013). This is particularly significant in a linguistically diverse country like South Africa (Fishman et al., 2008), where most citizens are multilingual (Evans, 2015; Sithole, 2015; Adelani et al., 2021).

Through language, individuals not only communicate but also articulate their origins, making it a fundamental dimension of cultural identity. In this, and many other ways, language and identity are intricately linked (Bucholtz and Hall, 2004). This aspect of language use extends beyond oral expression to also encompass written interactions and specifically so in colloquial contexts which allow for a more spontaneous and free use of language - for example, social media platforms. A tool which has the ability to accurately identify the languages used in a multilingual text could carry numerous benefits and play an important role in a linguistically diverse society. This would be especially true in terms of research focused on the actual use of language by those fluent in more than one language and the manner in which their language use expresses their cultural identity.

Identity, in its simplest form, is an expression of individuality and reflects the uniqueness of every human being (Buckingham, 2008). However, identity is also influenced to a large extent by the social groups to which an individual belongs (Baxter, 2016). This is particularly so in terms of the cultural and linguistic background into which one is born as this is the context in which one first learns about - and learns how to express - aspects of the world (Praeg, 2014). Linguistic and cultural identity are generally conflated and language use is often reflective of these aspects - along with other 'hints' about a speaker's identity (Bucholtz and Hall, 2005). For this reason, the relationship between language usage and cultural/linguistic identity is rife with possibilities.

Research on the link between language use and cultural or linguistic identity in a multilingual context assumes the ability to discern between the languages employed by the language users in that community. This is indeed the case with the research project of which this paper forms a component. The broader study aims to investigate linguistic strategies employed by isiXhosa language users to express their language identities on YouTube through the use of comments.

The scope of this study, therefore, underscores the necessity of a reliable language identifier to accurately detect the language(s) used within a particular text. The use of such a tool becomes indispensable when navigating through the substantial pool of comments in order to extract comments written in isiXhosa or code-switched between isiXhosa and other languages. The identification of instances of isiXhosa usage from our corpus of YouTube comments is thus necessary in order to delineate the data on which our study will focus.

This paper aims to evaluate the reliability of the South African Language Identifier (Puttkammer et al., 2016) when it is applied to a corpus of YouTube comments to ascertain the languages used. The following sections of this paper provide a brief literature review in terms of the core concepts in Section 2, an overview of our methodology for data collection in Section 3 and analysis as well as a summary of our findings in Section 4. The paper concludes with a discussion of our conclusions and recommendations in Section 5.

2. Background

2.1. The isiXhosa Language

While there are between 24 and 30 spoken languages in South Africa (Finlayson and Madiba, 2002), the constitution of the Republic of South Africa recognises 12 of these as official languages (Republic of South Africa, 1996, 2023). These official languages are typically grouped into six language groups, including: (i) South African Sign Language, (ii) Sotho-Tswana, which includes Sesotho, Setswana, and Sepedi, (iii) Sotho-Makua-Venda, which includes Tshivenḓa, (iv) West Germanic, which includes Afrikaans and English, (v) NguniTsonga, which includes Xitsonga, and (vi) Nguni, which includes isiZulu, Siswati, siNdebele and isiXhosa.

The Nguni language group occupies a significant position as the largest language group in South Africa. IsiXhosa, the second-most prominent Nguni language within South Africa, (Wheeler, 2018), is predominantly spoken in the Eastern Cape and the Western Cape Provinces. Notably, it is also officially recognised in Zimbabwe (Republic of Zimbabwe, 2021). According to Wheeler (2018), isiXhosa has much in common with isiZulu in terms of their linguistic roots. In fact, as discussed later in Section 4.3, isiXhosa demonstrates close linguistic ties and mutual intelligibility with other languages in the Nguni group as well (Dyers, 2000). Additionally, isiXhosa stands out for its use of clicks, a feature present in only about 0.5% of the world's languages (Brenzinger and Shah, 2023), including a few Bantu languages. These clicks are represented by the use of three consonants: /c, q, and x/ (Nogwina et al., 2013; Gxowa-Dlayedwa, 2015, 2018; Wheeler, 2018).

2.2. Identifying Languages

The initial step in comprehending written text is to ascertain the language in which it is written (Babhulgaonkar and Sonavane, 2020). Various language identification tools are developed for this purpose, with the goal of discerning the language(s) present in the text (Jauhiainen et al., 2024). Note that these language identifiers are designed to encompass both speech and written texts. However, due to the inherent differences between written text, composed of discrete characters, and speech, which involves a continuous signal relying on acoustic features, different natural language processing methods are traditionally employed for text and speech, resulting in limited methodological overlap between the two (Murthy and Kumar, 2006; Ambikairajah et al., 2011).

For the purpose of this discussion, our focus is specifically on language identifiers for written texts. Text language identification involves analyzing written linguistic features, including character n-grams and word frequency patterns. This analytical process often makes use of statistical models and machine learning algorithms (Nezhadi et al., 2017).

Traditionally, human beings are regarded as the most accurate language identifiers (Deshwal et al., 2020). Unfortunately, their ability to detect languages is limited by their language repertoires. As such, the limits of relying on humans for language identification become obvious when considering the estimated 7168 languages worldwide (Al-Jarf et al., 2022), or the twelve official languages in South Africa. Simply put, humans are unable to detect languages that are outside their current linguistic repertoires.

As a result, more non-human dependent approaches are needed in the task of identifying languages. Over time, computational approaches employing tailored algorithms and indexing structures have developed to discern language usage without human intervention (Calvo et al., 2017). This evolution includes the use of advanced techniques such as neural networks (Talpur and O'Sullivan, 2020) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) approaches (Saji et al., 2022), which are integrated into language identification tools.

2.3. Language Identification Tools

The introduction of automatic language identifiers serves as a valuable advancement in language detection. The primary task of a language identification tool is to analyse a given spoken or written text and generate a prediction of the language in which the text is spoken or written (Navrátil, 2006; Bergsma et al., 2012; Solorio et al., 2014). This process includes assessing the probability of each word in the provided text as belonging to one or more of the languages in the tool's library (Lui and Baldwin, 2012). The identified language is determined by the highest probability, initiating a competition among language models to determine the most likely match for the entire sample. Like humans, automatic language identifiers also rely on libraries (Agarwal et al., 2023). In this way, the automatic tools need to be trained using different languages and will not be able to detect new languages that are not in their existing libraries.

In this paper, as indicated in Section 1, we conduct a qualitative evaluation of the South African Language Identifier (henceforth SA-LID). The SA-LID was designed to classify text into one of the 11 official written languages of South Africa, either at the document or line level (Puttkammer et al., 2018). The SA-LID has been trained using government text corpora obtained during the National Centre for Human Language Technology (NCHLT) Text project and collected through collaboration between the South African Department of Arts and Culture and the Centre for Text Technology (Puttkammer et al., 2018).

The SA-LID uses feature extraction, identifying language-specific patterns through the analysis of character n-grams, ranging from bigrams to 6-grams. The model was trained using the Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier, incorporating labelled training samples and the selected feature extractor. These components collectively enable the model to discern languages effectively based on the extracted features. In the subsequent step of text classification, the trained classifier is applied to text inputs, resulting in a list of probable languages arranged by their respective probabilities. The final language determination is achieved by selecting the language with the highest probability, based on the model's consideration of learned patterns and characteristics during the training process, ensuring accurate identification of the language in the given text.

2.4. Related Research

Previous research has explored the application of language identification specifically to isiXhosa texts (Kyeyune, 2015). In their study, Kyeyune (2015) utilised corpora from the Language Resource Management Agency and employed the Java Text Categorising Library to extract n-grams for identifying isiXhosa using an n-gram language model. The study conducted by Duvenhage et al. (2017) investigated the use of a naive Bayes classifier for accurate language group identification. Additionally, they incorporated a lexicon-based classifier to differentiate the specific South African language in which the text is composed. Furthermore, in their work, Duvenhage (2019) introduces a hierarchical classifier that combines naive Bayesian and lexicon-based approaches for short-text Language Identification (LID). This approach proves particularly beneficial for under-resourced languages.

In this paper, we investigate the reliability of the SA-LID to assess its usability in detecting isiXhosa from YouTube comments, employing a qualitative approach for our discussion.

3. Methodology

A total of ten videos were selected from YouTube using a variety of pre-determined search terms such as: (i) amaXhosa ase South Africa, (ii) Introduction to the Xhosa culture (iii) The History of isiXhosa language, (iv) The history of isiXhosa culture, (v) Clicks used in isiXhosa music, and (vi) isiXhosa language-use in South Africa. The video selection process was based on the relevance to the title of the broader study, as well as evidence of the use of linguistic elements which identified commentators as isiXhosa.

We employed the YouTube API to mine comments from the 10 selected videos for our study. This process involved specifying the video IDs of the chosen content and extracting the associated comments. The API facilitated the extraction of text-based comments and emojis. We excluded information such as user details, timestamps, and other information.

The data collection was conducted on 19 January 2024. We identified videos that were uploaded more than a year before our investigation. As a result, we were not expecting any surge in the new comments on the videos.

3.1. Data Cleaning

During the data cleaning process, we addressed the presence of unexpected characters by replacing them with relevant punctuation. For example, we transformed (&#39; ) into the apostrophe (′ ), resulting in modifications for a total of 2371 + 83 instances. Additionally, occurrences of (&quot;) were replaced with (“) and (”), with a total of 730 errors identified and rectified. Furthermore, instances of (&lt; 3) were amended to <3, with twelve occurrences addressed. Finally, we removed line breaks that were indicated by (< br >) as we needed the comments to be counted as one and not to be separated.

This study employed the YouTube Data API to systematically extract comments and replies from specified YouTube videos using associated video IDs. The video IDs are provided in Table 1. An API key was configured for authentication, and a systematic approach was adopted to retrieve comments and replies for each video. The script iterated through the list of video IDs, employing the YouTube Data API to retrieve comments in batches of 100, with pagination support for handling larger datasets. The retrieved comments and replies were processed and organised into a pandas DataFrame for each video, facilitating subsequent analysis.

The dataset initially comprised 6885 lines. However, this figure was reduced after we eliminated duplicate lines. punctuation-only lines (such as question marks), and closing quotation marks (indicating the end of quotes from preceding lines) and instances involving full stops, numbers and further duplicates. Ultimately, our analysis is grounded in a dataset encompassing 6573 lines, inclusive of both text and emoji comments. The overview of videos and comment counts is provided in Table 1.

3.2. Data Analysis

We considered seven confidence levels available through the Language Identifier (namely 40%, 50%, 60%, 70%, 80%, 90%, and 99%) in order to evaluate the consistency of the findings. In the end, our discussion is based on the results of the default confidence setting for language identification, that is, 50% confidence. The SA-LID utilises an input file or folder, and the accepted file types are .txt files. It then outputs to either a log file or into a folder. The identity levels include document level and line level. It is important to note that when the line level option is selected, the output setting defaults to the "Copy/Write to folder" option. The output file names append the language code as a prefix to the original file name. 4. Qualitative Error Analysis

In evaluating the performance of the SA-LID on our YouTube corpus (identified for our larger project on the language identities of amaXhosa), we employed a qualitative error analysis. Our analysis was based on the default confidence setting, specifically the 50% confidence level (refer to Table 2 for the results of the SA-LID which reflects the different confidence levels from 40% to 99%).

4.1. The Unsure Caterogy

The SA-LID encountered 339 comments which it found uncertain. Upon examination, we identified a few reasons for the uncertainty. The uncertainty arose primarily from the identification of emojis and unfamiliar slang, such as 'wow' and 'yeah' as well as acronyms such as 'lol' and 'omg'. We assume that such words were not included in the development data for the SA-LID.

Secondly, a notable challenge emerged as we realised that the SA-LID encountered difficulties in accurately categorising language when spelling mistakes were present. Consequently, a significant number of comments ended up in the unsure category. For instance, one comment under the unsure category featured the misspelling 'qween,' which is appropriately spelt as 'queen.' These instances illustrate that when words are misspelt, it becomes more challenging for the SA-LID to accurately identify the languages. This underscores the critical importance of accurate spelling for the SA-LID to perform effectively in language categorisation.

Thirdly, notable instances of unexpected scripts were observed. For instance, the data contained comments in Japanese, Russian, and Arabic. Such scripts are not official in South Africa and, as such, they are not expected to be identified using the SA-LID.

4.2. Multilingual Comments

Ideally, the SA-LID as outlined earlier, will assign the language based on the higher probability. As an example, consider the sentence below:

(b) Awume kancane wena. uShaka uhlanganaphi nokubaleka kwamaXhosa. Ehamba nabelungu. Babuya Kuphi? Asibafuni iningi lethu Kwazulu KwaZulu. Loyalt to nothing asibafuni.

The SA-LID has categorised this sentence under isiZulu since it contains 15 isiZulu words although it also contains three English words, one of which is spelled incorrectly.

Furthermore, we observed that the SA-LID shows a preference for indigenous South African languages when an equal number of words from multiple languages are present in the same comment. To illustrate, consider the following comment classified under the isiXhosa comments:

(c) This woman is talking sh\%t... lo othi xa ungenamgidi awuyndoda... mxfm.... The only part I like is lena athi yimisebenzi yakho ebonakalisa ubudoda.

In this example, the term 'sh%t' is not recognised as English due to the inclusion of punctuation. Upon tokenisation, the word is divided into three tokens, making it less likely to be identified as a valid English word. Additionally, the term 'mxfm' is a misspelt word. Consequently, there are only ten English words in the comment. Similarly, the isiXhosa word 'awuyndoda' is spelt incorrectly, as such there are also ten isiXhosa words. Thus, the comment contains an equal number of English and isiXhosa words but even so, the SA-LID identified the comment as isiXhosa, thereby illustrating a preference for isiXhosa.

In more extreme circumstances, the SA-LID identified code-switched comments that are predominantly English under indigenous languages such as isiXhosa. To illustrate, consider the example below:

(d) Singabantu abanye. Xhosas from Zim moved to Zim from the Eastern Cape with Cecil John Rhodes, Ndicelumenywa.

This code-switched comment exhibits a prevailing use of English, interspersed with three isiXhosa words. The classification of this sentence as isiXhosa reinforces our inference that the SA-LID tends to favour indigenous languages when categorising code-switched texts.

Note that the SA-LID has no category for sentences that are multilingual. This is particularly problematic in the context of South African multilingual social media. That is, there is a need for an additional category in terms of those sentences considered 'multilingual' (rather than assigning them to one of the two language groups present in the sentence). The ability to identify the use of more than one language within a single text effectively ensures an alignment with real-life language use. However, the SA-LID currently identifies at least one language from the comment and then assigns a language label rather than noting the sentence as 'multilingual. Nonetheless, this ability to classify code-switched texts is a significant asset for our study which focuses on how amaXhosa articulate their linguistic identity.

Our aim in this paper was to investigate the ability of the SA-LID to identify comments in isiXhosa. Overall, the SA-LID was able to identify instances of the use of isiXhosa including sentences that are purely in isiXhosa and those that are code-switched.

In the larger study on language identities, we also hope to identify and analyse strategies used by multilingual commentators in their interactions on YouTube as a social media platform. Consequently, the accurate identification of isiXhosa through the SA-LID holds particular significance for our research objectives, facilitating the exclusion of comments lacking isiXhosa content.

4.4. Assumed linguistic and Cultural identities

As we analyzed the comments, we observed a diverse array of languages employed by commentators, including code-switching between indigenous South African languages as well as the unexpected occurrences of Japanese, Russian, and Arabic. We inferred that individuals who use both monolingual and multilingual sentences were concurrently expressing both their thoughts and cultural identities. Drawing on the insights of scholars like Bucholtz and Hall (2004) and others who explore the intricate relationship between language and identity, our findings suggested that commentators were strategically situating their linguistic and cultural identities through their language use.

Nevertheless, we acknowledge the inherently multilingual nature of the world, where individuals can learn languages beyond those spoken at home. According to Kinginger (2004), when individuals speak and learn a new language, they simultaneously adopt a new identity or engage in the reconstruction of their existing one. This concept is illustrated by Johanson Botha (2009)'s example of an English man learning isiXhosa. When he speaks isiXhosa, he becomes loud, which causes embarrassment to his wife. This loudness, not commonly associated with Western culture, is stereotypically linked to isiXhosa culture, portraying the construction of amaXhosa as assertive or loud. Therefore, we understand that to definitively ascertain whether someone identifies as isiXhosa or any other language, further investigation (for example conducting interviews) would be imperative.

Given this context, the primary investigation of our broader study will focus on conducting interviews to confirm linguistic identities. This was not, however, necessary for this paper as the objective was solely to assess the accuracy of the SA-LID when applied to a corpus of YouTube comments.

5. Conclusion

In this paper, we explore the use of automatic language identification using the South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) to discern languages within a YouTube comments corpus. This study forms part of a broader project aiming to uncover linguistic strategies employed by isiXhosa speakers in expressing their language and cultural identities in YouTube comments. As digital platforms continue to shape communication patterns, understanding language identities becomes crucial for fostering inclusive and accurate representation. To facilitate this, there is a need for accurate language identification in multilingual texts. As such, the context of our broader study led us to evaluate the reliability of the SA-LID in identifying any use of isiXhosa language elements in the relevant comments which we mined from YouTube. The question which underpinned our research, as reflected in Section 1, related to whether we could rely on the language identification results generated through the use of the SA-LID to accurately identify all instances of the use of isiXhosa.

Our analysis of the SA-LID reveals both strengths and challenges. The tool demonstrates proficiency in identifying languages used in multilingual comments, showcasing its versatility in capturing dynamic language use within the amaXhosa community on YouTube. This aspect prompts us to conclude that the SA-LID can indeed be effectively employed in situations where two languages coexist or code-switching occurs, showcasing its robust capabilities in language categorisation.

However, challenges arise, particularly in cases of mutual intelligibility between closely related languages like isiXhosa and other Nguni languages. This highlights the complicated nature of language identification and, therefore, urges further exploration.

Other challenges encountered with this tool include uncertainties related to emojis, slang, unconventional spelling and spelling errors. This emphasises the need for continuous refinement in language identification tools to accommodate diverse linguistic expressions. In the context of our corpus, the non-Latin scripts in the dataset further complicated language identification as they are unexpected in the South African context.

Our findings contribute to the ongoing discourse on language use and identity in digital spaces, offering insights into methodologies which can be employed in further research. The misidentification of languages, as noted in this study, opens up opportunities for future studies to explore how the choice of words or phrase structure in a text can potentially confuse a language identifier. In this study, we did not delve into grammatical complexities or sentence structures; our primary focus was to ascertain the ability of the SA-LID to identify the use of isiXhosa from written YouTube comments accurately. We acknowledge that potential issues may have arisen from variations in pre-processing steps. Specifically, different processes may have been employed for tokenisation, text normalisation, and handling special characters compared to those used in the training of the SA-LID.

Abstract

Résumé

The South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) has proven to be a valuable tool for data analysis in the multilingual context of South Africa, particularly in governmental texts. However, its suitability for broader projects has yet to be determined. This paper aims to assess the performance of the SA-LID in identifying isiXhosa in YouTube comments as part of the methodology for research on the expression of cultural identity through linguistic strategies. We curated a selection of 10 videos which focused on the isiXhosa culture in terms of theatre, poetry, language learning, culture, or music. The videos were predominantly in English as were most of the comments but the latter were interspersed with elements of isiXhosa, identifying the commentators as speakers of isiXhosa. The SA-LID was used to identify all instances of the use of isiXhosa to facilitate the analysis of the relevant items. Following the application of the SA-LID to this data, a manual evaluation was conducted to gauge the effectiveness of this tool in selecting all isiXhosa items. Our findings reveal significant limitations in the use of the SA-LID, encompassing the oversight of unconventional spellings in indigenous languages and misclassification of closely related languages within the Nguni group. Although proficient in identifying the use of Nguni languages, differentiating within this language group proved challenging for the SA-LID. These results underscore the necessity for manual checks to complement the use of the SA-LID when other Nguni languages may be present in the comment texts.

2.1. The isiXhosa Language

2.1. La langue isiXhosa

While there are between 24 and 30 spoken languages in South Africa (Finlayson and Madiba, 2002), the constitution of the Republic of South Africa recognises 12 of these as official languages (Republic of South Africa, 1996, 2023). These official languages are typically grouped into six language groups, including: (i) South African Sign Language, (ii) Sotho-Tswana, which includes Sesotho, Setswana, and Sepedi, (iii) Sotho-Makua-Venda, which includes Tshivenḓa, (iv) West Germanic, which includes Afrikaans and English, (v) NguniTsonga, which includes Xitsonga, and (vi) Nguni, which includes isiZulu, Siswati, siNdebele and isiXhosa.

Bien qu'il y ait entre 24 et 30 langues parlées en Afrique du Sud (Finlayson et Madiba, 2002), la constitution de la République d'Afrique du Sud en reconnaît 12 comme langues officielles (République d'Afrique du Sud, 1996, 2023). Ces langues officielles sont généralement regroupées en six groupes linguistiques, notamment : (i) la langue des signes sud-africaine, (ii) le groupe sotho-tswana, qui comprend le sesotho, le setswana et le sepedi, (iii) le groupe sotho-makua-venda, qui comprend le tshivenḓa, (iv) le groupe germanique occidental, qui comprend l'afrikaans et l'anglais, (v) le groupe nguni-tsonga, qui comprend le xitsonga, et (vi) le groupe nguni, qui comprend l'isiZulu, le siswati, le siNdebele et l'isiXhosa.

The Nguni language group occupies a significant position as the largest language group in South Africa. These clicks are represented by the use of three consonants: /c, q, and x/ (Nogwina et al., 2013; Gxowa-Dlayedwa, 2015, 2018; Wheeler, 2018).

2.2. Identifying Languages

2.2. Identification des langues

The initial step in comprehending written text is to ascertain the language in which it is written (Babhulgaonkar and Sonavane, 2020). Various language identification tools are developed for this purpose, with the goal of discerning the language(s) present in the text (Jauhiainen et al., 2024). Note that these language identifiers are designed to encompass both speech and written texts. However, due to the inherent differences between written text, composed of discrete characters, and speech, which involves a continuous signal relying on acoustic features, different natural language processing methods are traditionally employed for text and speech, resulting in limited methodological overlap between the two (Murthy and Kumar, 2006; Ambikairajah et al., 2011).

La première étape de la compréhension du texte écrit consiste à déterminer la langue dans laquelle il est écrit (Babhulgaonkar et Sonavane, 2020). Divers outils d'identification de la langue sont développés à cette fin, dans le but de discerner la ou les langues présentes dans le texte (Jauhiainen et al., 2024). Notez que ces identificateurs de langue sont conçus pour englober à la fois la parole et les textes écrits. Cependant, en raison des différences inhérentes entre le texte écrit, composé de caractères discrets, et la parole, qui implique un signal continu reposant sur des caractéristiques acoustiques, différentes méthodes de traitement du langage naturel sont traditionnellement utilisées pour le texte et la parole, ce qui entraîne un chevauchement méthodologique limité entre les deux (Murthy et Kumar, 2006; Ambikairajah et al., 2011).

For the purpose of this discussion, our focus is specifically on language identifiers for written texts. Text language identification involves analyzing written linguistic features, including character n-grams and word frequency patterns. This analytical process often makes use of statistical models and machine learning algorithms (Nezhadi et al., 2017).

Aux fins de cette discussion, nous nous concentrons spécifiquement sur les identifiants de langue pour les textes écrits. L'identification de la langue du texte consiste à analyser les caractéristiques linguistiques écrites, y compris les n-grammes de caractères et les modèles de fréquence des mots. Ce processus analytique utilise souvent des modèles statistiques et des algorithmes d’apprentissage automatique (Nezhadi et al., 2017).

Traditionally, human beings are regarded as the most accurate language identifiers (Deshwal et al., 2020). Unfortunately, their ability to detect languages is limited by their language repertoires. As such, the limits of relying on humans for language identification become obvious when considering the estimated 7168 languages worldwide (Al-Jarf et al., 2022), or the twelve official languages in South Africa. Simply put, humans are unable to detect languages that are outside their current linguistic repertoires.

Traditionnellement, les êtres humains sont considérés comme les identificateurs de langue les plus précis (Deshwal et al., 2020). Malheureusement, leur capacité à détecter les langues est limitée par leur répertoire linguistique. Ainsi, les limites de la dépendance aux humains pour l'identification de la langue deviennent évidentes lorsque l'on considère les quelque 7168 langues estimées dans le monde (Al-Jarf et al., 2022), ou les douze langues officielles en Afrique du Sud. En termes simples, les humains sont incapables de détecter les langues qui sont en dehors de leurs répertoires linguistiques actuels.

As a result, more non-human dependent approaches are needed in the task of identifying languages. Over time, computational approaches employing tailored algorithms and indexing structures have developed to discern language usage without human intervention (Calvo et al., 2017). This evolution includes the use of advanced techniques such as neural networks (Talpur and O'Sullivan, 2020) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) approaches (Saji et al., 2022), which are integrated into language identification tools.

Par conséquent, davantage d'approches indépendantes des humains sont nécessaires pour la tâche d'identification des langues. Au fil du temps, des approches informatiques employant des algorithmes sur mesure et des structures d'indexation ont été développées pour distinguer l'utilisation des langues sans intervention humaine (Calvo et al., 2017). Cette évolution inclut l'utilisation de techniques avancées telles que les réseaux de neurones artificiels (Talpur et O'Sullivan, 2020) et les approches de traitement automatique des langues (TAL) (Saji et al., 2022), qui sont intégrées dans les outils d'identification de la langue.

2.3. Language Identification Tools

2.3. Outils d'identification de la langue

The introduction of automatic language identifiers serves as a valuable advancement in language detection. The primary task of a language identification tool is to analyse a given spoken or written text and generate a prediction of the language in which the text is spoken or written (Navrátil, 2006; Bergsma et al., 2012; Solorio et al., 2014). This process includes assessing the probability of each word in the provided text as belonging to one or more of the languages in the tool's library (Lui and Baldwin, 2012). The identified language is determined by the highest probability, initiating a competition among language models to determine the most likely match for the entire sample. Like humans, automatic language identifiers also rely on libraries (Agarwal et al., 2023). In this way, the automatic tools need to be trained using different languages and will not be able to detect new languages that are not in their existing libraries.

L'introduction des identifiants linguistiques automatiques constitue une avancée précieuse en détection de langue. La tâche principale d’un outil d’identification de la langue est d’analyser un texte parlé ou écrit donné et de générer une prédiction de la langue dans laquelle le texte est parlé ou écrit (Navrátil, 2006; Bergsma et al., 2012; Solorio et al., 2014). Ce processus comprend l'évaluation de la probabilité que chaque mot du texte fourni appartienne à une ou plusieurs des langues de la bibliothèque de l'outil (Lui et Baldwin, 2012). La langue identifiée est déterminée par la probabilité la plus élevée, initiant une compétition entre les modèles de langage pour déterminer la correspondance la plus probable pour l'échantillon entier. Comme les humains, les identifiants linguistiques automatiques s'appuient également sur des bibliothèques (Agarwal et al., 2023). De cette manière, les outils automatiques doivent être entraînés en utilisant différentes langues et ne pourront pas détecter de nouvelles langues qui ne figurent pas dans leurs bibliothèques existantes.

In this paper, as indicated in Section 1, we conduct a qualitative evaluation of the South African Language Identifier (henceforth SA-LID). The SA-LID was designed to classify text into one of the 11 official written languages of South Africa, either at the document or line level (Puttkammer et al., 2018). The SA-LID has been trained using government text corpora obtained during the National Centre for Human Language Technology (NCHLT) Text project and collected through collaboration between the South African Department of Arts and Culture and the Centre for Text Technology (Puttkammer et al., 2018).

Dans cet article, comme indiqué dans la section 1, nous procédons à une évaluation qualitative de l’identifiant linguistique sud-africain (ci-après SA-LID). Le SA-LID a été conçu pour classer un texte dans l'une des 11 langues écrites officielles d'Afrique du Sud, soit au niveau du document, soit au niveau de la ligne (Puttkammer et al., 2018). Le SA-LID a été entraîné à l’aide de corpus de textes gouvernementaux obtenus dans le cadre du projet Text du National Centre for Human Language Technology (NCHLT) et recueillis dans le cadre d’une collaboration entre le ministère sud-africain des Arts et de la Culture et le Centre for Text Technology (Puttkammer et al., 2018).

The SA-LID uses feature extraction, identifying language-specific patterns through the analysis of character n-grams, ranging from bigrams to 6-grams. The model was trained using the Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier, incorporating labelled training samples and the selected feature extractor. These components collectively enable the model to discern languages effectively based on the extracted features. In the subsequent step of text classification, the trained classifier is applied to text inputs, resulting in a list of probable languages arranged by their respective probabilities. The final language determination is achieved by selecting the language with the highest probability, based on the model's consideration of learned patterns and characteristics during the training process, ensuring accurate identification of the language in the given text.

Lors de l'étape suivante de classification de texte, le classificateur entraîné est appliqué aux entrées de texte, résultant en une liste de langues probables classées par leurs probabilités respectives. La détermination finale de la langue est réalisée en sélectionnant la langue avec la probabilité la plus élevée, basée sur la considération par le modèle des motifs et caractéristiques appris lors du processus d'apprentissage, garantissant une identification précise de la langue dans le texte donné.

Nous en avons déduit que les personnes qui utilisent à la fois des phrases monolingues et multilingues exprimaient simultanément leurs pensées et leurs identités culturelles. En nous appuyant sur les travaux de chercheurs comme Bucholtz et Hall (2004) et d'autres qui explorent la relation complexe entre la langue et l'identité, nos résultats ont suggéré que les commentateurs situaient stratégiquement leurs identités linguistiques et culturelles par leur utilisation de la langue.

Nevertheless, we acknowledge the inherently multilingual nature of the world, where individuals can learn languages beyond those spoken at home. According to Kinginger (2004), when individuals speak and learn a new language, they simultaneously adopt a new identity or engage in the reconstruction of their existing one. This concept is illustrated by Johanson Botha (2009)'s example of an English man learning isiXhosa. When he speaks isiXhosa, he becomes loud, which causes embarrassment to his wife. This loudness, not commonly associated with Western culture, is stereotypically linked to isiXhosa culture, portraying the construction of amaXhosa as assertive or loud. Therefore, we understand that to definitively ascertain whether someone identifies as isiXhosa or any other language, further investigation (for example conducting interviews) would be imperative.

Cette intensité vocale, qui n'est pas communément associée à la culture occidentale, est stéréotypiquement liée à la culture isiXhosa, dépeignant la construction des amaXhosa comme assertifs ou bruyants. Par conséquent, nous comprenons que pour déterminer de manière définitive si quelqu'un s'identifie comme isiXhosa ou toute autre langue, une enquête plus approfondie (par exemple la réalisation d'entretiens) serait impérative.

Given this context, the primary investigation of our broader study will focus on conducting interviews to confirm linguistic identities. This was not, however, necessary for this paper as the objective was solely to assess the accuracy of the SA-LID when applied to a corpus of YouTube comments.

Dans ce contexte, l'investigation principale de notre étude plus large portera sur la réalisation d'entretiens pour confirmer les identités linguistiques. Cela n'était cependant pas nécessaire pour cet article, car l'objectif était uniquement d'évaluer la précision de SA-LID lorsqu'il est appliqué à un corpus de commentaires YouTube.

5. Conclusion

In this paper, we explore the use of automatic language identification using the South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) to discern languages within a YouTube comments corpus. This study forms part of a broader project aiming to uncover linguistic strategies employed by isiXhosa speakers in expressing their language and cultural identities in YouTube comments. As digital platforms continue to shape communication patterns, understanding language identities becomes crucial for fostering inclusive and accurate representation. To facilitate this, there is a need for accurate language identification in multilingual texts. As such, the context of our broader study led us to evaluate the reliability of the SA-LID in identifying any use of isiXhosa language elements in the relevant comments which we mined from YouTube. The question which underpinned our research, as reflected in Section 1, related to whether we could rely on the language identification results generated through the use of the SA-LID to accurately identify all instances of the use of isiXhosa.

Dans cet article, nous explorons l'utilisation de l'identification automatique de la langue à l'aide du South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) pour discerner les langues au sein d'un corpus de commentaires YouTube. Cette étude fait partie d'un projet plus large visant à découvrir les stratégies linguistiques employées par les locuteurs isiXhosa pour exprimer leur langue et leurs identités culturelles dans les commentaires YouTube. À mesure que les plateformes numériques continuent de façonner les schémas de communication, la compréhension des identités linguistiques devient cruciale pour favoriser une représentation inclusive et précise. Pour faciliter cela, il est nécessaire de disposer d'une identification précise de la langue dans les textes multilingues. Ainsi, le contexte de notre étude plus large nous a conduits à évaluer la fiabilité du SA-LID pour identifier toute utilisation d'éléments de la langue isiXhosa dans les commentaires pertinents que nous avons extraits de YouTube. La question qui sous-tendait notre recherche, telle que reflétée dans la section 1, portait sur la question de savoir si nous pouvions nous fier aux résultats d'identification de la langue générés par l'utilisation du SA-LID pour identifier avec précision toutes les instances d'utilisation d'isiXhosa.

Our analysis of the SA-LID reveals both strengths and challenges. The tool demonstrates proficiency in identifying languages used in multilingual comments, showcasing its versatility in capturing dynamic language use within the amaXhosa community on YouTube. This aspect prompts us to conclude that the SA-LID can indeed be effectively employed in situations where two languages coexist or code-switching occurs, showcasing its robust capabilities in language categorisation.

Notre analyse de la SA-LID révèle à la fois des forces et des défis. L'outil démontre sa compétence dans l'identification des langues utilisées dans les commentaires multilingues, démontrant sa polyvalence dans la capture de l'utilisation dynamique du langage au sein de la communauté amaXhosa sur YouTube. Cet aspect nous amène à conclure que la SA-LID peut effectivement être employée de manière efficace dans des situations où deux langues coexistent ou où il y a alternance codique, mettant en avant ses capacités robustes en catégorisation des langues.

However, challenges arise, particularly in cases of mutual intelligibility between closely related languages like isiXhosa and other Nguni languages. This highlights the complicated nature of language identification and, therefore, urges further exploration.

Cependant, des défis surgissent, particulièrement dans les cas d'intelligibilité mutuelle entre des langues étroitement apparentées comme l'isiXhosa et les autres langues nguni. Cela met en évidence la nature complexe de l'identification de la langue et, par conséquent, demande une exploration plus approfondie.

Other challenges encountered with this tool include uncertainties related to emojis, slang, unconventional spelling and spelling errors. This emphasises the need for continuous refinement in language identification tools to accommodate diverse linguistic expressions. In the context of our corpus, the non-Latin scripts in the dataset further complicated language identification as they are unexpected in the South African context.

D'autres défis rencontrés avec cet outil comprennent les incertitudes liées aux emojis, à l'argot, à l'orthographe non conventionnelle et aux erreurs d'orthographe. Cela souligne la nécessité d'un perfectionnement continu des outils d'identification de la langue pour prendre en compte des expressions linguistiques diverses. Dans le contexte de notre corpus, les écritures non latines dans l'ensemble de données ont compliqué davantage l'identification de la langue car elles sont inattendues dans le contexte sud-africain.

Our findings contribute to the ongoing discourse on language use and identity in digital spaces, offering insights into methodologies which can be employed in further research. The misidentification of languages, as noted in this study, opens up opportunities for future studies to explore how the choice of words or phrase structure in a text can potentially confuse a language identifier. In this study, we did not delve into grammatical complexities or sentence structures; our primary focus was to ascertain the ability of the SA-LID to identify the use of isiXhosa from written YouTube comments accurately. We acknowledge that potential issues may have arisen from variations in pre-processing steps. Specifically, different processes may have been employed for tokenisation, text normalisation, and handling special characters compared to those used in the training of the SA-LID.

Nos résultats contribuent au discours en cours sur l'utilisation de la langue et l'identité dans les espaces numériques, offrant un aperçu des méthodologies qui peuvent être utilisées dans d'autres recherches. La mauvaise identification des langues, telle que notée dans cette étude, ouvre la voie à de futures études pour explorer comment le choix des mots ou de la structure des phrases dans un texte peut potentiellement confondre un identificateur de langue. Dans cette étude, nous n'avons pas approfondi les complexités grammaticales ou les structures de phrases; notre objectif principal était de déterminer la capacité du SA-LID à identifier avec précision l'utilisation de isiXhosa à partir de commentaires écrits sur YouTube. Nous reconnaissons que des problèmes potentiels peuvent avoir été causés par des variations dans les étapes de prétraitement. Plus précisément, des processus différents peuvent avoir été utilisés pour la tokenisation, la normalisation du texte et le traitement des caractères spéciaux par rapport à ceux utilisés dans l'entraînement du SA-LID.

1. Utangulizi

The global linguistic landscape, comprising approximately 7168 languages, is dynamic and demands continuous exploration (Aitchison, 2005; Trask, 2003). This is particularly true in light of the core role played by language in terms of the social, cultural, intellectual and political vitality in any society (Lo Bianco, 2010). As such, there is a need for continuing research in order to understand the characteristics of each language as well as the cultures and identities that are linked to the concerned linguistic communities.

Mandhari ya lugha duniani, inayojumuisha takriban lugha 7168, ni badilifu na inadai uchunguzi endelevu (Aitchison, 2005; Trask, 2003). Hii ni kweli hasa kwa kuzingatia jukumu la msingi linalotekelezwa na lugha katika suala la uhai wa kijamii, kiutamaduni, kiakili na kisiasa katika jamii yoyote (Lo Bianco, 2010). Kwa hivyo, kuna haja ya utafiti unaoendelea ili kuelewa sifa za kila lugha pamoja na tamaduni na utambulisho unaohusishwa na jamii za kilugha zinazohusika.

Given the global linguistic diversity, an ability to distinguish between the languages being used in a particular context is understandably significant (Jaspers, 2015). Such an ability facilitates the decoding of the content of the message and thus fosters effective communication and comprehension (Hardan, 2013). This is particularly significant in a linguistically diverse country like South Africa (Fishman et al., 2008), where most citizens are multilingual (Evans, 2015; Sithole, 2015; Adelani et al., 2021).

Kwa kuzingatia utofauti wa lugha duniani, uwezo wa kutofautisha kati ya lugha zinazotumika katika muktadha fulani ni muhimu kwa kueleweka (Jaspers, 2015). Uwezo kama huo hurahisisha ufafanuzi wa maudhui ya ujumbe na hivyo kukuza mawasiliano na ufahamu wenye ufanisi (Hardan, 2013). Hili ni muhimu hasa katika nchi yenye utofauti wa lugha kama Afrika Kusini (Fishman et al., 2008), ambako wananchi wengi wana uwezo wa kutumia lugha nyingi (Evans, 2015; Sithole, 2015; Adelani et al., 2021).

Through language, individuals not only communicate but also articulate their origins, making it a fundamental dimension of cultural identity. In this, and many other ways, language and identity are intricately linked (Bucholtz and Hall, 2004). This aspect of language use extends beyond oral expression to also encompass written interactions and specifically so in colloquial contexts which allow for a more spontaneous and free use of language - for example, social media platforms. A tool which has the ability to accurately identify the languages used in a multilingual text could carry numerous benefits and play an important role in a linguistically diverse society. This would be especially true in terms of research focused on the actual use of language by those fluent in more than one language and the manner in which their language use expresses their cultural identity.

Kupitia lugha, watu binafsi sio tu kwamba wanawasiliana bali pia wanadhihirisha asili zao, na hivyo kuifanya kuwa mwelekeo wa msingi wa utambulisho wa kitamaduni. Katika njia hii, na nyingine nyingi, lugha na utambulisho vimefungamana kwa kina (Bucholtz and Hall, 2004). Kipengele hiki cha matumizi ya lugha kinaenea zaidi ya usemi wa mdomo hadi kujumuisha pia mwingiliano wa maandishi na hasa katika miktadha ya mazungumzo ya kawaida ambayo inaruhusu matumizi ya lugha ya hiari na huru zaidi - kwa mfano, majukwaa ya mitandao ya kijamii. Zana ambayo ina uwezo wa kutambua kwa usahihi lugha zinazotumika katika maandishi yenye lugha nyingi inaweza kuleta manufaa mengi na kuchukua nafasi muhimu katika jamii yenye utofauti wa lugha. Hili lingekuwa kweli hasa kuhusiana na utafiti unaolenga matumizi halisi ya lugha na wale wanaozungumza kwa ufasaha zaidi ya lugha moja na namna ambavyo matumizi yao ya lugha yanadhihirisha utambulisho wao wa kitamaduni.

Identity, in its simplest form, is an expression of individuality and reflects the uniqueness of every human being (Buckingham, 2008). However, identity is also influenced to a large extent by the social groups to which an individual belongs (Baxter, 2016). This is particularly so in terms of the cultural and linguistic background into which one is born as this is the context in which one first learns about - and learns how to express - aspects of the world (Praeg, 2014). Linguistic and cultural identity are generally conflated and language use is often reflective of these aspects - along with other 'hints' about a speaker's identity (Bucholtz and Hall, 2005). For this reason, the relationship between language usage and cultural/linguistic identity is rife with possibilities.

Utambulisho, katika umbo lake rahisi zaidi, ni udhihirisho wa ubinafsi na unaakisi upekee wa kila binadamu (Buckingham, 2008). Hata hivyo, utambulisho pia huathiriwa kwa kiasi kikubwa na makundi ya kijamii ambayo mtu binafsi anayashiriki (Baxter, 2016). Hii ni kweli hasa kuhusiana na usuli wa kitamaduni na wa lugha ambao mtu huzaliwa ndani yake kwani huu ndio muktadha ambapo mtu hujifunza kwanza kuhusu - na kujifunza jinsi ya kueleza - vipengele vya ulimwengu (Praeg, 2014). Utambulisho wa lugha na wa kitamaduni kwa ujumla huchanganywa na matumizi ya lugha mara nyingi huakisi vipengele hivi - pamoja na 'vidokezo' vingine kuhusu utambulisho wa mzungumzaji (Bucholtz na Hall, 2005). Kwa sababu hii, uhusiano kati ya matumizi ya lugha na utambulisho wa kitamaduni/lugha umejaa uwezekano.

Research on the link between language use and cultural or linguistic identity in a multilingual context assumes the ability to discern between the languages employed by the language users in that community. This is indeed the case with the research project of which this paper forms a component. The broader study aims to investigate linguistic strategies employed by isiXhosa language users to express their language identities on YouTube through the use of comments.

Utafiti kuhusu uhusiano kati ya matumizi ya lugha na utambulisho wa kitamaduni au wa lugha katika muktadha wa lugha nyingi huchukulia uwezo wa kutofautisha kati ya lugha zinazotumiwa na watumiaji wa lugha katika jamii hiyo. Hii ndiyo hali halisi kwa mradi wa utafiti ambao makala hii ni sehemu yake. Utafiti mpana zaidi unalenga kuchunguza mikakati ya lugha inayotumiwa na watumiaji wa lugha ya isiXhosa kueleza utambulisho wao wa lugha kwenye YouTube kupitia matumizi ya maoni.

The scope of this study, therefore, underscores the necessity of a reliable language identifier to accurately detect the language(s) used within a particular text. The use of such a tool becomes indispensable when navigating through the substantial pool of comments in order to extract comments written in isiXhosa or code-switched between isiXhosa and other languages. The identification of instances of isiXhosa usage from our corpus of YouTube comments is thus necessary in order to delineate the data on which our study will focus.

Kwa hiyo, wigo wa utafiti huu unasisitiza umuhimu wa kitambulishi cha lugha cha kuaminika ili kutambua kwa usahihi lugha zinazotumiwa ndani ya maandishi fulani. Matumizi ya zana kama hiyo huwa ya lazima wakati wa kupitia kundi kubwa la maoni ili kutoa maoni yaliyoandikwa kwa isiXhosa au yaliyochanganywa msimbo kati ya isiXhosa na lugha nyingine. Kwa hivyo, utambuzi wa matukio ya matumizi ya isiXhosa kutoka kwenye korasi yetu ya maoni ya YouTube ni muhimu ili kufafanua data ambayo utafiti wetu utalenga.

This paper aims to evaluate the reliability of the South African Language Identifier (Puttkammer et al., 2016) when it is applied to a corpus of YouTube comments to ascertain the languages used. The paper concludes with a discussion of our conclusions and recommendations in Section 5.

2. Background

2. Usuli

2.1. The isiXhosa Language

2.1. Lugha ya isiXhosa

While there are between 24 and 30 spoken languages in South Africa (Finlayson and Madiba, 2002), the constitution of the Republic of South Africa recognises 12 of these as official languages (Republic of South Africa, 1996, 2023). These official languages are typically grouped into six language groups, including: (i) South African Sign Language, (ii) Sotho-Tswana, which includes Sesotho, Setswana, and Sepedi, (iii) Sotho-Makua-Venda, which includes Tshivenḓa, (iv) West Germanic, which includes Afrikaans and English, (v) NguniTsonga, which includes Xitsonga, and (vi) Nguni, which includes isiZulu, Siswati, siNdebele and isiXhosa.

Ingawa kuna kati ya lugha 24 na 30 zinazozungumzwa Afrika Kusini (Finlayson and Madiba, 2002), katiba ya Jamhuri ya Afrika Kusini inatambua 12 kati ya hizo kama lugha rasmi (Republic of South Africa, 1996, 2023). Lugha hizi rasmi kwa kawaida zimepangwa katika vikundi sita vya lugha, vikijumuisha: (i) Lugha ya Ishara ya Afrika Kusini, (ii) Kisotho-Tswana, kinachojumuisha Kisotho, Kitswana, na Kipedi, (iii) Kisotho-Makua-Venda, kinachojumuisha Tshivenḓa, (iv) Kijerumaniki cha Magharibi, kinachojumuisha Kiafrikana na Kiingereza, (v) KinguniTsonga, kinachojumuisha Xitsonga, na (vi) Kinguni, kinachojumuisha isiZulu, Siswati, siNdebele na isiXhosa.

The Nguni language group occupies a significant position as the largest language group in South Africa. IsiXhosa, the second-most prominent Nguni language within South Africa, (Wheeler, 2018), is predominantly spoken in the Eastern Cape and the Western Cape Provinces. Notably, it is also officially recognised in Zimbabwe (Republic of Zimbabwe, 2021). According to Wheeler (2018), isiXhosa has much in common with isiZulu in terms of their linguistic roots. In fact, as discussed later in Section 4.3, isiXhosa demonstrates close linguistic ties and mutual intelligibility with other languages in the Nguni group as well (Dyers, 2000). Additionally, isiXhosa stands out for its use of clicks, a feature present in only about 0.5% of the world's languages (Brenzinger and Shah, 2023), including a few Bantu languages. These clicks are represented by the use of three consonants: /c, q, and x/ (Nogwina et al., 2013; Gxowa-Dlayedwa, 2015, 2018; Wheeler, 2018).

Kundi la lugha la Nguni linashikilia nafasi muhimu kama kundi kubwa zaidi la lugha nchini Afrika Kusini. IsiXhosa, lugha ya pili kwa umaarufu ya Nguni ndani ya Afrika Kusini, (Wheeler, 2018), inazungumzwa zaidi katika Mikoa ya Rasi ya Mashariki na Rasi ya Magharibi. Inafaa kuzingatia, pia inatambuliwa rasmi nchini Zimbabwe (Republic of Zimbabwe, 2021). Kulingana na Wheeler (2018), isiXhosa ina mambo mengi yanayofanana na isiZulu kuhusu mizizi yao ya kilugha. Kwa hakika, kama inavyojadiliwa baadaye katika Sehemu ya 4.3, isiXhosa inaonyesha uhusiano wa karibu wa kilugha na uelewano wa pande zote na lugha nyingine katika kundi la Nguni pia (Dyers, 2000). Zaidi ya hayo, isiXhosa inajitokeza kwa matumizi yake ya milio ya kubofya, sifa inayopatikana katika takriban asilimia 0.5 tu ya lugha za dunia (Brenzinger and Shah, 2023), ikijumuisha Lugha chache za Kibantu. Milio hii ya kubofya inawakilishwa kwa matumizi ya konsonanti tatu: /c, q, and x/ (Nogwina et al., 2013; Gxowa-Dlayedwa, 2015, 2018; Wheeler, 2018).

2.2. Identifying Languages

2.2. Kutambua Lugha

The initial step in comprehending written text is to ascertain the language in which it is written (Babhulgaonkar and Sonavane, 2020). Various language identification tools are developed for this purpose, with the goal of discerning the language(s) present in the text (Jauhiainen et al., 2024). Note that these language identifiers are designed to encompass both speech and written texts. However, due to the inherent differences between written text, composed of discrete characters, and speech, which involves a continuous signal relying on acoustic features, different natural language processing methods are traditionally employed for text and speech, resulting in limited methodological overlap between the two (Murthy and Kumar, 2006; Ambikairajah et al., 2011).

Hatua ya awali katika kuelewa maandishi ni kubaini lugha ambayo yameandikwa (Babhulgaonkar and Sonavane, 2020). Zana mbalimbali za utambuzi wa lugha zimetengenezwa kwa madhumuni haya, kwa lengo la kubaini lugha zilizopo katika maandishi (Jauhiainen et al., 2024). Kumbuka kwamba vitambulishi hivi vya lugha vimeundwa kujumuisha usemi na maandishi. Hata hivyo, kutokana na tofauti za kiasili kati ya maandishi, yanayoundwa na herufi za pekee, na usemi, unaohusisha ishara endelevu inayotegemea sifa za akustika, mbinu tofauti za uchakataji wa lugha asilia hutumika kijadi kwa maandishi na usemi, hivyo kusababisha mwingiliano mdogo wa kimbinu kati ya hivyo viwili (Murthy na Kumar, 2006; Ambikairajah et al., 2011).

For the purpose of this discussion, our focus is specifically on language identifiers for written texts. Text language identification involves analyzing written linguistic features, including character n-grams and word frequency patterns. This analytical process often makes use of statistical models and machine learning algorithms (Nezhadi et al., 2017).

Kwa madhumuni ya mjadala huu, lengo letu ni hasa vitambulishi vya lugha kwa maandishi. Utambuzi wa lugha ya maandishi unahusisha kuchanganua sifa za kiisimu za maandishi, ikiwemo n-gramu za herufi na mifumo ya marudio ya maneno. Mchakato huu wa uchanganuzi mara nyingi hutumia mifano ya kitakwimu na algoriti za ujifunzaji wa mashine (Nezhadi et al., 2017).

Traditionally, human beings are regarded as the most accurate language identifiers (Deshwal et al., 2020). Unfortunately, their ability to detect languages is limited by their language repertoires. As such, the limits of relying on humans for language identification become obvious when considering the estimated 7168 languages worldwide (Al-Jarf et al., 2022), or the twelve official languages in South Africa. Simply put, humans are unable to detect languages that are outside their current linguistic repertoires.

Kwa kawaida, binadamu huchukuliwa kama watambulishaji sahihi zaidi wa lugha (Deshwal et al., 2020). Kwa bahati mbaya, uwezo wao wa kutambua lugha umepunguzwa na msamiati wao wa lugha. Kwa hivyo, mipaka ya kutegemea binadamu kwa utambuzi wa lugha inadhihirika wakati wa kuzingatia makadirio ya lugha 7168 duniani kote (Al-Jarf et al., 2022), au lugha rasmi kumi na mbili nchini Afrika Kusini. Kwa ufupi, binadamu hawawezi kutambua lugha zilizo nje ya repertoa zao za sasa za kilugha.

As a result, more non-human dependent approaches are needed in the task of identifying languages. Over time, computational approaches employing tailored algorithms and indexing structures have developed to discern language usage without human intervention (Calvo et al., 2017). This evolution includes the use of advanced techniques such as neural networks (Talpur and O'Sullivan, 2020) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) approaches (Saji et al., 2022), which are integrated into language identification tools.

Kwa sababu hiyo, mbinu zaidi zisizotegemea binadamu zinahitajika katika kazi ya kutambua lugha. Baada ya muda, mbinu za kikompyuta zinazotumia algoriti maalum na miundo ya kuorodhesha zimeendelezwa ili kutambua matumizi ya lugha bila uingiliaji wa binadamu (Calvo et al., 2017). Mageuzi haya yanajumuisha matumizi ya mbinu za hali ya juu kama vile mitandao ya neva (Talpur and O'Sullivan, 2020) na mbinu za Uchakataji wa Lugha Asilia (NLP) (Saji et al., 2022), ambazo zimeunganishwa katika zana za utambuzi wa lugha.

2.3. Language Identification Tools

2.3. Zana za Utambuzi wa Lugha

The introduction of automatic language identifiers serves as a valuable advancement in language detection. The primary task of a language identification tool is to analyse a given spoken or written text and generate a prediction of the language in which the text is spoken or written (Navrátil, 2006; Bergsma et al., 2012; Solorio et al., 2014). This process includes assessing the probability of each word in the provided text as belonging to one or more of the languages in the tool's library (Lui and Baldwin, 2012). The identified language is determined by the highest probability, initiating a competition among language models to determine the most likely match for the entire sample. Like humans, automatic language identifiers also rely on libraries (Agarwal et al., 2023). In this way, the automatic tools need to be trained using different languages and will not be able to detect new languages that are not in their existing libraries.

Kuanzishwa kwa vitambuzi vya lugha otomatiki kunatumika kama maendeleo muhimu katika utambuzi wa lugha. Kazi kuu ya zana ya utambuzi wa lugha ni kuchanganua maandishi yaliyotamkwa au kuandikwa na kutoa utabiri wa lugha ambayo maandishi hayo yametamkwa au kuandikwa (Navrátil, 2006; Bergsma et al., 2012; Solorio et al., 2014). Mchakato huu unajumuisha kutathmini uwezekano wa kila neno katika maandishi yaliyotolewa kuwa ni mali ya lugha moja au zaidi katika maktaba ya zana (Lui na Baldwin, 2012). Lugha iliyotambuliwa huamuliwa na uwezekano wa juu zaidi, ikianzisha ushindani kati ya miundo ya lugha ili kubaini ulinganifu unaowezekana zaidi kwa sampuli nzima. Kama binadamu, vitambulishi vya lugha vya kiotomatiki pia hutegemea maktaba (Agarwal et al., 2023). Kwa njia hii, zana za kiotomatiki zinahitaji kufunzwa kwa kutumia lugha tofauti na hazitaweza kutambua lugha mpya ambazo hazipo katika maktaba zao zilizopo.

In this paper, as indicated in Section 1, we conduct a qualitative evaluation of the South African Language Identifier (henceforth SA-LID). The SA-LID was designed to classify text into one of the 11 official written languages of South Africa, either at the document or line level (Puttkammer et al., 2018). The SA-LID has been trained using government text corpora obtained during the National Centre for Human Language Technology (NCHLT) Text project and collected through collaboration between the South African Department of Arts and Culture and the Centre for Text Technology (Puttkammer et al., 2018).

Katika makala haya, kama ilivyoonyeshwa katika Sehemu ya 1, tunafanya tathmini ya kimaelezo ya Kitambulishi cha Lugha cha Afrika Kusini (hapa kikijulikana kama SA-LID). SA-LID iliundwa kuainisha maandishi katika mojawapo ya lugha 11 rasmi za maandishi za Afrika Kusini, ama katika kiwango cha hati au cha mstari (Puttkammer et al., 2018). SA-LID imefunzwa kwa kutumia korasi za maandishi za serikali zilizopatikana wakati wa mradi wa Maandishi wa Kituo cha Kitaifa cha Teknolojia ya Lugha ya Binadamu (NCHLT) na kukusanywa kupitia ushirikiano kati ya Idara ya Sanaa na Utamaduni ya Afrika Kusini na Kituo cha Teknolojia ya Maandishi (Puttkammer et al., 2018).

The SA-LID uses feature extraction, identifying language-specific patterns through the analysis of character n-grams, ranging from bigrams to 6-grams. The model was trained using the Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier, incorporating labelled training samples and the selected feature extractor. These components collectively enable the model to discern languages effectively based on the extracted features. In the subsequent step of text classification, the trained classifier is applied to text inputs, resulting in a list of probable languages arranged by their respective probabilities. The final language determination is achieved by selecting the language with the highest probability, based on the model's consideration of learned patterns and characteristics during the training process, ensuring accurate identification of the language in the given text.

SA-LID hutumia uchimbaji wa sifa, ikitambua mifumo mahususi ya lugha kupitia uchanganuzi wa n-gramu za herufi, kuanzia bigramu hadi 6-gramu. Mfano huo ulifunzwa kwa kutumia kiainishaji cha Multinomial Naive Bayes, kikijumuisha sampuli za mafunzo zilizo na lebo na kichimbuzi cha vipengele kilichochaguliwa. Vipengele hivi kwa pamoja huwezesha mfano huo kutambua lugha kwa ufanisi kulingana na vipengele vilivyotolewa. Katika hatua inayofuata ya uainishaji wa maandishi, kiainishaji kilichofunzwa kinatumika kwa pembejeo za maandishi, na kusababisha orodha ya lugha zinazowezekana zilizopangwa kulingana na uwezekano wake husika. Uamuzi wa mwisho wa lugha unafikiwa kwa kuchagua lugha yenye uwezekano mkubwa zaidi, kwa kuzingatia mazingatio ya mfano ya mifumo na sifa zilizojifunza wakati wa mchakato wa mafunzo, kuhakikisha utambuzi sahihi wa lugha katika maandishi yaliyotolewa.

2.4. Related Research

2.4 Utafiti unaohusiana na hilo

Previous research has explored the application of language identification specifically to isiXhosa texts (Kyeyune, 2015). In their study, Kyeyune (2015) utilised corpora from the Language Resource Management Agency and employed the Java Text Categorising Library to extract n-grams for identifying isiXhosa using an n-gram language model. Additionally, they incorporated a lexicon-based classifier to differentiate the specific South African language in which the text is composed. Furthermore, in their work, Duvenhage (2019) introduces a hierarchical classifier that combines naive Bayesian and lexicon-based approaches for short-text Language Identification (LID). This approach proves particularly beneficial for under-resourced languages.

Utafiti wa awali umechunguza matumizi ya utambuzi wa lugha hasa kwa maandishi ya isiXhosa (Kyeyune, 2015). Utafiti uliofanywa na Duvenhage et al. (2017) ulichunguza matumizi ya kiainishaji cha naive Bayes kwa utambuzi sahihi wa kikundi cha lugha. Zaidi ya hayo, walijumuisha kiainishaji chenye msingi wa leksika ili kutofautisha lugha mahususi ya Afrika Kusini ambayo maandishi yametungwa. Zaidi ya hayo, katika kazi yao, Duvenhage (2019) anatambulisha kiainishaji cha ngazi mbalimbali kinachochanganya mbinu za naive Bayesian na zenye msingi wa leksika kwa ajili ya Utambuzi wa Lugha (LID) wa maandishi mafupi. Mbinu hii inathibitika kuwa yenye manufaa hasa kwa lugha zenye rasilimali chache.

The SA-LID utilises an input file or folder, and the accepted file types are .txt files. It then outputs to either a log file or into a folder. The identity levels include document level and line level. It is important to note that when the line level option is selected, the output setting defaults to the "Copy/Write to folder" option. A screenshot of the interface is illustrated in Figure 1. The output files classify the sentences, so each output file includes only sentences identified as the specified language. The output file names append the language code as a prefix to the original file name. For example, when using the original file name for a bilingual dataset for English and isiZulu, dataset.txt, the SA-LID will output zu.dataset.txt and en.dataset.txt.

SA-LID hutumia faili ya pembejeo au folda, na aina za faili zinazokubalika ni faili za .txt. Kisha hutoa matokeo kwa faili ya kumbukumbu au ndani ya folda. Viwango vya utambulisho vinajumuisha kiwango cha hati na kiwango cha mstari. Ni muhimu kutambua kwamba wakati chaguo la kiwango cha mstari linapochaguliwa, mpangilio wa matokeo hubadilika kuwa chaguo la "Nakili/Andika kwenye folda". Picha ya skrini ya kiolesura imeonyeshwa katika Kielelezo cha 1. Faili za matokeo huainisha sentensi, hivyo kila faili ya matokeo hujumuisha sentensi zilizotambuliwa tu kama lugha iliyobainishwa. Majina ya faili za matokeo huongeza msimbo wa lugha kama kiambishi awali kwa jina la faili asili. Kwa mfano, wakati wa kutumia jina la faili asili kwa seti ya data ya lugha mbili kwa Kiingereza na isiZulu, dataset.txt, SA-LID itatoa zu.dataset.txt na en.dataset.txt.

4.1. The Unsure Caterogy

4.1. Kundi la wasio na uhakika

The SA-LID encountered 339 comments which it found uncertain. Upon examination, we identified a few reasons for the uncertainty. The uncertainty arose primarily from the identification of emojis and unfamiliar slang, such as 'wow' and 'yeah' as well as acronyms such as 'lol' and 'omg'. We assume that such words were not included in the development data for the SA-LID.

SA-LID ilikutana na maoni 339 ambayo iliyapata kuwa na kutokuwa na uhakika. Baada ya uchunguzi, tulibaini sababu chache za kutokuwa na uhakika. Kutokuwa na uhakika kulitokana hasa na utambuzi wa emojis na misimu isiyojulikana, kama vile 'wow' na 'yeah' pamoja na vifupisho kama vile 'lol' na 'omg'. Tunadhani kwamba maneno kama hayo hayakujumuishwa katika data ya maendeleo ya SA-LID.

Secondly, a notable challenge emerged as we realised that the SA-LID encountered difficulties in accurately categorising language when spelling mistakes were present. Consequently, a significant number of comments ended up in the unsure category. For instance, one comment under the unsure category featured the misspelling 'qween,' which is appropriately spelt as 'queen.' These instances illustrate that when words are misspelt, it becomes more challenging for the SA-LID to accurately identify the languages. This underscores the critical importance of accurate spelling for the SA-LID to perform effectively in language categorisation.

Pili, changamoto kubwa ilijitokeza tulipogundua kwamba SA-LID ilikumbana na matatizo katika kuainisha lugha kwa usahihi wakati makosa ya tahajia yalipokuwepo. Kwa hiyo, idadi kubwa ya maoni iliishia katika kategoria ya kutokuwa na uhakika. Kwa mfano, maoni moja chini ya kategoria ya kutokuwa na uhakika yalikuwa na tahajia isiyo sahihi 'qween,' ambayo kwa usahihi inaandikwa kama 'queen.' Mifano hii inaonyesha kwamba maneno yanapoandikwa vibaya, inakuwa vigumu zaidi kwa SA-LID kutambua lugha kwa usahihi. Hii inasisitiza umuhimu mkubwa wa tahajia sahihi kwa SA-LID kufanya kazi kwa ufanisi katika uainishaji wa lugha.

Thirdly, notable instances of unexpected scripts were observed. For instance, the data contained comments in Japanese, Russian, and Arabic. Such scripts are not official in South Africa and, as such, they are not expected to be identified using the SA-LID.

Tatu, matukio muhimu ya mifumo ya uandishi isiyotarajiwa yalionekana. Kwa mfano, data ilikuwa na maoni katika Kijapani, Kirusi, na Kiarabu. Mifumo hiyo ya uandishi si rasmi nchini Afrika Kusini na, kwa hivyo, haitarajiwi kutambuliwa kwa kutumia SA-LID.

We also noted that some comments in English were also categorised as unsure. For example, consider the comment:

Tulibaini pia kwamba baadhi ya maoni kwa Kiingereza yaliainishwa kama yasiyo na uhakika. Kwa mfano, zingatia maoni haya:

We are unable to account for these results. However, such occurrences prompt questions about whether the majority of words in such comments were absent from the language library used by the identifier.

Hatuwezi kutoa sababu za matokeo haya. Hata hivyo, matukio kama hayo yanaibua maswali kuhusu kama maneno mengi katika maoni hayo hayakuwepo kwenye maktaba ya lugha iliyotumiwa na kitambulishi.

4.2. Multilingual Comments

4.2. Maoni ya Lugha Nyingi

Ideally, the SA-LID as outlined earlier, will assign the language based on the higher probability. As an example, consider the sentence below:

Kwa hakika, SA-LID kama ilivyoelezwa awali, itagawa lugha kulingana na uwezekano wa juu zaidi. Kama mfano, zingatia sentensi ifuatayo:

(b) Awume kancane wena. uShaka uhlanganaphi nokubaleka kwamaXhosa. Ehamba nabelungu. Babuya Kuphi? Asibafuni iningi lethu Kwazulu KwaZulu. Loyalt to nothing asibafuni.

The SA-LID has categorised this sentence under isiZulu since it contains 15 isiZulu words although it also contains three English words, one of which is spelled incorrectly.

SA-LID imeweka sentensi hii chini ya isiZulu kwa kuwa ina maneno 15 ya isiZulu ingawa pia ina maneno matatu ya Kiingereza, mojawapo likiwa limeandikwa kimakosa.

Furthermore, we observed that the SA-LID shows a preference for indigenous South African languages when an equal number of words from multiple languages are present in the same comment. To illustrate, consider the following comment classified under the isiXhosa comments:

Aidha, tuliona kwamba SA-LID inaonyesha upendeleo kwa lugha za asili za Afrika Kusini wakati idadi sawa ya maneno kutoka lugha nyingi zinapopatikana katika maoni hayo hayo. Ili kufafanua, zingatia maoni yafuatayo yaliyowekwa chini ya maoni ya isiXhosa:

(c) This woman is talking sh\%t... lo othi xa ungenamgidi awuyndoda... mxfm.... The only part I like is lena athi yimisebenzi yakho ebonakalisa ubudoda.

In this example, the term 'sh%t' is not recognised as English due to the inclusion of punctuation. Upon tokenisation, the word is divided into three tokens, making it less likely to be identified as a valid English word. Additionally, the term 'mxfm' is a misspelt word. Consequently, there are only ten English words in the comment. Similarly, the isiXhosa word 'awuyndoda' is spelt incorrectly, as such there are also ten isiXhosa words. Thus, the comment contains an equal number of English and isiXhosa words but even so, the SA-LID identified the comment as isiXhosa, thereby illustrating a preference for isiXhosa.

Katika mfano huu, neno 'sh%t' halitambuliwi kama Kiingereza kwa sababu ya kujumuishwa kwa alama ya uakifishaji. Baada ya utokenishaji, neno hilo linagawanywa katika tokeni tatu, na hivyo kufanya iwe vigumu zaidi kutambuliwa kama neno halali la Kiingereza. Zaidi ya hayo, neno 'mxfm' ni neno lililoandikwa vibaya. Kwa hiyo, kuna maneno kumi tu ya Kiingereza katika maoni hayo. Vivyo hivyo, neno la isiXhosa 'awuyndoda' limeandikwa vibaya, kwa hivyo pia kuna maneno kumi ya isiXhosa. Hivyo, maoni hayo yana idadi sawa ya maneno ya Kiingereza na isiXhosa lakini hata hivyo, SA-LID ilitambua maoni hayo kama isiXhosa, na hivyo kuonyesha upendeleo kwa isiXhosa.

In more extreme circumstances, the SA-LID identified code-switched comments that are predominantly English under indigenous languages such as isiXhosa. To illustrate, consider the example below:

Katika hali kali zaidi, SA-LID ilitambua maoni yaliyobadilishana msimbo ambayo kwa kiasi kikubwa ni ya Kiingereza chini ya lugha za kiasili kama vile isiXhosa. Ili kufafanua, zingatia mfano ufuatao:

(d) Singabantu abanye. Xhosas from Zim moved to Zim from the Eastern Cape with Cecil John Rhodes, Ndicelumenywa.

This code-switched comment exhibits a prevailing use of English, interspersed with three isiXhosa words. The classification of this sentence as isiXhosa reinforces our inference that the SA-LID tends to favour indigenous languages when categorising code-switched texts.

Maoni haya yenye mchanganyiko wa misimbo yanaonyesha matumizi makubwa ya Kiingereza, yakichanganywa na maneno matatu ya isiXhosa. Uainishaji wa sentensi hii kama isiXhosa unaimarisha inferensi yetu kwamba SA-LID ina mwelekeo wa kupendelea lugha za kiasili wakati wa kuainisha maandishi yenye mchanganyiko wa misimbo.

Note that the SA-LID has no category for sentences that are multilingual. This is particularly problematic in the context of South African multilingual social media. That is, there is a need for an additional category in terms of those sentences considered 'multilingual' (rather than assigning them to one of the two language groups present in the sentence). The ability to identify the use of more than one language within a single text effectively ensures an alignment with real-life language use. However, the SA-LID currently identifies at least one language from the comment and then assigns a language label rather than noting the sentence as 'multilingual. Nonetheless, this ability to classify code-switched texts is a significant asset for our study which focuses on how amaXhosa articulate their linguistic identity.

Kumbuka kuwa SA-LID haina kategoria kwa sentensi zinazotumia lugha nyingi. Hili ni tatizo hasa katika muktadha wa mitandao ya kijamii ya Afrika Kusini yenye lugha nyingi. Yaani, kuna haja ya kategoria ya ziada kuhusiana na sentensi zile zinazochukuliwa kuwa 'za lugha nyingi' (badala ya kuziweka katika mojawapo ya vikundi viwili vya lugha vilivyopo kwenye sentensi). Uwezo wa kutambua matumizi ya lugha zaidi ya moja ndani ya maandishi moja kwa ufanisi huhakikisha upatanisho na matumizi ya lugha ya maisha halisi. Hata hivyo, kwa sasa SA-LID inatambua angalau lugha moja kutoka kwenye maoni na kisha inapeana lebo ya lugha badala ya kubainisha sentensi kama 'ya lugha nyingi'. Hata hivyo, uwezo huu wa kuainisha maandishi yaliyochanganya lugha ni rasilimali muhimu kwa utafiti wetu unaozingatia jinsi amaXhosa wanavyoeleza utambulisho wao wa lugha.

Our aim in this paper was to investigate the ability of the SA-LID to identify comments in isiXhosa. Overall, the SA-LID was able to identify instances of the use of isiXhosa including sentences that are purely in isiXhosa and those that are code-switched.

Lengo letu katika makala hii lilikuwa kuchunguza uwezo wa SA-LID wa kutambua maoni katika isiXhosa. Kwa ujumla, SA-LID iliweza kutambua matukio ya matumizi ya isiXhosa ikiwa ni pamoja na sentensi ambazo ni za isiXhosa pekee na zile ambazo zimechanganya lugha.

In the larger study on language identities, we also hope to identify and analyse strategies used by multilingual commentators in their interactions on YouTube as a social media platform. Consequently, the accurate identification of isiXhosa through the SA-LID holds particular significance for our research objectives, facilitating the exclusion of comments lacking isiXhosa content.

Katika utafiti mkubwa zaidi kuhusu utambulisho wa lugha, pia tunatumaini kutambua na kuchambua mikakati inayotumiwa na watoa maoni wenye lugha nyingi katika mwingiliano wao kwenye YouTube kama jukwaa la mitandao ya kijamii. Kwa hiyo, utambuzi sahihi wa isiXhosa kupitia SA-LID una umuhimu wa pekee kwa malengo yetu ya utafiti, ukiwezesha kutengwa kwa maoni yasiyo na maudhui ya isiXhosa.

4.4. Assumed linguistic and Cultural identities

4.4. Vitambulisho vya lugha na kitamaduni vilivyodhaniwa

As we analyzed the comments, we observed a diverse array of languages employed by commentators, including code-switching between indigenous South African languages as well as the unexpected occurrences of Japanese, Russian, and Arabic. We inferred that individuals who use both monolingual and multilingual sentences were concurrently expressing both their thoughts and cultural identities. Drawing on the insights of scholars like Bucholtz and Hall (2004) and others who explore the intricate relationship between language and identity, our findings suggested that commentators were strategically situating their linguistic and cultural identities through their language use.

Tulipochambua maoni, tuliona safu mbalimbali ya lugha zilizotumiwa na watoa maoni, ikiwemo ubadilishaji msimbo kati ya lugha za kiasili za Afrika Kusini pamoja na matukio yasiyotarajiwa ya Kijapani, Kirusi, na Kiarabu. Tulihitimisha kuwa watu wanaotumia sentensi za lugha moja na za lugha nyingi walikuwa wakieleza kwa wakati mmoja mawazo yao na vitambulisho vyao vya kitamaduni. Kwa kutumia maarifa ya wasomi kama Bucholtz na Hall (2004) na wengine wanaochunguza uhusiano tata kati ya lugha na utambulisho, matokeo yetu yalidokeza kuwa watoa maoni walikuwa wakiweka kimkakati vitambulisho vyao vya lugha na kitamaduni kupitia matumizi yao ya lugha.

Nevertheless, we acknowledge the inherently multilingual nature of the world, where individuals can learn languages beyond those spoken at home. According to Kinginger (2004), when individuals speak and learn a new language, they simultaneously adopt a new identity or engage in the reconstruction of their existing one. This concept is illustrated by Johanson Botha (2009)'s example of an English man learning isiXhosa. When he speaks isiXhosa, he becomes loud, which causes embarrassment to his wife. This loudness, not commonly associated with Western culture, is stereotypically linked to isiXhosa culture, portraying the construction of amaXhosa as assertive or loud. Therefore, we understand that to definitively ascertain whether someone identifies as isiXhosa or any other language, further investigation (for example conducting interviews) would be imperative.

Hata hivyo, tunakiri asili ya ulimwengu yenye lugha nyingi, ambapo watu wanaweza kujifunza lugha zaidi ya zile zinazozungumzwa nyumbani. Kulingana na Kinginger (2004), watu wanapozungumza na kujifunza lugha mpya, wakati huo huo wanachukua utambulisho mpya au kujihusisha na ujenzi upya wa ule waliokuwa nao. Dhana hii inaonyeshwa na mfano wa Johanson Botha (2009) wa mwanaume Mwingereza anayejifunza isiXhosa. Anapozungumza isiXhosa, anakuwa na sauti kubwa, jambo linalosababisha aibu kwa mkewe. Sauti hii kubwa, ambayo haihusishwi kwa kawaida na utamaduni wa Kimagharibi, inahusishwa kwa njia ya kimazoea na utamaduni wa isiXhosa, ikionyesha ujenzi wa amaXhosa kama watu wenye kujiamini au wenye sauti kubwa. Kwa hiyo, tunaelewa kwamba ili kubaini kwa uthabiti ikiwa mtu anajitambulisha kama isiXhosa au lugha nyingine yoyote, uchunguzi zaidi (kwa mfano kufanya mahojiano) ungekuwa wa lazima.

Given this context, the primary investigation of our broader study will focus on conducting interviews to confirm linguistic identities. This was not, however, necessary for this paper as the objective was solely to assess the accuracy of the SA-LID when applied to a corpus of YouTube comments.

Kwa kuzingatia muktadha huu, uchunguzi wa msingi wa utafiti wetu mpana utalenga kufanya mahojiano ili kuthibitisha utambulisho wa lugha. Hili, hata hivyo, halikuwa la lazima kwa makala hii kwani lengo lilikuwa tu kutathmini Usahihi wa SA-LID inapotumika kwenye korasi ya maoni ya YouTube.

5. Conclusion

5. Hitimisho

In this paper, we explore the use of automatic language identification using the South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) to discern languages within a YouTube comments corpus. This study forms part of a broader project aiming to uncover linguistic strategies employed by isiXhosa speakers in expressing their language and cultural identities in YouTube comments. As digital platforms continue to shape communication patterns, understanding language identities becomes crucial for fostering inclusive and accurate representation. To facilitate this, there is a need for accurate language identification in multilingual texts. As such, the context of our broader study led us to evaluate the reliability of the SA-LID in identifying any use of isiXhosa language elements in the relevant comments which we mined from YouTube. The question which underpinned our research, as reflected in Section 1, related to whether we could rely on the language identification results generated through the use of the SA-LID to accurately identify all instances of the use of isiXhosa.

Katika makala hii, tunachunguza matumizi ya utambuzi wa lugha kiotomatiki kwa kutumia South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) ili kubaini lugha ndani ya korasi ya maoni ya YouTube. Utafiti huu ni sehemu ya mradi mpana unaolenga kufichua mikakati ya kiisimu inayotumiwa na wazungumzaji wa isiXhosa katika kueleza utambulisho wao wa lugha na utamaduni katika maoni ya YouTube. Kadiri majukwaa ya kidijitali yanavyoendelea kuunda mifumo ya mawasiliano, kuelewa utambulisho wa lugha kunakuwa muhimu kwa ajili ya kukuza uwakilishi jumuishi na sahihi. Ili kuwezesha hili, kuna haja ya utambuzi wa lugha sahihi katika maandishi ya lugha nyingi. Kwa hivyo, muktadha wa utafiti wetu mpana ulituongoza kutathmini uaminifu wa SA-LID katika kutambua matumizi yoyote ya vipengele vya lugha ya isiXhosa katika maoni husika ambayo tuliyachimba kutoka YouTube. Swali ambalo lilikuwa msingi wa utafiti wetu, kama ilivyoonyeshwa katika Sehemu ya 1, lilihusu iwapo tunaweza kutegemea matokeo ya utambuzi wa lugha yaliyotokana na matumizi ya SA-LID ili kutambua kwa usahihi matukio yote ya matumizi ya isiXhosa.

Our analysis of the SA-LID reveals both strengths and challenges. The tool demonstrates proficiency in identifying languages used in multilingual comments, showcasing its versatility in capturing dynamic language use within the amaXhosa community on YouTube. This aspect prompts us to conclude that the SA-LID can indeed be effectively employed in situations where two languages coexist or code-switching occurs, showcasing its robust capabilities in language categorisation.

Uchambuzi wetu wa SA-LID unaonyesha nguvu na changamoto zote mbili. Zana hiyo inaonyesha ustadi katika kutambua lugha zinazotumiwa katika maoni ya lugha nyingi, ikionyesha uwezo wake wa kunasa matumizi ya lugha yenye mabadiliko ndani ya jamii ya amaXhosa kwenye YouTube. Kipengele hiki kinatuhimiza kuhitimisha kwamba SA-LID kwa hakika inaweza kutumika kwa ufanisi katika hali ambapo lugha mbili zinaishi pamoja au ubadilishaji msimbo unatokea, ikionyesha uwezo wake imara katika uainishaji wa lugha.

However, challenges arise, particularly in cases of mutual intelligibility between closely related languages like isiXhosa and other Nguni languages. This highlights the complicated nature of language identification and, therefore, urges further exploration.

Hata hivyo, changamoto zinajitokeza, hasa katika hali za kuelewana kati ya lugha zinazohusiana kwa karibu kama isiXhosa na lugha nyingine za Kinguni. Hii inaangazia asili tata ya utambuzi wa lugha na, kwa hivyo, inahimiza uchunguzi zaidi.

Other challenges encountered with this tool include uncertainties related to emojis, slang, unconventional spelling and spelling errors. This emphasises the need for continuous refinement in language identification tools to accommodate diverse linguistic expressions. In the context of our corpus, the non-Latin scripts in the dataset further complicated language identification as they are unexpected in the South African context.

Changamoto nyingine zilizojitokeza kwa zana hii zinajumuisha kutokuwa na uhakika kuhusiana na emojis, misimu, tahajia isiyo ya kawaida na makosa ya tahajia. Hili linasisitiza hitaji la uboreshaji endelevu katika zana za utambuzi wa lugha ili kukidhi semi mbalimbali za kiisimu. Katika muktadha wa korasi yetu, maandishi yasiyo ya Kilatini katika seti ya data yalichanganya zaidi utambuzi wa lugha kwa kuwa hayatarajiwi katika muktadha wa Afrika Kusini.

Our findings contribute to the ongoing discourse on language use and identity in digital spaces, offering insights into methodologies which can be employed in further research. The misidentification of languages, as noted in this study, opens up opportunities for future studies to explore how the choice of words or phrase structure in a text can potentially confuse a language identifier. In this study, we did not delve into grammatical complexities or sentence structures; our primary focus was to ascertain the ability of the SA-LID to identify the use of isiXhosa from written YouTube comments accurately. We acknowledge that potential issues may have arisen from variations in pre-processing steps. Specifically, different processes may have been employed for tokenisation, text normalisation, and handling special characters compared to those used in the training of the SA-LID.
