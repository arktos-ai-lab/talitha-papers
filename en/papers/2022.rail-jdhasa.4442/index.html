Abstract

Psycholinguistic research on isiXhosa and related Bantu languages is scarce. For research on lexical processing in particular, a prerequisite is data on lexical properties that impact word recognition, such as word frequency and neighbourhood density. This paper describes the derivation of these and related lexical statistics from a newly created 4.8-million-word isiXhosa corpus. It then reviews the potential applications of such a lexical database for research on language acquisition, language development, and language processing. The paper closes with recommendations for further work in this domain.

Keywords

isiXhosa, psycholinguistics, lexical statistics, lexical processing, corpus

Introduction

The vast majority of language acquisition and processing research focuses on a small subset of the world's languages, specifically those from the Germanic and Romance branches of the Indo-European language family (Bylund, Khafif & Berghoff 2022; Norcliffe, Harris & Jaeger 2015). Within the neglected language groups, the Bantu languages are particularly understudied. This narrow focus in terms of language typology severely limits the generalizability of theories of language processing.

isiXhosa is an agglutinating language, meaning that its words typically consist of multiple morphemes that are concatenated in a relatively transparent manner. It has two particular features that distinguish it from more commonly studied agglutinating languages such as Turkish, Basque, and Hungarian (see van de Velde et al. 2019 for discussion). Firstly, alongside suffixation, it makes widespread use of prefixation to produce morphologically complex words. Secondly, it has a rich grammatical gender or noun class system, whereby nouns are divided into 15 groups, with noun class agreement being marked on several syntactic constituents (e.g., verbs, adjectives/relatives, determiners). As language-specific properties of affixation (e.g., Boudelaa & Marslen-Wilson 2011) and grammatical gender (e.g., Colé, Pynte & Andriamamonjy 2003) are known to affect language processing, psycholinguistic examinations of isiXhosa and related languages have much to contribute to theories of lexical and morphological processing. Further, in terms of practical applications, an understanding of how language and literacy development proceeds in languages of this sort is indispensable in designing teaching and intervention materials for young learners (Pretorius 2019).

A sine qua non of robust psycholinguistic research into lexical processing are statistics on certain lexical properties known to influence word recognition, such as word frequency and neighbourhood density. Databases of such statistics are increasingly being developed and made available to facilitate research on more commonly studied languages. Examples include GreekLex, for Greek (Ktori, van Heuven & Pitchford 2008); EsPal, for Spanish (Duchon et al. 2013); Aralex, for Modern Standard Arabic (Boudelaa & Marslen-Wilson 2010); StimulStat, for Russian (Alexeeva, Slioussar & Chernova 2018); the Chinese Lexical Database, for Mandarin (Sun et al. 2018); P-PAL, for Portuguese (Soares et al. 2018); and E-Hitz, for Basque (Perea et al. 2006); as well as CLEARPOND (Cross-Linguistic Easy Access Resource for Phonological and Orthographic Neighbourhood Densities; Marian et al. 2012), which provides lexical data for five widely examined European languages.

Resources on African indigenous languages are scarce. Unsurprisingly, then, the kinds of data needed for robust psycholinguistic research on isiXhosa processing and acquisition are lacking. This paper describes the generation of lexical statistics for isiXhosa. It identifies and defines the types of lexical statistics needed for lexical processing research on isiXhosa and exemplifies their calculation based on a newly created 4.8-million-word isiXhosa corpus. The paper concludes by reviewing potential applications of such a database and outlining steps for future work.

Characteristics to be included in a database of lexical statistics for isiXhosa

This section reviews the characteristics that should, at a minimum, be included in a database of lexical statistics for isiXhosa. This overview focuses on characteristics that are relevant specifically to visual word recognition.

Frequency

Frequency is arguably the most important variable in studies of lexical processing (van Heuven et al. 2014), where more frequent words are processed more rapidly than less frequent words. This effect can be explained on the basis of lexical activation, whereby more frequently encountered words have higher resting activation levels and are thus accessed more quickly than their low-frequency counterparts. A word's frequency is calculated based on the number of its occurrences in a corpus. It can be expressed on the standardized Zipf frequency scale, where the lower half of the scale (1-3) represents low-frequency words and the upper half of the scale (4-6) represents high-frequency words (words with a Zipf frequency above 7 tend to be function words). Zipf frequency is calculated as log10 (frequency per million words) + 3 (van Heuven et al. 2014).

Word length

Word length is calculated simply as the number of letters in a given word. At least in English, it has been found to have non-linear effects on word recognition independently of other variables such as number of syllables (New et al. 2006).

Neighbourhood statistics

A neighbour of a given word is any word that can be created by substituting, adding, or deleting a single letter (for example, the isiXhosa ubisi 'milk' has as neighbours ubusi 'honey ' and usisi 'sister' , among others). The neighbourhood density of a word is equal to the number of its neighbours. Neighbourhood density effects on lexical processing typically manifest as processing slowdowns for words with more neighbours (Andrews 1997), which is attributed to the fact that when recognizing a word with many neighbours, numerous candidate lexical items become activated and must consequently be inhibited for the correct item to be selected. A related variable that is also of importance is neighbourhood frequency, which is the average frequency of a given word's neighbours. Here, a word with high-frequency neighbours takes longer to recognize than a word with low-frequency neighbours (e.g., Brysbaert, Mandera & Keuleers 2018).

Method

The corpus

The calculation of lexical statistics requires a sizeable corpus of contemporary language materials. The corpus used in this paper was created by combining the isiXhosa corpora provided in the Leipzig Corpora Collection (Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012) with a new corpus created by the author from the online isiXhosa newssite Isolezwe lesiXhosa. The Leipzig isiXhosa corpora consist of texts randomly collected from the web and Wikipedia and therefore cover a multitude of topics (see Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012 for discussion). The Isolezwe corpus, on the other hand, contains reports on general news, sports, entertainment, opinion, and agriculture. This corpus was created via web-scraping using the rvest package (version 1.0.2; Wickham 2021) in the R environment for statistical computing (version 4.2.1; R Core Team 2022). The entire history of articles that was available from the site's inception (26 June 2015) up until 24 June 2022 was scraped.

To create the final corpus, each subcorpus was read into R and subjected to basic cleaning (e.g., removal of digits) using the stringr package (version 1.4; Wickham 2019). Tokenization of each subcorpus was then performed using the "unnest_tokens" function from the tidytext package (version 0.3.3; Silge & Robinson 2016).

Calculation of statistics

Frequency numbers were obtained using the tidytext package in R. These raw numbers were then converted to Zipf frequencies. The other lexical statistics were calculated using the LexiCAL program (Chee et al. 2021). This Windows application allows the user to input a corpus file, which specifies the tokens and their frequency in the corpus, for any alphabetic language. It then calculates the selected metrics and outputs the results to a separate file. For neighbourhood statistics, it also provides the neighbours of the words that are included in the corpus.

Applications

A database of lexical statistics such as that described in this paper has numerous applications for research on lexical processing, which requires careful control of word-level properties such as frequency and neighbourhood density. There is, for example, significant interest in whether recognition of morphologically complex words takes place at the whole-word level or whether it entails breaking a word down into its constituent morphemes. To the best of the author's knowledge, only one study has investigated this question in relation to a Bantu language (Setswana; Ciaccio, Kgolo & Clahsen 2020). The extent to which morphosyntactic processing differs across first- and second-language speakers of a language is also theoretically important and critically understudied in relation to Bantu languages (Spinner 2011).

Another set of applications arises in the domain of language development. Lexical databases of the type described in this paper have been developed specifically for use in psycholinguistic studies of children's language processing and for evaluating literacy materials aimed at developing readers (e.g., Corral, Ferrero & Goikoetxea 2009; Masterson et al. 2010; Schroeder et al. 2015; Terzopoulos et al. 2017). For such applications, corpora are typically compiled using a large selection of materials created specifically for children in order to increase the likelihood of children having been exposed to the language it contains.

Limitations and suggestions for further work

There are several additional steps that can be taken to improve on and expand the database presented in this paper. For one, after compiling the corpus and before processing it to derive lexical statistics, it would be desirable to cross-reference the corpus word list with a word list from an official isiXhosa dictionary. This cross-referencing process would enable misspellings to be filtered out from the corpus, thus reducing the number of spurious neighbours identified, and also facilitate the removal of non-isiXhosa words. At the time of writing, no such dictionary word list could be obtained in a digital format, and so this step has not yet been taken.

Another notable consideration is that all of the above calculations were based on word forms rather than roots or lemmas. This means that instead of, for example, -lala being treated as a lemma that surfaces in ukulala, uyalala, siyalala, and so forth, each of these word forms is treated as an individual item. This can also lead to inflation of neighbourhood density (however, the words affected by this issue - most notably, verbs - will tend to have their neighbourhood density inflated to the same extent). It remains an empirical question whether it is properties of the word form or the lemma that are better predictors of, for example, word recognition latency in languages such as isiXhosa. In order to address this question, lemmas could be obtained from corpus data using a morphological analyzer (e.g., du Toit & Puttkammer 2021).

Lastly, the work presented here could also be expanded by deriving phonological statistics for isiXhosa, such as syllable number and phonological neighbourhood density. Such statistics can be obtained via LexiCAL if each word entry is paired with a phonetic transcription and would enable research on spoken word processing in the language.

Conclusion

Psycholinguistic techniques that can capture language processing as it unfolds in real time have yet to be leveraged to examine the processing of isiXhosa and related languages. This paper has discussed one kind of resource - a database of lexical statistics on the language, compiled based on a large-scale corpus - that is necessary to address this research gap and realize the considerable theoretical and practical benefits of doing so. Future collaboration between (computational) linguists and language specialists will allow for the refinement of this resource and the creation of others.

La fréquence d'un mot est calculée en fonction du nombre de ses occurrences dans un corpus. Elle peut être exprimée sur l'échelle de fréquence Zipf standardisée, où la moitié inférieure de l'échelle (1-3) représente les mots de basse fréquence et la moitié supérieure de l'échelle (4-6) représente les mots de haute fréquence (les mots avec une fréquence Zipf supérieure à 7 ont tendance à être des mots de fonction). La fréquence Zipf est calculée en log10 (fréquence par million de mots) + 3 (van Heuven et al. 2014).

Word length

Longueur du mot

Word length is calculated simply as the number of letters in a given word. At least in English, it has been found to have non-linear effects on word recognition independently of other variables such as number of syllables (New et al. 2006).

La longueur du mot est calculée simplement comme le nombre de lettres dans un mot donné. Au moins en anglais, il a été démontré qu'elle a des effets non linéaires sur la reconnaissance des mots, indépendamment d'autres variables telles que le nombre de syllabes (New et al. 2006).

Neighbourhood statistics

Statistiques de voisinage

A neighbour of a given word is any word that can be created by substituting, adding, or deleting a single letter (for example, the isiXhosa ubisi 'milk' has as neighbours ubusi 'honey ' and usisi 'sister' , among others). The neighbourhood density of a word is equal to the number of its neighbours. Neighbourhood density effects on lexical processing typically manifest as processing slowdowns for words with more neighbours (Andrews 1997), which is attributed to the fact that when recognizing a word with many neighbours, numerous candidate lexical items become activated and must consequently be inhibited for the correct item to be selected. A related variable that is also of importance is neighbourhood frequency, which is the average frequency of a given word's neighbours. Here, a word with high-frequency neighbours takes longer to recognize than a word with low-frequency neighbours (e.g., Brysbaert, Mandera & Keuleers 2018).

Un voisin d'un mot donné est tout mot qui peut être créé en substituant, en ajoutant ou en supprimant une seule lettre (par exemple, le isiXhosa ubisi «lait» a comme voisins ubusi «miel» et usisi «soeur», entre autres). La densité de voisinage d'un mot est égale au nombre de ses voisins. Les effets de densité de voisinage sur le traitement lexical se manifestent généralement par des ralentissements de traitement pour les mots avec plus de voisins (Andrews 1997), ce qui est attribué au fait que lors de la reconnaissance d'un mot avec de nombreux voisins, de nombreux éléments lexicaux candidats deviennent activés et doivent par conséquent être inhibés pour que l'élément correct soit sélectionné. Une variable connexe qui est également importante est la fréquence de voisinage, qui est la fréquence moyenne des voisins d'un mot donné. Ici, un mot avec des voisins de haute fréquence prend plus de temps à reconnaître qu'un mot avec des voisins de faible fréquence (par exemple, Brysbaert, Mandera & Keuleers 2018).

The corpus

Le corpus

The calculation of lexical statistics requires a sizeable corpus of contemporary language materials. The corpus used in this paper was created by combining the isiXhosa corpora provided in the Leipzig Corpora Collection (Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012) with a new corpus created by the author from the online isiXhosa newssite Isolezwe lesiXhosa. The Leipzig isiXhosa corpora consist of texts randomly collected from the web and Wikipedia and therefore cover a multitude of topics (see Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012 for discussion). The Isolezwe corpus, on the other hand, contains reports on general news, sports, entertainment, opinion, and agriculture. This corpus was created via web-scraping using the rvest package (version 1.0.2; Wickham 2021) in the R environment for statistical computing (version 4.2.1; R Core Team 2022). The entire history of articles that was available from the site's inception (26 June 2015) up until 24 June 2022 was scraped.

Le calcul des statistiques lexicales nécessite un corpus important de matériaux linguistiques contemporains. Le corpus utilisé dans cet article a été créé en combinant les corpus isiXhosa fournis dans la Leipzig Corpora Collection (Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012) avec un nouveau corpus créé par l'auteur à partir du site d'actualités isiXhosa en ligne Isolezwe lesiXhosa. Les corpus isiXhosa de Leipzig sont constitués de textes collectés aléatoirement sur le web et sur Wikipedia et couvrent donc une multitude de sujets (voir Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012 pour discussion). Le corpus Isolezwe, quant à lui, contient des reportages sur l'actualité générale, le sport, le divertissement, les opinions et l'agriculture. Applications

Applications

A database of lexical statistics such as that described in this paper has numerous applications for research on lexical processing, which requires careful control of word-level properties such as frequency and neighbourhood density. There is, for example, significant interest in whether recognition of morphologically complex words takes place at the whole-word level or whether it entails breaking a word down into its constituent morphemes. To the best of the author's knowledge, only one study has investigated this question in relation to a Bantu language (Setswana; Ciaccio, Kgolo & Clahsen 2020). The extent to which morphosyntactic processing differs across first- and second-language speakers of a language is also theoretically important and critically understudied in relation to Bantu languages (Spinner 2011).

Une base de données de statistiques lexicales telle que celle décrite dans cet article a de nombreuses applications pour la recherche sur le traitement lexical, qui nécessite un contrôle minutieux des propriétés au niveau des mots telles que la fréquence et la densité de voisinage. Il y a, par exemple, un intérêt significatif à savoir si la reconnaissance des mots morphologiquement complexes a lieu au niveau du mot entier ou si elle implique de décomposer un mot en ses morphèmes constitutifs. À la connaissance de l'auteur, une seule étude a étudié cette question en relation avec une langue bantoue (Setswana; Ciaccio, Kgolo & Clahsen 2020). La mesure dans laquelle le traitement morphosyntaxique diffère entre les locuteurs de langue maternelle et les locuteurs de langue seconde d’une langue est également théoriquement importante et sous-étudiée de façon critique en ce qui concerne les langues bantoues (Spinner 2011).

Another set of applications arises in the domain of language development. Lexical databases of the type described in this paper have been developed specifically for use in psycholinguistic studies of children's language processing and for evaluating literacy materials aimed at developing readers (e.g., Corral, Ferrero & Goikoetxea 2009; Masterson et al. 2010; Schroeder et al. 2015; Terzopoulos et al. 2017). For such applications, corpora are typically compiled using a large selection of materials created specifically for children in order to increase the likelihood of children having been exposed to the language it contains.

Un autre ensemble d'applications se situe dans le domaine du développement du langage. Les bases de données lexicales du type décrit dans cet article ont été développées spécifiquement pour être utilisées dans les études psycholinguistiques sur le traitement du langage chez les enfants et pour évaluer les matériels d'alphabétisation visant à développer les lecteurs (p. ex., Corral, Ferrero et Goikoetxea 2009; Masterson et coll. 2010; Schroeder et coll. 2015; Terzopoulos et coll. 2017). Pour de telles applications, les corpus sont généralement compilés en utilisant une grande sélection de matériel créé spécifiquement pour les enfants afin d'augmenter la probabilité que les enfants aient été exposés au langage qu'il contient.

Limitations and suggestions for further work

Limites et suggestions pour la suite des travaux

There are several additional steps that can be taken to improve on and expand the database presented in this paper. For one, after compiling the corpus and before processing it to derive lexical statistics, it would be desirable to cross-reference the corpus word list with a word list from an official isiXhosa dictionary. This cross-referencing process would enable misspellings to be filtered out from the corpus, thus reducing the number of spurious neighbours identified, and also facilitate the removal of non-isiXhosa words. At the time of writing, no such dictionary word list could be obtained in a digital format, and so this step has not yet been taken.

Il existe plusieurs étapes supplémentaires qui peuvent être prises pour améliorer et étendre la base de données présentée dans cet article. D'une part, après avoir compilé le corpus et avant de le traiter pour dériver des statistiques lexicales, il serait souhaitable de croiser la liste de mots du corpus avec une liste de mots provenant d'un dictionnaire officiel isiXhosa. Ce processus de croisement permettrait de filtrer les fautes d'orthographe du corpus, réduisant ainsi le nombre de voisins erronés identifiés, et faciliterait également l'élimination des mots non isiXhosa. Au moment de la rédaction, aucune telle liste de mots de dictionnaire n'a pu être obtenue au format numérique, et donc cette étape n'a pas encore été prise.

Another notable consideration is that all of the above calculations were based on word forms rather than roots or lemmas. This means that instead of, for example, -lala being treated as a lemma that surfaces in ukulala, uyalala, siyalala, and so forth, each of these word forms is treated as an individual item. This can also lead to inflation of neighbourhood density (however, the words affected by this issue - most notably, verbs - will tend to have their neighbourhood density inflated to the same extent). It remains an empirical question whether it is properties of the word form or the lemma that are better predictors of, for example, word recognition latency in languages such as isiXhosa. In order to address this question, lemmas could be obtained from corpus data using a morphological analyzer (e.g., du Toit & Puttkammer 2021).

Une autre considération notable est que tous les calculs ci-dessus étaient basés sur les formes de mots plutôt que sur les racines ou les lemmes. Cela signifie qu'au lieu, par exemple, de traiter -lala comme un lemme qui apparaît dans ukulala, uyalala, siyalala, et ainsi de suite, chacune de ces formes de mots est traitée comme un élément individuel. Cela peut également conduire à une inflation de la densité de voisinage (cependant, les mots affectés par ce problème - plus particulièrement les verbes - auront tendance à avoir leur densité de voisinage gonflée dans la même mesure). Il reste une question empirique de savoir si ce sont les propriétés de la forme du mot ou du lemme qui sont de meilleurs prédicteurs, par exemple, de la latence de reconnaissance des mots dans des langues telles que l'isiXhosa. Pour répondre à cette question, les lemmes pourraient être obtenus à partir de données de corpus à l’aide d’un analyseur morphologique (p. ex., du Toit & Puttkammer 2021).

Lastly, the work presented here could also be expanded by deriving phonological statistics for isiXhosa, such as syllable number and phonological neighbourhood density. Such statistics can be obtained via LexiCAL if each word entry is paired with a phonetic transcription and would enable research on spoken word processing in the language.

Enfin, le travail présenté ici pourrait également être étendu en dérivant des statistiques phonologiques pour isiXhosa, telles que le nombre de syllabes et la densité phonologique de voisinage. De telles statistiques peuvent être obtenues via LexiCAL si chaque entrée de mot est associée à une transcription phonétique et permettraient de mener des recherches sur le traitement des mots parlés dans la langue.

Conclusion

Conclusion

Psycholinguistic techniques that can capture language processing as it unfolds in real time have yet to be leveraged to examine the processing of isiXhosa and related languages. This paper has discussed one kind of resource - a database of lexical statistics on the language, compiled based on a large-scale corpus - that is necessary to address this research gap and realize the considerable theoretical and practical benefits of doing so. Future collaboration between (computational) linguists and language specialists will allow for the refinement of this resource and the creation of others.

Les techniques psycholinguistiques qui peuvent capturer le traitement du langage à mesure qu'il se déroule en temps réel n'ont pas encore été exploitées pour examiner le traitement de l'isiXhosa et des langues connexes. Cet article a discuté d'un type de ressource - une base de données de statistiques lexicales sur la langue, compilée à partir d'un corpus à grande échelle - qui est nécessaire pour combler cette lacune de recherche et réaliser les avantages théoriques et pratiques considérables qui en découlent. La collaboration future entre linguistes (informatiques) et spécialistes des langues permettra d’affiner cette ressource et d’en créer d’autres.

Abstract

Psycholinguistic research on isiXhosa and related Bantu languages is scarce. For research on lexical processing in particular, a prerequisite is data on lexical properties that impact word recognition, such as word frequency and neighbourhood density. Introduction

Utangulizi

The vast majority of language acquisition and processing research focuses on a small subset of the world's languages, specifically those from the Germanic and Romance branches of the Indo-European language family (Bylund, Khafif & Berghoff 2022; Norcliffe, Harris & Jaeger 2015). Within the neglected language groups, the Bantu languages are particularly understudied. This narrow focus in terms of language typology severely limits the generalizability of theories of language processing.

Idadi kubwa mno ya utafiti wa upataji na uchakataji wa lugha inalenga kikundi kidogo cha lugha za dunia, hasa zile kutoka matawi ya Kijerumaniki na Kiromansi ya familia ya lugha ya Kihindi-Kiulaya (Bylund, Khafif & Berghoff 2022; Norcliffe, Harris & Jaeger 2015). Ndani ya makundi ya lugha yaliyopuuzwa, Lugha za Kibantu hazijafanyiwa utafiti hasa. Mtazamo huu finyu katika taaluma ya aina za lugha unazuia kwa kiasi kikubwa uwezo wa kujumlisha wa nadharia za uchakataji wa lugha.

isiXhosa is an agglutinating language, meaning that its words typically consist of multiple morphemes that are concatenated in a relatively transparent manner. It has two particular features that distinguish it from more commonly studied agglutinating languages such as Turkish, Basque, and Hungarian (see van de Velde et al. 2019 for discussion). Firstly, alongside suffixation, it makes widespread use of prefixation to produce morphologically complex words. Secondly, it has a rich grammatical gender or noun class system, whereby nouns are divided into 15 groups, with noun class agreement being marked on several syntactic constituents (e.g., verbs, adjectives/relatives, determiners). As language-specific properties of affixation (e.g., Boudelaa & Marslen-Wilson 2011) and grammatical gender (e.g., Colé, Pynte & Andriamamonjy 2003) are known to affect language processing, psycholinguistic examinations of isiXhosa and related languages have much to contribute to theories of lexical and morphological processing. Further, in terms of practical applications, an understanding of how language and literacy development proceeds in languages of this sort is indispensable in designing teaching and intervention materials for young learners (Pretorius 2019).

isiXhosa ni lugha ambatizi, ikimaanisha kwamba maneno yake kwa kawaida hujumuisha mofimu nyingi ambazo huambatanishwa kwa namna iliyo wazi kiasi. Ina vipengele viwili mahususi vinavyotofautisha na lugha ambatizi zinazofanyiwa utafiti zaidi kama vile Kituruki, Kibaski, na Kihungaria (angalia van de Velde et al. 2019 kwa mjadala). Kwanza, pamoja na uambishaji nyuma, inatumia kwa kiasi kikubwa uambishaji mbele ili kutoa maneno yenye mofimu changamano. Pili, ina mfumo tajiri wa jinsia ya kisarufi au ngeli, ambapo nomino hugawanywa katika vikundi 15, huku upatanisho wa ngeli ukiwekwa alama kwenye viambajengo kadhaa vya kisintaksia (k.m., vitenzi, vivumishi/virejeshi, vibainishi). Kwa vile sifa mahususi za lugha za uambishaji (k.m., Boudelaa & Marslen-Wilson 2011) na jinsia ya kisarufi (k.m., Colé, Pynte & Andriamamonjy 2003) zinajulikana kuathiri uchakataji wa lugha, uchunguzi wa kisaikolojia-lugha wa isiXhosa na lugha zinazohusiana una mchango mkubwa katika nadharia za uchakataji wa kileksika na kimofolojia. Zaidi ya hayo, katika suala la matumizi ya kivitendo, uelewa wa jinsi ukuzaji wa lugha na ujuzi wa kusoma na kuandika unavyoendelea katika lugha za aina hii ni muhimu sana katika kubuni nyenzo za kufundishia na za kuingilia kati kwa wanafunzi wachanga (Pretorius 2019).

A sine qua non of robust psycholinguistic research into lexical processing are statistics on certain lexical properties known to influence word recognition, such as word frequency and neighbourhood density. Databases of such statistics are increasingly being developed and made available to facilitate research on more commonly studied languages. Examples include GreekLex, for Greek (Ktori, van Heuven & Pitchford 2008); EsPal, for Spanish (Duchon et al. 2013); Aralex, for Modern Standard Arabic (Boudelaa & Marslen-Wilson 2010); StimulStat, for Russian (Alexeeva, Slioussar & Chernova 2018); the Chinese Lexical Database, for Mandarin (Sun et al. 2018); P-PAL, for Portuguese (Soares et al. 2018); and E-Hitz, for Basque (Perea et al. 2006); as well as CLEARPOND (Cross-Linguistic Easy Access Resource for Phonological and Orthographic Neighbourhood Densities; Marian et al. 2012), which provides lexical data for five widely examined European languages.

Sharti lisiloepukika la utafiti imara wa kisaikolojia-lugha kuhusu uchakataji wa kileksika ni takwimu za sifa fulani za kileksika zinazojulikana kuathiri utambuzi wa neno, kama vile marudio ya neno na msongamano wa ujirani. Hifadhidata za takwimu kama hizo zinazidi kutengenezwa na kupatikana ili kuwezesha utafiti kuhusu lugha zinazosomwa zaidi. Mifano ni pamoja na GreekLex, kwa Kigiriki (Ktori, van Heuven & Pitchford 2008); EsPal, kwa Kihispania (Duchon et al. 2013); Aralex, kwa Kiarabu Sanifu cha Kisasa (Boudelaa & Marslen-Wilson 2010); StimulStat, kwa Kirusi (Alexeeva, Slioussar & Chernova 2018); Hifadhidata ya Kileksika ya Kichina, kwa Kimandarini (Sun et al. 2018); P-PAL, kwa Kireno (Soares et al. 2018); na E-Hitz, kwa Kibaski (Perea et al. 2006); pamoja na CLEARPOND (Rasilimali ya Ufikiaji Rahisi wa Lugha Mbalimbali kwa Msongamano wa Ujirani wa Kifonolojia na Kiothografia; Marian et al. 2012), ambayo hutoa data ya kileksika kwa lugha tano za Ulaya zilizochunguzwa sana.

Resources on African indigenous languages are scarce. Unsurprisingly, then, the kinds of data needed for robust psycholinguistic research on isiXhosa processing and acquisition are lacking. This paper describes the generation of lexical statistics for isiXhosa. It identifies and defines the types of lexical statistics needed for lexical processing research on isiXhosa and exemplifies their calculation based on a newly created 4.8-million-word isiXhosa corpus. The paper concludes by reviewing potential applications of such a database and outlining steps for future work.

Kwa hivyo, haishangazi kwamba aina za data zinazohitajika kwa utafiti imara wa kisaikolojia-lugha kuhusu uchakataji na ujifunzaji wa isiXhosa hazipo. Makala haya yanaelezea uzalishaji wa takwimu za kileksika kwa isiXhosa. Frequency

Marudio

Frequency is arguably the most important variable in studies of lexical processing (van Heuven et al. 2014), where more frequent words are processed more rapidly than less frequent words. This effect can be explained on the basis of lexical activation, whereby more frequently encountered words have higher resting activation levels and are thus accessed more quickly than their low-frequency counterparts. A word's frequency is calculated based on the number of its occurrences in a corpus. It can be expressed on the standardized Zipf frequency scale, where the lower half of the scale (1-3) represents low-frequency words and the upper half of the scale (4-6) represents high-frequency words (words with a Zipf frequency above 7 tend to be function words). Zipf frequency is calculated as log10 (frequency per million words) + 3 (van Heuven et al. 2014).

Marudio bila shaka ni kigezo muhimu zaidi katika tafiti za uchakataji wa kileksika (van Heuven et al. 2014), ambapo maneno yenye marudio zaidi huchakatwa kwa kasi zaidi kuliko maneno yenye marudio machache. Athari hii inaweza kuelezwa kwa msingi wa uamilishaji wa kileksika, ambapo maneno yanayokutana nayo mara nyingi zaidi yana viwango vya juu vya uamilishaji wa utulivu na hivyo hufikiwa kwa haraka zaidi kuliko maneno ya marudio ya chini yanayolingana nayo. Masafa ya neno yanahesabiwa kulingana na idadi ya matukio yake katika korpusi. Inaweza kuonyeshwa kwenye kipimo sanifu cha mara kwa mara cha Zipf, ambapo nusu ya chini ya kipimo (1-3) inawakilisha maneno ya mara kwa mara ya chini na nusu ya juu ya kipimo (4-6) inawakilisha maneno ya mara kwa mara ya juu (maneno yenye mara kwa mara ya Zipf zaidi ya 7 huwa ni maneno ya kazi). Masafa ya Zipf yanahesabiwa kama log10 (masafa kwa kila milioni ya maneno) + 3 (van Heuven et al. 2014).

Word length

Urefu wa neno

Word length is calculated simply as the number of letters in a given word. At least in English, it has been found to have non-linear effects on word recognition independently of other variables such as number of syllables (New et al. 2006).

Urefu wa neno hukokotolewa kwa urahisi kama idadi ya herufi katika neno husika. Angalau kwa Kiingereza, imegundulika kuwa na athari zisizo za mstari kwenye utambuzi wa neno bila kutegemea vigezo vingine kama vile idadi ya silabi (New et al. 2006).

Neighbourhood statistics

Takwimu za majirani

A neighbour of a given word is any word that can be created by substituting, adding, or deleting a single letter (for example, the isiXhosa ubisi 'milk' has as neighbours ubusi 'honey ' and usisi 'sister' , among others). The neighbourhood density of a word is equal to the number of its neighbours. Neighbourhood density effects on lexical processing typically manifest as processing slowdowns for words with more neighbours (Andrews 1997), which is attributed to the fact that when recognizing a word with many neighbours, numerous candidate lexical items become activated and must consequently be inhibited for the correct item to be selected. A related variable that is also of importance is neighbourhood frequency, which is the average frequency of a given word's neighbours. Here, a word with high-frequency neighbours takes longer to recognize than a word with low-frequency neighbours (e.g., Brysbaert, Mandera & Keuleers 2018).

Jirani wa neno fulani ni neno lolote linaloweza kuundwa kwa kubadilisha, kuongeza, au kufuta herufi moja (kwa mfano, neno la isiXhosa ubisi 'maziwa' lina majirani ubusi 'asali' na usisi 'dada', miongoni mwa mengine). Msongamano wa ujirani wa neno ni sawa na idadi ya majirani zake. Athari za msongamano wa ujirani kwenye uchakataji wa kileksika kwa kawaida hujitokeza kama kupungua kwa kasi ya uchakataji kwa maneno yenye majirani wengi zaidi (Andrews 1997), jambo ambalo linahusishwa na ukweli kwamba wakati wa kutambua neno lenye majirani wengi, vipashio vingi vya kileksika vinavyowezekana huamilishwa na kwa hiyo lazima vizuiliwe ili kipashio sahihi kichaguliwe. Kigezo kinachohusiana ambacho pia ni muhimu ni marudio ya ujirani, ambayo ni wastani wa marudio ya majirani wa neno fulani. Hapa, neno lenye majirani wa masafa ya juu huchukua muda mrefu zaidi kutambuliwa kuliko neno lenye majirani wa masafa ya chini (k.m., Brysbaert, Mandera & Keuleers 2018).

Method

Mbinu

The corpus

Korasi

The calculation of lexical statistics requires a sizeable corpus of contemporary language materials. The corpus used in this paper was created by combining the isiXhosa corpora provided in the Leipzig Corpora Collection (Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012) with a new corpus created by the author from the online isiXhosa newssite Isolezwe lesiXhosa. The Leipzig isiXhosa corpora consist of texts randomly collected from the web and Wikipedia and therefore cover a multitude of topics (see Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012 for discussion). The Isolezwe corpus, on the other hand, contains reports on general news, sports, entertainment, opinion, and agriculture. This corpus was created via web-scraping using the rvest package (version 1.0.2; Wickham 2021) in the R environment for statistical computing (version 4.2.1; R Core Team 2022). The entire history of articles that was available from the site's inception (26 June 2015) up until 24 June 2022 was scraped.

Hesabu ya takwimu za kileksika inahitaji korasi kubwa ya nyenzo za lugha za kisasa. Korasi iliyotumika katika makala hii iliundwa kwa kuunganisha korasi za isiXhosa zilizotolewa katika Leipzig Corpora Collection (Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012) na korasi mpya iliyoundwa na mwandishi kutoka kwenye tovuti ya habari ya mtandaoni ya isiXhosa Isolezwe lesiXhosa. Korasi za Leipzig za isiXhosa zinajumuisha maandishi yaliyokusanywa kwa nasibu kutoka kwenye wavuti na Wikipedia na kwa hiyo zinashughulikia mada nyingi (tazama Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff 2012 kwa majadiliano). Korasi ya Isolezwe, kwa upande mwingine, ina ripoti kuhusu habari za jumla, michezo, burudani, maoni, na kilimo. Korasi hii iliundwa kupitia ukusanyaji wa data kutoka wavuti kwa kiotomatiki kwa kutumia kifurushi cha rvest (toleo 1.0.2; Wickham 2021) katika mazingira ya R kwa kompyuta ya takwimu (toleo 4.2.1; R Core Team 2022). Historia nzima ya makala iliyopatikana tangu kuanzishwa kwa tovuti (26 Juni 2015) hadi 24 Juni 2022 ilikusanywa kiotomatiki kutoka kwenye tovuti.

To create the final corpus, each subcorpus was read into R and subjected to basic cleaning (e.g., removal of digits) using the stringr package (version 1.4; Wickham 2019). Tokenization of each subcorpus was then performed using the "unnest_tokens" function from the tidytext package (version 0.3.3; Silge & Robinson 2016).

Calculation of statistics

Hesabu ya takwimu

Frequency numbers were obtained using the tidytext package in R. These raw numbers were then converted to Zipf frequencies. The other lexical statistics were calculated using the LexiCAL program (Chee et al. 2021). This Windows application allows the user to input a corpus file, which specifies the tokens and their frequency in the corpus, for any alphabetic language. It then calculates the selected metrics and outputs the results to a separate file. For neighbourhood statistics, it also provides the neighbours of the words that are included in the corpus.

Nambari za marudio zilipatikana kwa kutumia kifurushi cha tidytext katika R. Nambari hizi ghafi zilibadilishwa kuwa marudio ya Zipf. Takwimu nyingine za kileksika zilikokotolewa kwa kutumia programu ya LexiCAL (Chee et al. 2021). Programu hii ya Windows inamruhusu mtumiaji kuingiza faili ya korasi, ambayo inabainisha tokeni na marudio yake katika korasi, kwa lugha yoyote ya kialfabeti. Kisha inahesabu metriki zilizoteuliwa na kutokeza matokeo kwenye faili tofauti. Kwa takwimu za ujirani, pia hutoa majirani ya maneno ambayo yamejumuishwa katika korasi.

Applications

Matumizi

A database of lexical statistics such as that described in this paper has numerous applications for research on lexical processing, which requires careful control of word-level properties such as frequency and neighbourhood density. There is, for example, significant interest in whether recognition of morphologically complex words takes place at the whole-word level or whether it entails breaking a word down into its constituent morphemes. To the best of the author's knowledge, only one study has investigated this question in relation to a Bantu language (Setswana; Ciaccio, Kgolo & Clahsen 2020). The extent to which morphosyntactic processing differs across first- and second-language speakers of a language is also theoretically important and critically understudied in relation to Bantu languages (Spinner 2011).

Hifadhidata ya takwimu za kileksika kama ile iliyoelezwa katika karatasi hii ina matumizi mengi kwa utafiti wa uchakataji wa kileksika, ambao unahitaji udhibiti makini wa sifa za kiwango cha neno kama vile marudio na msongamano wa ujirani. Kuna, kwa mfano, hamu kubwa katika kujua kama utambuzi wa maneno yenye mofimu changamano unafanyika katika kiwango cha neno zima au kama unahusisha kugawanya neno katika mofimu zake za msingi. Kwa ufahamu wa mwandishi, ni utafiti mmoja tu ambao umechunguza swali hili kuhusiana na lugha ya Kibantu (Setswana; Ciaccio, Kgolo & Clahsen 2020). Kiwango ambacho uchakataji wa kimofosintaksia hutofautiana kati ya wazungumzaji wa lugha ya kwanza na wa lugha ya pili wa lugha fulani pia ni muhimu kinadharia na hakijafanyiwa utafiti wa kutosha kuhusiana na Lugha za Kibantu (Spinner 2011).

Another set of applications arises in the domain of language development. Lexical databases of the type described in this paper have been developed specifically for use in psycholinguistic studies of children's language processing and for evaluating literacy materials aimed at developing readers (e.g., Corral, Ferrero & Goikoetxea 2009; Masterson et al. 2010; Schroeder et al. 2015; Terzopoulos et al. 2017). For such applications, corpora are typically compiled using a large selection of materials created specifically for children in order to increase the likelihood of children having been exposed to the language it contains.

Seti nyingine ya matumizi hujitokeza katika kikoa cha ukuzaji wa lugha. Hifadhidata za kileksika za aina iliyoelezwa katika karatasi hii zimetengenezwa mahususi kwa ajili ya matumizi katika tafiti za kisaikolojia-lugha za uchakataji wa lugha ya watoto na kwa ajili ya kutathmini nyenzo za kusoma na kuandika zinazolenga kukuza wasomaji (k.m., Corral, Ferrero & Goikoetxea 2009; Masterson et al. 2010; Schroeder et al. 2015; Terzopoulos et al. 2017). Kwa matumizi kama hayo, korasi kwa kawaida hukusanywa kwa kutumia uteuzi mkubwa wa nyenzo zilizoundwa mahususi kwa ajili ya watoto ili kuongeza uwezekano kwamba watoto wamewahi kukutana na lugha iliyomo ndani yake.

Limitations and suggestions for further work

Vikwazo na mapendekezo kwa kazi zaidi

There are several additional steps that can be taken to improve on and expand the database presented in this paper. For one, after compiling the corpus and before processing it to derive lexical statistics, it would be desirable to cross-reference the corpus word list with a word list from an official isiXhosa dictionary. This cross-referencing process would enable misspellings to be filtered out from the corpus, thus reducing the number of spurious neighbours identified, and also facilitate the removal of non-isiXhosa words. At the time of writing, no such dictionary word list could be obtained in a digital format, and so this step has not yet been taken.

Kuna hatua kadhaa za ziada zinazoweza kuchukuliwa ili kuboresha na kupanua hifadhidata iliyowasilishwa katika karatasi hii. Kwanza, baada ya kukusanya korasi na kabla ya kuichakata ili kupata takwimu za kileksika, ingependeza kuoanisha orodha ya maneno ya korasi na orodha ya maneno kutoka kamusi rasmi ya isiXhosa. Mchakato huu wa kuoanisha mtambuka ungewezesha makosa ya tahajia kuchujwa kutoka kwenye korasi, hivyo kupunguza idadi ya majirani bandia wanaotambuliwa, na pia kurahisisha uondoaji wa maneno yasiyo ya Kixhosa. Wakati wa kuandika, hakuna orodha ya maneno ya kamusi kama hiyo iliyoweza kupatikana katika muundo wa kidijitali, na kwa hiyo hatua hii bado haijachukuliwa.

Another notable consideration is that all of the above calculations were based on word forms rather than roots or lemmas. This means that instead of, for example, -lala being treated as a lemma that surfaces in ukulala, uyalala, siyalala, and so forth, each of these word forms is treated as an individual item. This can also lead to inflation of neighbourhood density (however, the words affected by this issue - most notably, verbs - will tend to have their neighbourhood density inflated to the same extent). It remains an empirical question whether it is properties of the word form or the lemma that are better predictors of, for example, word recognition latency in languages such as isiXhosa. In order to address this question, lemmas could be obtained from corpus data using a morphological analyzer (e.g., du Toit & Puttkammer 2021).

Jambo lingine muhimu la kuzingatia ni kwamba hesabu zote zilizo hapo juu ziliegemezwa kwenye maumbo ya maneno badala ya mizizi au lemma. Hii ina maana kwamba badala ya, kwa mfano, -lala kuchukuliwa kama lemma inayojitokeza katika ukulala, uyalala, siyalala, na kadhalika, kila moja ya maumbo haya ya maneno huchukuliwa kama kipengele cha mtu binafsi. Hii inaweza pia kusababisha mfumuko wa msongamano wa ujirani (hata hivyo, maneno yaliyoathiriwa na suala hili - hasa, vitenzi - yataelekea kuwa na msongamano wao wa ujirani uliofumuka kwa kiwango sawa). Inabaki kuwa swali la kiuchunguzi ikiwa ni sifa za umbo la neno au za lemma ambazo ni vitabiri bora vya, kwa mfano, muda wa utambuzi wa neno katika lugha kama vile isiXhosa. Ili kushughulikia swali hili, lemma zinaweza kupatikana kutoka kwenye data ya korasi kwa kutumia kichanganuzi cha kimofolojia (k.m., du Toit & Puttkammer 2021).

Lastly, the work presented here could also be expanded by deriving phonological statistics for isiXhosa, such as syllable number and phonological neighbourhood density. Such statistics can be obtained via LexiCAL if each word entry is paired with a phonetic transcription and would enable research on spoken word processing in the language.

Mwisho, kazi iliyowasilishwa hapa inaweza pia kupanuliwa kwa kupata takwimu za kifonolojia za Kixhosa, kama vile idadi ya silabi na msongamano wa ujirani wa kifonolojia. Takwimu kama hizo zinaweza kupatikana kupitia LexiCAL ikiwa kila ingizo la neno limeoanishwa na unukuzi wa kifonetiki na zingewezesha utafiti kuhusu uchakataji wa maneno ya kusemwa katika lugha hiyo.

Conclusion

Hitimisho

Psycholinguistic techniques that can capture language processing as it unfolds in real time have yet to be leveraged to examine the processing of isiXhosa and related languages. This paper has discussed one kind of resource - a database of lexical statistics on the language, compiled based on a large-scale corpus - that is necessary to address this research gap and realize the considerable theoretical and practical benefits of doing so. Future collaboration between (computational) linguists and language specialists will allow for the refinement of this resource and the creation of others.

Mbinu za saikolojia-lugha ambazo zinaweza kunasa uchakataji wa lugha unapojitokeza kwa wakati halisi bado hazijatumiwa kuchunguza uchakataji wa isiXhosa na lugha zinazohusiana. Karatasi hii imejadili aina moja ya rasilimali - hifadhidata ya takwimu za kileksika kuhusu lugha, iliyokusanywa kwa msingi wa korasi ya kiwango kikubwa - ambayo ni muhimu kushughulikia pengo hili la utafiti na kutambua manufaa makubwa ya kinadharia na ya kivitendo ya kufanya hivyo. Ushirikiano wa baadaye kati ya wanaisimu (wa kikompyuta) na wataalamu wa lugha utaruhusu uboreshaji wa rasilimali hii na uundaji wa nyingine.
