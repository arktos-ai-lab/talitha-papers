Abstract

The aim of this article is to provide some insight into the link between diacritic orthography and the implicit sounds in these orthographies that are applied in writing the Igala Language corpus. 30 vowels were identified (5 short vowels, and 25 mid to long vowels of different variety) plus 8 diphthongs. Examples in the form of sentences and interpretation were provided. The article combines up to seven diacritic forms in order to better tackle the oft encountered problem of pronouncing words in texts written in foreign language by non-native speakers and learners with supporting indicators provided to guide end users on how to pronounce the words using the diacritic forms and vocal representation of these forms that are herein provided in the double slash oral transcription of the words.

Keywords

vowels, Igala, Language, Resources

1. Introduction

The Igala language is mainly spoken in the East Senatorial District of Kogi State, Nigeria (Oguagha, 1980) within a geographical coordinate lying between 6 3° and 8° north of latitude and 6 3° and 7 4° east of longitude (Saleh and Yunusa, 2013). The people occupy an area that is roughly around 4,982 miles of the Niger-Benue rivers bypass numbering over 2 million speakers of the language (Saleh and Yunusa, 2013). Wikipedia.com pointed out that; “the Igala is Yoruboid branch of the Volta–Niger language family, spoken by the Igala, Agatu, Idoma, and Bassa people”. In the same vein Obayemi, (1980) recognized Igala as being, “among the more conspicuous entities in the Niger-Benue confluence area.

In this study, the key priority is to point out some important vowels and lexicons applicable to day-to-day communication in Igala land. It should be emphasized that the words indicated in this work are by no means ‘self-exhaustive’, for this is but just a drop out of the ocean of words in existence within the Igala lexicographical system. The Latin letters with their associated phonetic symbols and sound were used as reference points.

2 Vowels and Words in Igala Language

The Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary of English defined lexicon as; “all the words and phrases used in a particular language or subject; all the words and phrases used and known by a particular person or group of people” (Hornby, 2015). With respect to the Igala as with other languages, lexical formation begins with conjoining strings of phones or alphabets to form morphemes from which words are created. Currently, no known homemade or standard alphabetical or orthographical system exists for writing the Igala language except for the esoteric signs and markings; usually on the floor, used by traditional witchdoctors, which is not known to the vast majority of the people and that also did not serve formal purposes within society. In the midst of this foibles induced by this lack of inbred phonemes, writers beginning from the modern era, are known to either select the Latin phonetic symbols or the Arabic phonemes to replicate or transcribe words of Igala origin, what is at times referred to in West Africa as a’jami or ajami (Mc Laughlin, 2017).

Notwithstanding the fact that Igala is a tone-based language (Rodriguez, 2014; Dawson et al. 2015; Adeniyi, 2016), while attempting to study the structure of the Igala language, most of the attention of scholars has been on parts of speech, without much effort been dedicated towards tones and sounds with the result that without a prior knowledge of the language words written are hard to pronounce, in some cases not even with the English translation of these provided, and as for those categories of writers who tried to express tones using diacritic orthographies, without a prior knowledge of diacritic by the readers, it is still hard for the readers to decipher what was written, besides, through occasional failures of writers to provide double slash transcription boxes or oral parenthesis of sounds represented by orthographic symbols in their works, the fact that these symbols produce certain sounds from place to place and from one writer to the other makes it very hard for readers to grasp with the sound frame being inferred by these writers.

Abraham (2017; 2019) as well as, Emah and Ojonugwa (2020) while using diacritic orthography to write words, partly to show how differences in pitch change word meanings as was once observed by Yip (2002), failed to provide double slash transcription of the sounds represented by the orthographic symbols incorporated. Kigalaonline.wordpress.com2 made an attempt that came close to remedying the above stated shortcomings but ended up identifying just three form each for the five main vowels and two supplementary vowels (ó and é to sound /ó:/ and /é:/ [Sic] - or as in the more commonly recognized phonological form /eu/ and /ei/), which gave only seventeen vowels at the expense of the 30 vowels + 8 diphthongs recognized in this very paper. This shortcoming is not limited to the Igala language alone. Other likewise languages that are still plodding around the threshold of traditional education by the start of the 20th Century, mostly in respect of the low-resourced language groups in Africa are also faced with this very problem, a development Ken Lodge, observed thus:

Traditional education largely ignores spoken languages… little attention is paid to the details of speech in an objective way. We therefore need a method of describing speech in objective, verifiable terms as opposed to the lay approaches which typically describes sound as ‘‘hard’’, ‘‘soft’’, ‘‘sharp’’, and so on which can only be understood by the person using such description (Lodge, 2009).

In the context of this particular paper thus, Latin alphabets were adopted for fashioning Igala sounds, words, and phrasal forms using diacritic symbols bearing phonemic modes similar to Blench (2011). All the English phonemes were adopted except the letters, Q, V, S, X, and Z which were discarded consonants for lack of relevance as it is with Yoruba (Kasali et al. 2021) while the letter ‘c’ is applied only in combination with the letter ‘h’ to produce the digraph /t∫/ that is used for writing words such as ūchōnā (somebody’s name meaning ‘creator’ or just ‘the creator’ in a more general sense of the word), as can be had with English words as chicken and kitchen. More also, the letter ‘c’ is not used in writing /k/ sounding words that are often written with the letter ‘c’ such as can /kæn/, neither can it be devoted to writing a sound /s/ bearing word such as ceiling for this would have been sílīni /si:lini/ or actually chílīni /t∫i:lini/ because of the still the more total absence of the ‘s’ consonant within the Igala lexical system. Similarly, rather than write the word quantity with the letter ‘q’, in our own Igala orthography, the spelling of quantity would appear as ‘kwontity’.

Writing vowels during word formation often involves the embracement of any one out of some three alternative styles. The first is the exclusive use of plain alphabets in which case only vowels that are unadorned with glyphs would be put to use. This first form does not embody the use of diacritic in any way and if vowel are to be crafted, that would depend upon the efficacious amalgam of plain alphabets to produce words that are not delineated in the existing letter ordering as in the spelling of the word Igalaa in wiktionary.org in which case the underlined double ‘a’ in that word Igalaa does not merely suggest a long vowel (i.e.; /a:/) but a repetition of the vowels as /a/ + /a/ to give a sound much like a gliding ‘are are’. The second form of these on-hand modes which this author here assume to be actually impractical depend exclusively on the use of diacritic, if not in writing the plain orthographic characters but in the scrawling of the oral parenthesis of words as in the Webster’s II New College Dictionary. The third style involves the commixing of the other two diametrical forms, i.e.; the adoption of both the plain vowels and the diacritic–based vowels. Any of these three forms is cogent enough provided they goose-step along the conventional morphophonemic norms. It is the third form of these approaches-that of mixing plain Latin alphabet with Latin diacritic symbols, that would be utilized in this particular work.

In the oral parenthesis provided in the Webster’s II New College Dictionary and thesaraus.yourdictionary.com, short vowels were represented with the application of the breve- a form of diacritic that is presented like the lower half of a cycle above the vowels, i.e.; ă, ĕ, ĭ, ŏ, ŭ, but in writing this work, rather than to vent these letters using the breve, they were written just as plain as possible, i.e.; ‘a, e, i, o, u’. Be that said, the other point of departure concern the sounds the other diacritic orthographies used by these esteem dictionaries produced with respect to our own keys here. In the Webster’s II New College Dictionary (1999), the macron was adopted to produce the unique form of the long vowels ō ∼ /eu/ as in the word old /ōld/ in much of a respect used by Dawson et al. (2015) and ā ∼ as in the word said /sād/, but in this work, moved by the peculiarity of the Igala language which partially sets it apart from English and the prima facie claim by Wells (2001) that the macron is ratified for initiating a middle locus between the short vowels and the long vowels.

Macrons were therefore used in this work, not for the production of the ā = /ei/ and ō = /eu/ sounds for these are in this work illustrated by the use of the letters ‘o’ and ‘e’ with dots on top of them (i.e.; ȯ = /eu/ and ė = /ei/). Although, there are a few improvements lately, most of the previous writings, following mainly from the recommendation of the 1984 Igala Orthographic Committee which recognized the under dotted ọ and ẹ for the low tones o ∼ /o˩/ | e ∼ /ee˩˩/ (i.e.; as in the spelling of ọjọ /oo˩˩joo˩˩/ in Okwoli (1996) and Ahiaba (2015), and ẹrẹ /ee˩˩ree˩˩/ in Ayegba et al. (2017) for sounding the falling /o/ and /e/ sounds, while the quartet reserved the plain forms of ‘o’ and ‘e’ alphabets for the rising and falling forms of /eu/ and /ei/ sounds as used for Yoruba in aroadedictionary.com, most of these scholars recognized only 7 vowels plus a so called 24 consonant sounds (Ojoaogwu, 2017) or 32 symbols in whole according to Miachi and Armstrong (1986), which meant they left out a number of vowels and therefore downplaying the use of the macron in the work of succeeding writers with the only exceptions being Omachonu (2011) and to an extent, Dawson et al. (2015) who either used it in much of a respect as done in this paper or as was done in the Webster’s II New College Dictionary.

Thus, the use of the macron in this article follows a model that is similar to its use by Karshima (2012) in respect to the Tiv language or in respect to the Igala word ìkpā (bag) in Rodriguez (2014); not to be confused with íkpa (knock knees), in which ā = /æ/ as in the American English pronunciation of the words ‘plan’ and ‘stand’ in oxfordlearnersdictionary.com as an intermediate tone between the sound /a/ and /a:/ of the IPA sound system, ē = /ɛ/ as in the English word ‘any’, i.e.; an intermediate tone between the sound /e/ and /e:/ of the IPA sound system) as was used in respect to the word ònēkèlē in Rodriguez (2015) which the author of this very paper would here again rewrite as ōnēkēlē (male), ī = /i:/ > /x/ > /i/, i.e.; an intermediate tone between the sound /i/ and /i:/ of the IPA sound system, ō = /ɔ/ as in the cardinal mid-back rounded ‘o’ used in sounding the English word ‘but’ or the Igala word ōkō /ɔkɔ/ in respect to airplane or ship which is in contrast to òkò /o˩koo˩˩/ (millipede) or okā /okæ/ (style, i.e.; acrobatic) or ókō /o:kɔ/ (money), with ‘ō’ being an intermediate tone between the sound /o/ and /o:/ of the IPA sound system, ū = /u:/ > /x/ > /u/ as in Lithuanian, Livonian, and Maori, i.e.; an intermediate tone between the sound /u/ and /u:/ of the IPA sound system. The x in the oral parentheses represents the indefinite phonemic sound for the diacritic orthographies ī & ū ∼ x. Decision to jettison the breve in this paper has a twofold underling. First was to ensure the design of a coherent orthographic system with pure vowels used in their original orthographic forms as it is in most English language texts, notwithstanding whatever graphical modification or characterization that the addition of impending glyphs to the first letter thenceforth might confer. The second reason propped from the need to eschew the immanent supererogatory burden of having to write them with an added character when they could remain plain or without partial alteration and still maintain their functions as short vowels.

The desuetude towards the breve as done here was not a deliberate attempt to undo the merits of the Webster’s II New College Dictionary, their discretion are still very valid, specifically with respect to the study of the English language. This writer is well abreast with the unequivocal fact that left with a vagaries of indicative keys to choose from, it is always incumbent upon each writer to cherry-pick both the phonotactical and the morphotactical forms that are amenable to the language being worked on (Pretorius and Bosch, 2009), it should however be noted that the use of ā = /ei/ and ō = /eu/, despite not being wrong in any sense, doesn’t nonetheless conforms with the original phonological flow pattern enshrined in their root alphabet ‘a’ which produces the sounds /a/ and /a:/, and ‘e’ which produces the sounds /e/ and /e:/. Bearing in mind the fact that the middle tone is sometimes perceived to be a conflation of two vowels (i.e.; /e/ + /i/ = /ei/ and /e/ + /u/ = /eu/ as it is in English), then it would not be totally wrong to say that the editors of the Webster’s II New College Dictionary were right in their own right to have used the macron in the context they employed it, besides, language analyst do agree that, ‘there is no such thing as a standard form when it comes to matters of language’ (Laperre 2020) and as Szczegielniak laconically hinted, “spelling or orthography does not consistently represent the sounds of language”. Thus, in Igala language, middle tone implies is a position between a long vowel and a short vowel such as the middle sound between the high /a:/ and the low /a/, i.e.; an ‘a’ that has a higher tone than /a/ but yet is still less pitched than /a:/ represented as x > /a/ < /a:/.

This writer would have loved to have the alphabet ė which is hereby sounded /ei/ written as ȧ, but because most words in the language are already written with the letter ‘e’ to sound /ei/, as in words such as ugbede /ugbeidei/ or ele /eilei/, the author therefore chose to maintain the letter ė which comes closer to the now more popular tradition. The decision to use the alphabet ȧ instead of ė would have matched perfectly with the use of the over dotted ‘o’ (ȯ) to produce the sound /eu/. The use of the macron itself becomes vital considering the fact that, most words in the Igala language are sounded within this mid tone context.

As in writing the Yoruba language or French (Olufemi, 2022; Nolte et al. 2018), the acute and the grave forms of diacritic were also used although with a contrastive difference in phonation between the Igala words written here using this form that is different from French as regards to the word café, in which case, the é as in the French and in the form used by Rodriguez (2014) in relation to the word ‘éʤú’ ∼ /eiʤu:/ (face), is pronounced /ei/, more like the English word rake /reik/, whereas, the orthography é as it relates with the Igala and sometimes with the Yoruba; as with the word òré (friend) in (Oshodi, 2016) ∼ /oo˩˩re:/, is nonetheless pronounced /e:/ as in English. Thus, we must agree with Cahill, (2019) that, in using diacritic tools, “the challenge is that the tone marks are usually not consistent among scholars”, with the result that, a letter é that is pronounced as /e:/ in one instance or by one scholar is pronounced at another instance or by another scholar as /ei/ as in spelling of wálé ∼ /wa:lei/ (come home) in Dalamu (2019) which is at variance with Oshodi (2016)’s /e:/ above.

Here too, the grave accent (i.e.; à, è, ì, ò, ù) was used to indicate the low tones as was done in Yip (2002). The use of certain diacritic marks in this work might be counted as inappropriate as is now apparent with respect to how the macron is used here which is already in conflict with how it was used in the Webster’s II Dictionary or how any of these marks are represented in other languages such as French, Dutch, Portuguese, Italian, and Mohawk (Finegan, 2012).

A critic might ask to know why ‘this author did not use the breve diacritic in writing the short vowels’, which has already been reflected upon above. To cover any anomaly that emanates from how certain things are represented in this work, enabling tools with instructions regarding how to pronounce the diacritic forms is provided, with the hope that the writer of this piece and the readers might be on a common wavelength in comprehending what has been put down. Hence, words having these marks which as this writer suppose might not follow the existing methods in some languages, can be pronounced; if not so perfectly, it would be ‘nearly perfect’.

The Igala vowels are still yet not limited to the above mentioned accent forms. In this study, it is revealed that some vowels are a combination of double sound of the same vowel either ways raised between short or long vowel and then pitched lowly or highly with the corresponding vowel (like a bend), as was done with respect to that closing tone in the word Igalaa. For this category of vowels, the author came up with the idea of having orthographies with macrons on top of them that are further capped with a grave or the acute diacritic but only two of the short vowels ḕ, ḗ and ṑ, ṓ could meet this requirement, and so the author thought of remedying this problem with the use of the circumflex having either the acute or the grave diacritic mark on top of them, i.e.; ấ, ầ, but only the letter ‘a’ has this feature out of the other three still missing letters conveying the short vowels (a, i. and u). The next that could be done was to check for diacritic graphemes that have the features that can best solve the ‘u’ and the ‘i’ problem (regarding this category of sound), but this author once again stumbled upon the idea of using the ‘u’ dieresis capped with either the grave or the acute mark, i.e.; ǘ and ǜ, but the ‘i’ symbols was still missing, and so, the author thought of using the Greek small letter iota with psili and varia (ἲ) and ἵ small letter iota with psili and oxia which the author found as the only current fitting option for use here.

2.1 Contextual Usage of Vowels

In writing the orthographic forms of Igala vowels, one would thus have 30 of these, with each of the short vowels producing 5 variants each while from the glyph ȯ and ė can be gotten an additional 8 diphthongs. In the Webster’s II New College Dictionary, orthographic characters and phonetic sounds adhering or being inherent in the long vowels of the English language were written in the forms; /â/ or /ä/ = /a: /, /ê/ = /e: /, /ē/ = /i: /, /ô/ = /o: /, /û/ = /u: /, /ā/ = /ei/, and ō = /eu/. In this work the above 5 high tones were replicated as á = /a: /, é = /e: /, í = /i: /, ó = /o: /, ú = /u: /. Added to these long vowels are the 5 short vowels a = /a/, e = /e/, i = /i/, o = /o/, u = /u/. There are also 5 macron diacritic to produce the intermediate of these vowels, ā, ē, ī, ō, ū, whose vocal forms have been explained above.

There are 5 long vowels that are stressed with falling tones, i.e.; as in à = /aa˩˩/, è = /ee˩˩/, ì = /ii˩˩/, ò = /oo˩˩/, ù = /uu˩˩/. With the exception of the orthographic symbol ‘o’ which is capped with the double grave and double acute accent (i.e.; ȍ /oo˩˩˩˩/ and ő /o::/) used to express the extra-low and the extra-high accent of the /o/ sound, the other four orthographies bearing the primary vowels (a, e, i, and u) whose logographic form (as lone orthographies) expresses the pronouns ‘a’ (me), ‘e’ (you), ‘i’ (s/he or it), and ‘u’ (first person me) which do convey emphasis and mood, have dieresis-like rise-rise inflective sounds with one, the combine ‘a’ (ấ = /a:a:/) having a circumflex on top of it with an acute glyph capping the circumflex. Others are, the combined ‘e’ (ḗ = /e:e:/) which has a macron glyphs on top of it with an acute accents capping the macron, one other has a dieresis glyph with a grave accent on top of the glyph (ǘ = /u:u:/), and the fourth is the Greek iota with psili and varia with the combined ‘i’ (ἵ = /i:i:/). Each of these 4 vowel begin with short forms of vowels that are succeeded by corresponding long vowels of the same sound bracket within given syllables, more like ending the sound flow in the middle but yet again stressing it forward (an intermittent break that is followed by a change in flow).

Following a reverse, there are also 4 fall-fall inflective sounds, ‘a’ (ầ = /aa˩˩aa˩˩/) took on a circumflex on top of it with a grave glyph on top of the circumflex, ‘e’ (ḕ = /ee˩˩ee˩˩/) has a macron glyph on top of it with a grave accents on top of the macron, ‘u’ (ǜ = /uu˩˩uu˩˩/) has a dieresis glyph with a grave accent on top of the glyph, and the fourth is the Greek iota with psili and oxia ‘i’ (ἲ = /ii˩˩ii˩˩/), to make 4 of these, all having the sound of the pure vowels combined with the corresponding low tones within given syllables, more like ending the flow of the sound in the middle but yet again stressing it lower. Thus, this writer shall try to justify the assumption of the other 30 vowels as adduced.

For letter ‘a’;

á lȱn (we didn’t go), a lȯ (we went), ā lȯn (let us not go), à l + (ȯ capped with an acute diacritic accent (?) (should we go?), ấ lȱn? (won’t we go?), ầ lȱ? (are we going?).

For letter ‘e’;

é wán (you didn’t come), e wā (you came), ē wān (don’t come), è wā? (will you come?), ḗ wān? (won’t you come?), and ḕ d’ōmō? (will you be there?).

For letter ‘i’;

í wán (s/he didn’t come), i wā (s/he came), ī wān (s/he should not come), ì wā? (should, she/he come?), ἵ wān? (shouldn’t s/he come?), and ἲ d’ōmō? (is s/he there?).

For letter ‘o’;

óna (as for a man), onā (dream), ōna (road), òkò (millipede), őgbá (front), and ȍgbā (as demarcation of land).

For letter ‘u’;

ú kān (I didn’t say), u ka (I said), ū ka tān’ (let me not say yet), ù ká? (should I say?), ǘ kān? (won’t I say?), ǜ kān? (should I not say?).

2.2 Diphthongs

In writing the Igala token, the ‘mid-tones’ ‘ȯ’ and ‘ė’ with respect to the sounds /eu/ and /ei/ do not always remain the same for some of these sounds could be in context short, intermediate or long in tone, meaning that up to eight varieties of these two sounds can be gotten but in the main time, the author could only come about four glyphs for writing them, that being ė = /ei/, ǡ = /eiei/, ȯ = /eu/, and ȱ = /eueu/. These eight sounds are generated from the first vowels /e/ + /i/ = /ei/ and /e/ + /u/ = /eu/, out of which we have 8 diphthongs, although some of these are not currently portrayed by any orthographic symbol. There would thus be the need for additional symbols, i.e.; dotted ‘ė’ or ‘ȧ’ and ȯ orthographies with a grave or acute accents placed (either ways) on top of the dots on each to sound /eieii˩˩/ and /eueuu˩˩/ or /eiei:/ and /eueu:/.

The author would take just one example on this in respect of the word ele as is currently spelt which could mean gift or python. Here, the author has spelt them ėlė only as a matter of necessity for if they are to be spelt in their actual sense, the more fitting orthography for these could not all be gotten on the IPA list of symbols on Microsoft word, but this nonetheless (the act of writing them with dotted ‘e’, i.e.; ėlė) brings one closer to not confusing the spelling of the word as merely ele to mean four, ‘well fried’, or the other two instances (gift and python). For if the author was to spell the word python correctly, that would probably be, (ė with an acute on top of the dot) or (ė:) + lė, while gift would be, (ė with an acute on top of the dot) or (ė:) + l + (ė with an acute on top of the dot) or (ė:), while palm kernel oil would just be ėnė, ‘lies’ would be (ė with a grave on top of the dot) or (ė:˩) + mī, as leaf would be (ė with an acute on top of the dot) or (ǡ) + nghmi. The same applies to the letter ȯ where the fruit of the Palmyra palm ought to be (ȯ with an acute on top of the dot) + d + (ȯ with an acute on top of the dot), ȯbè (ant hill), which ought to be (ȯ with a grave on top of the dot) or (ė:) + bè, while ‘peep’ would have been ȯ + p + ė or just ‘ȯpė as in the statement ‘‘kp’ȯpė kā g’ēnē ki yā wā’’ (make a peep let’s see who’s coming).

2.3 Elision and other forms of Vowel Combination

Since the Igala language ‘somewhat’ follows the ‘French Lemon Rule’ or the VCV (vowel – consonant – vowel) Rule for it is rare to have situation where vowels follow themselves concurrently during words formation. Even in one instance where this rule is broken as shall be demonstrated below, this is more the result of an elision of a corresponding consonant sound than a naturally fixed format for that word. The only known exception to this rule is in respect to the word ābū which means ‘how’ or ‘what’, or where’, in which case, speakers tend to deliberately elicit the /b/ consonant or both the /b/ and and the succeeding /u/ vowels during communication for only the vowel /a/, or /a/ with /u/ in respect to the word mà (them) is educed so that when succeeded with the suffices ū (I or me), ē (you), ā (we), ī (she/he/it), and mà (them), the word becomes sounded as ā’ū (how or what did I, as in the sentence “ā’ū kā?” (how or what did I say), ā’ē (how or what did you, as in the sentence “ā’ē mā?” (how do you know), ā’ā (how or what did we, as in the statement, “ā’ā chė?” (how or what did we do), ā’ī (how or where did she/he/it, as in the sentence, “ā’ī lė?” (how or where did she/he/it go), and ā’ū mà or ā’mà (how or what do they, as in the sentence “ā’ū mà kō” or “ā’mā kō?” (What or how did they write?). Although, these other forms are used as substitute for ābū, they don’t however constitute a better alternative to ābū which is the actual form of how the word should be used for these are only corrupt versions of that word ābū.

More also, when writing sentences in the Igala language, certain letters are omitted from some words to avoid creepiness, muddiness or jumpiness and in the place of these omitted alphabets the apostrophe is used as a way of showing these elisions. The hyphen can be used to write certain words but for a writer who has a better understanding of the parts of speech, the use of the hyphen becomes almost unnecessary, but rather than committing the error of agglutinating the words or creating ambiguity problems in word usage as was observed by Malema et al. (2020), with a better understanding of inherent speech parts, it would have been preferable to have these words hyphenated. Since vowels are not reechoed, the use of the umlaut or dieresis as is common with some Northern European languages or English is not common since there was no need for any two vowels to be cluttered to be vocalized. More so, since there are currently no homegrown phonemic orthographies at the moment or because the development of a standardized version of the Igala language is still an evolving process, there is the need to check against the practice in the English language sound system where vowels could produce multiple sounds or where certain sounds are repeated even when the repetition is almost nearly unnecessary, as one will have with the repetition of the letter ‘l’ in the word ‘ball’, or the repetition of the ‘g’ in ‘egg’.

In English, letters as used in certain words do not usually follow a uniform pattern, i.e., letter ‘a’ (which could sound as /ei/ as in ‘cake’ or /æ/ as in ‘can’ /kæn/, or /o:/ as in ball and as in /a:/ in ‘cart’), letter ‘e’ (which could sound as /e/ as in ‘end’ or /i/ as in ‘elastic’ or ‘penis’), letter ‘i’ (which could pronounced as /i/ as in ink or /ai/ as in kite), letter ‘o’ (which could sound like /o/ as in on or /o:/ as in off, and as /u/ or /u:/ as in oops or ooze when repeated or as /Ʌ/, /o/, or /u:/ when letter ‘u’ comes immediately in front of letter ‘o’ in a spelling as we have in the words coupling or thought or coup), letter ‘u’ (which could be pronounced as /u/ as in book or /u:/ as in boom or /Ʌ/ as in umbrella, or as /3:/ as in urban when r is placed after letter u). Like the Hungarian language or other languages that do not depend exclusively on the use of the plain alphabet but employs some measure of diacritic to widen the range of available alphabets so as to accommodate more letters that are produced distinctly, while writing the Igala language, we must not necessarily change the sound of a monophony by that mere rule that some words having similar spelling be pronounced differently, neither are additional phoneme added to a vowel or a consonant as a way of changing the sound that the carrying letter is supposed to produce into another phonetic sound as we have with the English words plough or bought, and fort and resort. This problem of pronunciation could pose some headache to a new learner of the English language.

3 Conclusion

This paper provides an overview of vowels used in the Igala language. Vowels are central to word formation and without them speech making becomes impossible. These vowels combine with the consonant sound to form words but this is not to say that without the consonant sounds, certain words cannot be verbalized or written as in the case of the Igala language where certain words can be written without the combination of consonant sounds with the consonant sounds as it applies to the four vowels, a, e. i, u which by standing alone means something. This is largely the result of the fact that, the Igala language has a wide ranging tone system.

4 Limitation of the Study

As an under-resourced indigenous language whose contextual study as a culture transmitting agent is relatively new (Ojoajogwu, 2017), approaches towards formalization of the Igala language in a more modern sense has been done scantily but also indiscriminately in ways that are devoid of any serious scientific consideration, more so that those who made attempt at writing the language are either not language specialist who understand scientific rudiments regarding the rules of language as it relates with grammar and tone pattern and structure, or were individuals who were too hasty in a way that deep thinking regarding the depth and breadth of both grammar and tones contained in the language were not attended to at great depth. It was therefore not surprising that among some of the available materials that contain words, sentences and meanings, one often see variations in diacritic forms used to demonstrate tones and pronunciation from scholar to scholar and in some cases certain diacritic orthographies that can represent some tones are left out. Added to this is also a variation with respect to accent and tribal differences in tones from one area to the other which has further influence the work output of the various writers. This factor therefore contribute to the first limitation of this very study, because understanding the inherent flaws regarding the inappropriate diacritic orthographies adopted by other writers, the very writer developed separate tools representing several tones, which while being scientifically logical, nonetheless, confine exclusively not to any previous methods used by the earlier writers.

Although, prior to this current work, no effort was made to study the oral form of the Igala language from the basic in a more comprehensive form, such as is done in this paper, a fact which accounted for the neglect of some vowel forms represented by special symbols as stated above. But vowels were not the only orthographies left out for even consonant orthographies that present a single symbol as a unit of consonant cluster such as the ‘c’ with an over dot, i.e.; ċ, or the caron or hacek as it is at times called; č, both of whom represent the sound /ch/ in other world languages, as with respect to the English word ‘church’, and the symbol ŋ which is supposed to represent a single unit of the cluster /ng/ as in the word ‘ānyīngā’ (finger nail) which ought to have been ‘āɲīŋā’ or the ‘ny’ cluster (ɲ) is hardly used. While the non-use of these orthographies by previous writer posed some limitation with respect to how this very writer would have written syllables that should carry these orthographies, it was also a limitation of this study because this very writer failed to do otherwise from what has been held down for so long owing to English influence on the writing of the Igala language. While the tilde ñ which bear the /nn/ cluster and which is occasionally used by some of the earlier writers, albeit with some misconception here and there, in this work, words boiling around this very sign were not used and for that reason, the sign was bypassed, but in future attempt, it shall be treated with special consideration.

This fact also poses a limitation of its own. Added to this missing vowels or undiscovered vowels by this author is the absence of a single symbol for the consonant clusters ‘kw’, ‘kp’, nw, and ‘gh’. Could it then be that this symbolization is the result of the fact that, there is no over dotted ‘k’ on the IPA list of symbols or because the ‘p’ in the combination forms a more voiced phoneme than the ‘k’ or it is because the people by choice, chose to write like that? 2 Vowels and Words in Igala Language

2 Voyelles et mots dans la langue Igala

The Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary of English defined lexicon as; “all the words and phrases used in a particular language or subject; all the words and phrases used and known by a particular person or group of people” (Hornby, 2015). With respect to the Igala as with other languages, lexical formation begins with conjoining strings of phones or alphabets to form morphemes from which words are created. Currently, no known homemade or standard alphabetical or orthographical system exists for writing the Igala language except for the esoteric signs and markings; usually on the floor, used by traditional witchdoctors, which is not known to the vast majority of the people and that also did not serve formal purposes within society. In the midst of this foibles induced by this lack of inbred phonemes, writers beginning from the modern era, are known to either select the Latin phonetic symbols or the Arabic phonemes to replicate or transcribe words of Igala origin, what is at times referred to in West Africa as a’jami or ajami (Mc Laughlin, 2017).

Le Oxford Advanced Learner's Dictionary of English définit le lexique comme suit : « l'ensemble des mots et expressions utilisés dans une langue ou un domaine particulier ; l'ensemble des mots et expressions utilisés et connus par une personne ou un groupe de personnes donné » (Hornby, 2015). En ce qui concerne les Igala comme pour les autres langues, la formation lexicale commence par la jonction de chaînes de phones ou d'alphabets pour former des morphèmes à partir desquels les mots sont créés. Actuellement, aucun alphabet ou système orthographique standard ou maison n'existe pour écrire la langue Igala, sauf les signes ésotériques et les marques, généralement sur le sol, utilisés par les médecins traditionnels, qui ne sont pas connus de la grande majorité des gens et qui ne servent pas non plus de buts formels au sein de la société. Au milieu de ces faiblesses induites par ce manque de phonèmes innés, les auteurs, à partir de l'ère moderne, sont connus pour choisir soit les symboles phonétiques latins, soit les phonèmes arabes afin de reproduire ou de transcrire des mots d'origine igala, ce que l'on appelle parfois en Afrique de l'Ouest a'jami ou ajami (Mc Laughlin, 2017).

Notwithstanding the fact that Igala is a tone-based language (Rodriguez, 2014; Dawson et al. 2015; Adeniyi, 2016), while attempting to study the structure of the Igala language, most of the attention of scholars has been on parts of speech, without much effort been dedicated towards tones and sounds with the result that without a prior knowledge of the language words written are hard to pronounce, in some cases not even with the English translation of these provided, and as for those categories of writers who tried to express tones using diacritic orthographies, without a prior knowledge of diacritic by the readers, it is still hard for the readers to decipher what was written, besides, through occasional failures of writers to provide double slash transcription boxes or oral parenthesis of sounds represented by orthographic symbols in their works, the fact that these symbols produce certain sounds from place to place and from one writer to the other makes it very hard for readers to grasp with the sound frame being inferred by these writers.

Nonobstant le fait que l'igala est une langue à tons (Rodriguez, 2014 ; Dawson et al. 2015 ; Adeniyi, 2016), en tentant d'étudier la structure de la langue igala, l'essentiel de l'attention des chercheurs a porté sur les parties du discours, sans que beaucoup d'efforts soient consacrés aux tons et aux sons, avec pour résultat que, sans une connaissance préalable de la langue, les mots écrits sont difficiles à prononcer, dans certains cas même avec la traduction anglaise de ceux-ci fournie, et quant aux catégories d'auteurs qui ont essayé d'exprimer les tons à l'aide d'orthographes diacritiques, sans une connaissance préalable des diacritiques par les lecteurs, il est toujours difficile pour les lecteurs de déchiffrer ce qui a été écrit ; en outre, en raison des manquements occasionnels des auteurs à fournir des encadrés de transcription entre doubles barres obliques ou des parenthèses orales des sons représentés par les symboles orthographiques dans leurs travaux, le fait que ces symboles produisent certains sons d'un endroit à l'autre et d'un auteur à l'autre rend très difficile pour les lecteurs de saisir le cadre sonore inféré par ces auteurs.

Abraham (2017; 2019) as well as, Emah and Ojonugwa (2020) while using diacritic orthography to write words, partly to show how differences in pitch change word meanings as was once observed by Yip (2002), failed to provide double slash transcription of the sounds represented by the orthographic symbols incorporated. In the context of this particular paper thus, Latin alphabets were adopted for fashioning Igala sounds, words, and phrasal forms using diacritic symbols bearing phonemic modes similar to Blench (2011). All the English phonemes were adopted except the letters, Q, V, S, X, and Z which were discarded consonants for lack of relevance as it is with Yoruba (Kasali et al. 2021) while the letter ‘c’ is applied only in combination with the letter ‘h’ to produce the digraph /t∫/ that is used for writing words such as ūchōnā (somebody’s name meaning ‘creator’ or just ‘the creator’ in a more general sense of the word), as can be had with English words as chicken and kitchen. More also, the letter ‘c’ is not used in writing /k/ sounding words that are often written with the letter ‘c’ such as can /kæn/, neither can it be devoted to writing a sound /s/ bearing word such as ceiling for this would have been sílīni /si:lini/ or actually chílīni /t∫i:lini/ because of the still the more total absence of the ‘s’ consonant within the Igala lexical system. Dans le contexte de cet article particulier, les alphabets latins ont été adoptés pour façonner des sons, des mots et des formes phrasées Igala en utilisant des symboles diacritiques portant des modes phonémiques similaires à Blench (2011). Toutes les phonèmes anglaises ont été adoptées sauf les lettres Q, V, S, X et Z qui ont été écartées en tant que consonnes pour manque de pertinence comme c'est le cas avec le yoruba (Kasali et al. 2021) tandis que la lettre ‘c’ est appliquée uniquement en combinaison avec la lettre ‘h’ pour produire le digraphe /t∫/ qui est utilisé pour écrire des mots tels que ūchōnā (le nom de quelqu'un signifiant ‘créateur’ ou simplement ‘le créateur’ dans un sens plus général du mot), comme on peut le voir avec les mots anglais chicken et kitchen. De plus, la lettre « c » n'est pas utilisée pour écrire des mots au son /k/ qui sont souvent écrits avec la lettre « c » comme can /kæn/, et elle ne peut pas non plus être consacrée à l'écriture d'un mot portant le son /s/ comme ceiling, car cela aurait été sílīni /si:lini/ ou en fait chílīni /t∫i:lini/ en raison de l'absence encore plus totale de la consonne « s » dans le système lexical igala. De même, plutôt que d'écrire le mot quantity avec la lettre ‘q’, dans notre propre orthographe igala, l'orthographe du mot quantity apparaîtrait comme ‘kwontity’.

Writing vowels during word formation often involves the embracement of any one out of some three alternative styles. The first is the exclusive use of plain alphabets in which case only vowels that are unadorned with glyphs would be put to use. This first form does not embody the use of diacritic in any way and if vowel are to be crafted, that would depend upon the efficacious amalgam of plain alphabets to produce words that are not delineated in the existing letter ordering as in the spelling of the word Igalaa in wiktionary.org in which case the underlined double ‘a’ in that word Igalaa does not merely suggest a long vowel (i.e.; /a:/) but a repetition of the vowels as /a/ + /a/ to give a sound much like a gliding ‘are are’. The second form of these on-hand modes which this author here assume to be actually impractical depend exclusively on the use of diacritic, if not in writing the plain orthographic characters but in the scrawling of the oral parenthesis of words as in the Webster’s II New College Dictionary. The third style involves the commixing of the other two diametrical forms, i.e.; the adoption of both the plain vowels and the diacritic–based vowels. Any of these three forms is cogent enough provided they goose-step along the conventional morphophonemic norms. It is the third form of these approaches-that of mixing plain Latin alphabet with Latin diacritic symbols, that would be utilized in this particular work.

L'écriture des voyelles pendant la formation des mots implique souvent l'adoption de l'un des trois styles alternatifs. La première est l'utilisation exclusive d'alphabets simples, auquel cas seules les voyelles qui ne sont pas ornées de glyphes seraient utilisées. Cette première forme n'incorpore en aucune façon l'utilisation de signes diacritiques et, si des voyelles doivent être façonnées, cela dépendrait de l'amalgame efficace d'alphabets simples pour produire des mots qui ne sont pas délimités dans l'ordre des lettres existant, comme dans l'orthographe du mot Igalaa dans wiktionary.org, auquel cas le double « a » souligné dans ce mot Igalaa ne suggère pas simplement une voyelle longue (c'est-à-dire /a:/) mais une répétition des voyelles comme /a/ + /a/ pour donner un son ressemblant beaucoup à un « are are » glissant. La deuxième forme de ces modes disponibles, que l'auteur considère ici comme réellement impraticable, dépend exclusivement de l'utilisation de signes diacritiques, sinon dans l'écriture des caractères orthographiques simples, du moins dans le griffonnage de la parenthèse orale des mots, comme dans le Webster's II New College Dictionary. Le troisième style implique le mélange des deux autres formes diamétrales, c'est-à-dire l'adoption à la fois des voyelles simples et des voyelles à base diacritique. Chacune de ces trois formes est suffisamment convaincante pourvu qu'elles suivent les normes morphophonémiques conventionnelles. C'est la troisième forme de ces approches - celle de mélanger l'alphabet latin ordinaire avec des symboles diacritiques latins, qui serait utilisée dans ce travail particulier.

In the oral parenthesis provided in the Webster’s II New College Dictionary and thesaraus.yourdictionary.com, short vowels were represented with the application of the breve- a form of diacritic that is presented like the lower half of a cycle above the vowels, i.e.; ă, ĕ, ĭ, ŏ, ŭ, but in writing this work, rather than to vent these letters using the breve, they were written just as plain as possible, i.e.; ‘a, e, i, o, u’. Be that said, the other point of departure concern the sounds the other diacritic orthographies used by these esteem dictionaries produced with respect to our own keys here. In the Webster’s II New College Dictionary (1999), the macron was adopted to produce the unique form of the long vowels ō ∼ /eu/ as in the word old /ōld/ in much of a respect used by Dawson et al. (2015) and ā ∼ as in the word said /sād/, but in this work, moved by the peculiarity of the Igala language which partially sets it apart from English and the prima facie claim by Wells (2001) that the macron is ratified for initiating a middle locus between the short vowels and the long vowels.

Dans la parenthèse orale fournie dans le Webster’s II New College Dictionary et thesaraus.yourdictionary.com, les voyelles courtes ont été représentées avec l’application du breve – une forme de diacritique qui est présentée comme la moitié inférieure d’un cycle au-dessus des voyelles, c’est-à-dire ă, ĕ, ĭ, ŏ, ŭ, mais dans l’écriture de ce travail, plutôt que d’émettre ces lettres en utilisant le breve, elles ont été écrites aussi simplement que possible, c’est-à-dire ‘a, e, i, o, u’. Cela étant dit, l'autre point de départ concerne les sons des autres orthographes diacritiques utilisées par ces dictionnaires d'estime produits par rapport à nos propres clés ici. Dans le Webster's II New College Dictionary (1999), le macron a été adopté pour produire la forme unique des voyelles longues ō ∼ /eu/ comme dans le mot old /ōld/, dans une large mesure de la même manière que l'ont utilisé Dawson et al. (2015), et ā ∼ comme dans le mot said /sād/, mais dans ce travail, motivé par la particularité de la langue igala qui la distingue en partie de l'anglais et par l'affirmation prima facie de Wells (2001) selon laquelle le macron est validé pour instaurer un lieu intermédiaire entre les voyelles brèves et les voyelles longues.

Macrons were therefore used in this work, not for the production of the ā = /ei/ and ō = /eu/ sounds for these are in this work illustrated by the use of the letters ‘o’ and ‘e’ with dots on top of them (i.e.; ȯ = /eu/ and ė = /ei/). Although, there are a few improvements lately, most of the previous writings, following mainly from the recommendation of the 1984 Igala Orthographic Committee which recognized the under dotted ọ and ẹ for the low tones o ∼ /o˩/ | e ∼ /ee˩˩/ (i.e.; as in the spelling of ọjọ /oo˩˩joo˩˩/ in Okwoli (1996) and Ahiaba (2015), and ẹrẹ /ee˩˩ree˩˩/ in Ayegba et al. (2017) for sounding the falling /o/ and /e/ sounds, while the quartet reserved the plain forms of ‘o’ and ‘e’ alphabets for the rising and falling forms of /eu/ and /ei/ sounds as used for Yoruba in aroadedictionary.com, most of these scholars recognized only 7 vowels plus a so called 24 consonant sounds (Ojoaogwu, 2017) or 32 symbols in whole according to Miachi and Armstrong (1986), which meant they left out a number of vowels and therefore downplaying the use of the macron in the work of succeeding writers with the only exceptions being Omachonu (2011) and to an extent, Dawson et al. (2015) who either used it in much of a respect as done in this paper or as was done in the Webster’s II New College Dictionary.

Les macrons ont donc été utilisés dans ce travail, non pour la production des sons ā = /ei/ et ō = /eu/, car ceux-ci sont dans ce travail illustrés par l'utilisation des lettres ‘o’ et ‘e’ avec des points au-dessus d'elles (c'est-à-dire ; ȯ = /eu/ et ė = /ei/). Bien qu'il y ait eu quelques améliorations récemment, la plupart des écrits antérieurs, suivant principalement la recommandation du Comité orthographique igala de 1984 qui a reconnu les lettres avec un point souscrit ọ et ẹ pour les tons bas o ∼ /o˩/ | e ∼ /ee˩˩/ (c'est-à-dire comme dans l'orthographe de ọjọ /oo˩˩joo˩˩/ dans Okwoli (1996) et Ahiaba (2015), et ẹrẹ /ee˩˩ree˩˩/ dans Ayegba et al. (2017) pour réaliser les sons descendants /o/ et /e/, tandis que le quartet réservait les formes simples des alphabets « o » et « e » pour les formes ascendantes et descendantes des sons /eu/ et /ei/ comme utilisé pour le yoruba dans aroadedictionary.com), la plupart de ces chercheurs n'ont reconnu que 7 voyelles plus un ensemble dit de 24 sons consonantiques (Ojoaogwu, 2017) ou 32 symboles au total selon Miachi et Armstrong (1986), ce qui signifie qu'ils ont omis un certain nombre de voyelles et, par conséquent, minimisé l'emploi du macron dans les travaux des auteurs ultérieurs, les seules exceptions étant Omachonu (2011) et, dans une certaine mesure, Dawson et al. (2015) qui l'ont utilisé soit avec un respect comparable à celui observé dans le présent article, soit comme cela a été fait dans le Webster's II New College Dictionary.

Thus, the use of the macron in this article follows a model that is similar to its use by Karshima (2012) in respect to the Tiv language or in respect to the Igala word ìkpā (bag) in Rodriguez (2014); not to be confused with íkpa (knock knees), in which ā = /æ/ as in the American English pronunciation of the words ‘plan’ and ‘stand’ in oxfordlearnersdictionary.com as an intermediate tone between the sound /a/ and /a:/ of the IPA sound system, ē = /ɛ/ as in the English word ‘any’, i.e.; an intermediate tone between the sound /e/ and /e:/ of the IPA sound system) as was used in respect to the word ònēkèlē in Rodriguez (2015) which the author of this very paper would here again rewrite as ōnēkēlē (male), ī = /i:/ > /x/ > /i/, i.e.; an intermediate tone between the sound /i/ and /i:/ of the IPA sound system, ō = /ɔ/ as in the cardinal mid-back rounded ‘o’ used in sounding the English word ‘but’ or the Igala word ōkō /ɔkɔ/ in respect to airplane or ship which is in contrast to òkò /o˩koo˩˩/ (millipede) or okā /okæ/ (style, i.e.; acrobatic) or ókō /o:kɔ/ (money), with ‘ō’ being an intermediate tone between the sound /o/ and /o:/ of the IPA sound system, ū = /u:/ > /x/ > /u/ as in Lithuanian, Livonian, and Maori, i.e.; an intermediate tone between the sound /u/ and /u:/ of the IPA sound system. The x in the oral parentheses represents the indefinite phonemic sound for the diacritic orthographies ī & ū ∼ x. Decision to jettison the breve in this paper has a twofold underling. First was to ensure the design of a coherent orthographic system with pure vowels used in their original orthographic forms as it is in most English language texts, notwithstanding whatever graphical modification or characterization that the addition of impending glyphs to the first letter thenceforth might confer. The second reason propped from the need to eschew the immanent supererogatory burden of having to write them with an added character when they could remain plain or without partial alteration and still maintain their functions as short vowels.

Ainsi, l'utilisation du macron dans cet article suit un modèle similaire à son utilisation par Karshima (2012) en ce qui concerne la langue Tiv ou en ce qui concerne le mot Igala ìkpā (sac) dans Rodriguez (2014) ; à ne pas confondre avec íkpa (genoux cagneux), où ā = /æ/ comme dans la prononciation américaine des mots ‘plan’ et ‘stand’ sur oxfordlearnersdictionary.com en tant que ton intermédiaire entre le son /a/ et /a:/ du système de sons de l'IPA, ē = /ɛ/ comme dans le mot anglais ‘any’, c'est-à-dire ; un ton intermédiaire entre le son /e/ et /e:/ du système de sons de l'IPA) comme cela a été utilisé en ce qui concerne le mot ònēkèlē dans Rodriguez (2015) que l'auteur de cet article même réécrirait ici à nouveau comme ōnēkēlē (mâle), ī = /i:/ > /x/ > /i/, c'est-à-dire ; un ton intermédiaire entre le son /i/ et /i:/ du système de sons de l'IPA, ō = /ɔ/ comme dans le cardinal mi-arrière arrondi ‘o’ utilisé pour prononcer le mot anglais ‘but’ ou le mot Igala ōkō /ɔkɔ/ en ce qui concerne l'avion ou le bateau qui est en contraste avec òkò /o˩koo˩˩/ (mille-pattes) ou okā /okæ/ (style, c'est-à-dire ; acrobatique) ou ókō /o:kɔ/ (argent), avec ‘ō’ étant un ton intermédiaire entre le son /o/ et /o:/ du système de sons de l'IPA, ū = /u:/ > /x/ > /u/ comme en lituanien, livonien et maori, c'est-à-dire ; un ton intermédiaire entre le son /u/ et /u:/ du système de sons de l'IPA. Le x dans les parenthèses orales représente le son phonémique indéfini pour les orthographies diacritiques ī & ū ∼ x. La décision d’abandonner le breve dans ce document a une double motivation. Le premier était d'assurer la conception d'un système orthographique cohérent avec des voyelles pures utilisées dans leurs formes orthographiques originales comme c'est le cas dans la plupart des textes de langue anglaise, nonobstant toute modification ou caractérisation graphique que l'ajout de glyphes imminents à la première lettre pourrait désormais conférer. La seconde raison s'appuyait sur la nécessité d'éviter le fardeau immanent de devoir les écrire avec un caractère ajouté alors qu'elles pouvaient rester simples ou sans altération partielle et conserver leurs fonctions de voyelles courtes.

The desuetude towards the breve as done here was not a deliberate attempt to undo the merits of the Webster’s II New College Dictionary, their discretion are still very valid, specifically with respect to the study of the English language. This writer is well abreast with the unequivocal fact that left with a vagaries of indicative keys to choose from, it is always incumbent upon each writer to cherry-pick both the phonotactical and the morphotactical forms that are amenable to the language being worked on (Pretorius and Bosch, 2009), it should however be noted that the use of ā = /ei/ and ō = /eu/, despite not being wrong in any sense, doesn’t nonetheless conforms with the original phonological flow pattern enshrined in their root alphabet ‘a’ which produces the sounds /a/ and /a:/, and ‘e’ which produces the sounds /e/ and /e:/. Bearing in mind the fact that the middle tone is sometimes perceived to be a conflation of two vowels (i.e.; /e/ + /i/ = /ei/ and /e/ + /u/ = /eu/ as it is in English), then it would not be totally wrong to say that the editors of the Webster’s II New College Dictionary were right in their own right to have used the macron in the context they employed it, besides, language analyst do agree that, ‘there is no such thing as a standard form when it comes to matters of language’ (Laperre 2020) and as Szczegielniak laconically hinted, “spelling or orthography does not consistently represent the sounds of language”. Thus, in Igala language, middle tone implies is a position between a long vowel and a short vowel such as the middle sound between the high /a:/ and the low /a/, i.e.; an ‘a’ that has a higher tone than /a/ but yet is still less pitched than /a:/ represented as x > /a/ < /a:/.

La désuétude envers le brève telle qu'utilisée ici n'était pas une tentative délibérée de remettre en cause les mérites du Webster’s II New College Dictionary, leurs discrétions restent encore très valables, spécifiquement en ce qui concerne l'étude de la langue anglaise. Cet auteur est bien conscient du fait incontestable que, laissé avec les caprices des touches indicatives à choisir, il incombe toujours à chaque auteur de sélectionner à la fois les formes phonotactiques et morphotactiques qui sont propices à la langue sur laquelle on travaille (Pretorius et Bosch, 2009), il convient toutefois de noter que l'utilisation de ā = /ei/ et ō = /eu/, bien que n'étant pas incorrecte en soi, ne respecte néanmoins pas le modèle de flux phonologique original inscrit dans leur alphabet racine 'a' qui produit les sons /a/ et /a:/, et 'e' qui produit les sons /e/ et /e:/. Gardant à l'esprit le fait que le ton moyen est parfois perçu comme une fusion de deux voyelles (c'est-à-dire /e/ + /i/ = /ei/ et /e/ + /u/ = /eu/ comme c'est le cas en anglais), alors il ne serait pas totalement faux de dire que les éditeurs du Webster's II New College Dictionary étaient dans leur bon droit d'avoir utilisé le macron dans le contexte où ils l'ont employé, d'ailleurs, les analystes du langage s'accordent à dire qu'« il n'existe pas de forme standard en matière de langage » (Laperre 2020) et comme Szczegielniak l'a laconiquement suggéré, « l'orthographe ne représente pas systématiquement les sons du langage ». Ainsi, en langue igala, le ton moyen implique une position entre une voyelle longue et une voyelle courte, comme le son intermédiaire entre le /a:/ haut et le /a/ bas, c.-à-d. ; un 'a' qui a un ton plus haut que /a/ mais qui est encore moins aigu que /a:/, représenté par x > /a/ < /a:/.

This writer would have loved to have the alphabet ė which is hereby sounded /ei/ written as ȧ, but because most words in the language are already written with the letter ‘e’ to sound /ei/, as in words such as ugbede /ugbeidei/ or ele /eilei/, the author therefore chose to maintain the letter ė which comes closer to the now more popular tradition. The decision to use the alphabet ȧ instead of ė would have matched perfectly with the use of the over dotted ‘o’ (ȯ) to produce the sound /eu/. The use of the macron itself becomes vital considering the fact that, most words in the Igala language are sounded within this mid tone context.

Cet auteur aurait aimé avoir l'alphabet ė qui est ici prononcé /ei/ écrit comme ȧ, mais parce que la plupart des mots de la langue sont déjà écrits avec la lettre ‘e’ pour sonner /ei/, comme dans les mots tels que ugbede /ugbeidei/ ou ele /eilei/, l'auteur a donc choisi de maintenir la lettre ė qui se rapproche davantage de la tradition désormais plus populaire. La décision d'utiliser l'alphabet ȧ au lieu de ė aurait parfaitement correspondu à l'utilisation du ‘o' surmonté d'un point (ȯ) pour produire le son /eu/. L'utilisation du macron lui-même devient vitale compte tenu du fait que la plupart des mots dans la langue Igala sont prononcés dans ce contexte de ton moyen.

As in writing the Yoruba language or French (Olufemi, 2022; Nolte et al. 2018), the acute and the grave forms of diacritic were also used although with a contrastive difference in phonation between the Igala words written here using this form that is different from French as regards to the word café, in which case, the é as in the French and in the form used by Rodriguez (2014) in relation to the word ‘éʤú’ ∼ /eiʤu:/ (face), is pronounced /ei/, more like the English word rake /reik/, whereas, the orthography é as it relates with the Igala and sometimes with the Yoruba; as with the word òré (friend) in (Oshodi, 2016) ∼ /oo˩˩re:/, is nonetheless pronounced /e:/ as in English. Thus, we must agree with Cahill, (2019) that, in using diacritic tools, “the challenge is that the tone marks are usually not consistent among scholars”, with the result that, a letter é that is pronounced as /e:/ in one instance or by one scholar is pronounced at another instance or by another scholar as /ei/ as in spelling of wálé ∼ /wa:lei/ (come home) in Dalamu (2019) which is at variance with Oshodi (2016)’s /e:/ above.

Comme dans l'écriture de la langue Yoruba ou du français (Olufemi, 2022; Nolte et al. 2018), les formes aiguës et graves de diacritiques ont également été utilisées, bien qu'avec une différence contrastive de phonation entre les mots Igala écrits ici sous cette forme, différente du français concernant le mot café, auquel cas, le é comme dans le français et sous la forme utilisée par Rodriguez (2014) en relation avec le mot ‘éʤú’ ∼ /eiʤu:/ (visage), est prononcé /ei/, plus comme le mot anglais rake /reik/, tandis que, l'orthographe é telle qu'elle se rapporte à l'Igala et parfois au Yoruba; comme dans le mot òré (ami) dans (Oshodi, 2016) ∼ /oo˩˩re:/, est néanmoins prononcé /e:/ comme en anglais. Ainsi, nous devons être d'accord avec Cahill, (2019) pour dire que, dans l'utilisation des outils diacritiques, « le défi est que les marques de ton ne sont généralement pas cohérentes entre les chercheurs », avec pour résultat qu'une lettre é qui est prononcée /e:/ dans un cas ou par un chercheur est prononcée dans un autre cas ou par un autre chercheur comme /ei/, comme dans l'orthographe de wálé ∼ /wa:lei/ (viens à la maison) dans Dalamu (2019), ce qui est en désaccord avec le /e:/ de Oshodi (2016) ci-dessus.

Here too, the grave accent (i.e.; à, è, ì, ò, ù) was used to indicate the low tones as was done in Yip (2002). The use of certain diacritic marks in this work might be counted as inappropriate as is now apparent with respect to how the macron is used here which is already in conflict with how it was used in the Webster’s II Dictionary or how any of these marks are represented in other languages such as French, Dutch, Portuguese, Italian, and Mohawk (Finegan, 2012).

Ici aussi, l'accent grave (c'est-à-dire ; à, è, ì, ò, ù) a été utilisé pour indiquer les tons bas comme cela a été fait dans Yip (2002). L'utilisation de certaines marques diacritiques dans ce travail pourrait être considérée comme inappropriée, comme il est maintenant apparent en ce qui concerne l'utilisation du macron ici, qui est déjà en conflit avec la manière dont il a été utilisé dans le Webster’s II Dictionary ou avec la manière dont l'une de ces marques est représentée dans d'autres langues telles que le français, le néerlandais, le portugais, l'italien et le mohawk (Finegan, 2012).

A critic might ask to know why ‘this author did not use the breve diacritic in writing the short vowels’, which has already been reflected upon above. To cover any anomaly that emanates from how certain things are represented in this work, enabling tools with instructions regarding how to pronounce the diacritic forms is provided, with the hope that the writer of this piece and the readers might be on a common wavelength in comprehending what has been put down. Hence, words having these marks which as this writer suppose might not follow the existing methods in some languages, can be pronounced; if not so perfectly, it would be ‘nearly perfect’.

Un critique pourrait demander pourquoi « cet auteur n’a pas utilisé le bref diacritique pour écrire les voyelles courtes », ce qui a déjà été évoqué plus haut. Pour couvrir toute anomalie qui émane de la façon dont certaines choses sont représentées dans ce travail, des outils d'activation avec des instructions sur la façon de prononcer les formes diacritiques sont fournis, dans l'espoir que l'auteur de ce morceau et les lecteurs pourraient être sur une longueur d'onde commune dans la compréhension de ce qui a été mis par écrit. Par conséquent, les mots ayant ces marques qui, comme l'écrivain le suppose, pourraient ne pas suivre les méthodes existantes dans certaines langues, peuvent être prononcés; si ce n'est pas si parfaitement, ce serait «presque parfait».

The Igala vowels are still yet not limited to the above mentioned accent forms. In this study, it is revealed that some vowels are a combination of double sound of the same vowel either ways raised between short or long vowel and then pitched lowly or highly with the corresponding vowel (like a bend), as was done with respect to that closing tone in the word Igalaa. For this category of vowels, the author came up with the idea of having orthographies with macrons on top of them that are further capped with a grave or the acute diacritic but only two of the short vowels ḕ, ḗ and ṑ, ṓ could meet this requirement, and so the author thought of remedying this problem with the use of the circumflex having either the acute or the grave diacritic mark on top of them, i.e.; ấ, ầ, but only the letter ‘a’ has this feature out of the other three still missing letters conveying the short vowels (a, i. and u). The next that could be done was to check for diacritic graphemes that have the features that can best solve the ‘u’ and the ‘i’ problem (regarding this category of sound), but this author once again stumbled upon the idea of using the ‘u’ dieresis capped with either the grave or the acute mark, i.e.; ǘ and ǜ, but the ‘i’ symbols was still missing, and so, the author thought of using the Greek small letter iota with psili and varia (ἲ) and ἵ small letter iota with psili and oxia which the author found as the only current fitting option for use here.

Les voyelles Igala ne sont pas encore limitées aux formes d'accent mentionnées ci-dessus. Dans cette étude, il est révélé que certaines voyelles sont une combinaison du double son de la même voyelle, élevé de part et d'autre entre voyelle courte ou longue, puis abaissé ou élevé avec la voyelle correspondante (comme une courbure), comme cela a été fait pour ce ton de clôture dans le mot Igalaa. Pour cette catégorie de voyelles, l'auteur a eu l'idée d'avoir des orthographes avec des macrons au-dessus d'elles, surmontés en outre d'un diacritique grave ou aigu, mais seules deux des voyelles courtes ḕ, ḗ et ṑ, ṓ pouvaient satisfaire à cette exigence, et l'auteur a donc pensé remédier à ce problème en utilisant le circonflexe portant soit le diacritique aigu, soit le diacritique grave au-dessus d'eux, c'est-à-dire ; ấ, ầ, mais seule la lettre « a » possède cette caractéristique parmi les trois autres lettres encore manquantes qui transmettent les voyelles courtes (a, i. et u). La chose suivante qui pouvait être faite était de chercher des graphèmes diacritiques ayant les caractéristiques pouvant le mieux résoudre le problème du « u » et du « i » (concernant cette catégorie de son), mais cet auteur est une fois de plus tombé sur l'idée d'utiliser le tréma du « u » surmonté soit du signe grave, soit du signe aigu, c'est-à-dire ; ǘ et ǜ, mais les symboles du « i » manquaient encore, et l'auteur a donc pensé utiliser la lettre minuscule grecque iota avec esprit doux et varia (ἲ) et ἵ la lettre minuscule iota avec esprit doux et oxia, que l'auteur a trouvées comme la seule option actuellement appropriée à utiliser ici.

2.1 Contextual Usage of Vowels

2.1 Utilisation contextuelle des voyelles

In writing the orthographic forms of Igala vowels, one would thus have 30 of these, with each of the short vowels producing 5 variants each while from the glyph ȯ and ė can be gotten an additional 8 diphthongs. In the Webster’s II New College Dictionary, orthographic characters and phonetic sounds adhering or being inherent in the long vowels of the English language were written in the forms; /â/ or /ä/ = /a: /, /ê/ = /e: /, /ē/ = /i: /, /ô/ = /o: /, /û/ = /u: /, /ā/ = /ei/, and ō = /eu/. In this work the above 5 high tones were replicated as á = /a: /, é = /e: /, í = /i: /, ó = /o: /, ú = /u: /. Added to these long vowels are the 5 short vowels a = /a/, e = /e/, i = /i/, o = /o/, u = /u/. There are also 5 macron diacritic to produce the intermediate of these vowels, ā, ē, ī, ō, ū, whose vocal forms have been explained above.

Dans l'écriture des formes orthographiques des voyelles Igala, on aurait ainsi 30 de celles-ci, chacune des voyelles courtes produisant 5 variantes chacune, tandis que les glyphes ȯ et ė permettent d'obtenir 8 diphtongues supplémentaires. Dans le Webster’s II New College Dictionary, les caractères orthographiques et les sons phonétiques adhérant ou étant inhérents aux voyelles longues de la langue anglaise ont été écrits sous les formes ; /â/ ou /ä/ = /a: /, /ê/ = /e: /, /ē/ = /i: /, /ô/ = /o: /, /û/ = /u: /, /ā/ = /ei/, et ō = /eu/. Dans ce travail, les 5 tons aigus ci-dessus ont été reproduits comme á = /a: /, é = /e: /, í = /i: /, ó = /o: /, ú = /u: /. À ces voyelles longues s'ajoutent les 5 voyelles courtes a = /a/, e = /e/, i = /i/, o = /o/, u = /u/. Il existe également 5 macrons diacritiques pour produire les voyelles intermédiaires, ā, ē, ī, ō, ū, dont les formes vocaliques ont été expliquées ci-dessus.

There are 5 long vowels that are stressed with falling tones, i.e.; as in à = /aa˩˩/, è = /ee˩˩/, ì = /ii˩˩/, ò = /oo˩˩/, ù = /uu˩˩/. With the exception of the orthographic symbol ‘o’ which is capped with the double grave and double acute accent (i.e.; ȍ /oo˩˩˩˩/ and ő /o::/) used to express the extra-low and the extra-high accent of the /o/ sound, the other four orthographies bearing the primary vowels (a, e, i, and u) whose logographic form (as lone orthographies) expresses the pronouns ‘a’ (me), ‘e’ (you), ‘i’ (s/he or it), and ‘u’ (first person me) which do convey emphasis and mood, have dieresis-like rise-rise inflective sounds with one, the combine ‘a’ (ấ = /a:a:/) having a circumflex on top of it with an acute glyph capping the circumflex. Others are, the combined ‘e’ (ḗ = /e:e:/) which has a macron glyphs on top of it with an acute accents capping the macron, one other has a dieresis glyph with a grave accent on top of the glyph (ǘ = /u:u:/), and the fourth is the Greek iota with psili and varia with the combined ‘i’ (ἵ = /i:i:/). Each of these 4 vowel begin with short forms of vowels that are succeeded by corresponding long vowels of the same sound bracket within given syllables, more like ending the sound flow in the middle but yet again stressing it forward (an intermittent break that is followed by a change in flow).

Il y a 5 voyelles longues qui sont accentuées avec des tons descendants, c'est-à-dire ; comme dans à = /aa˩˩/, è = /ee˩˩/, ì = /ii˩˩/, ò = /oo˩˩/, ù = /uu˩˩/. À l'exception du symbole orthographique « o » qui est surmonté du double accent grave et du double accent aigu (c'est-à-dire ; ȍ /oo˩˩˩˩/ et ő /o::/) utilisé pour exprimer l'accent extra-bas et extra-haut du son /o/, les quatre autres orthographes portant les voyelles primaires (a, e, i et u) dont la forme logographique (en tant qu'orthographes isolées) exprime les pronoms « a » (me), « e » (you), « i » (s/he or it) et « u » (first person me) qui transmettent l'emphase et le mode, ont des sons inflexionnels montants-montants de type diérèse, l'un d'eux, le « a » combiné (ấ = /a:a:/) ayant un accent circonflexe au-dessus de lui avec un glyphe aigu coiffant l'accent circonflexe. In writing the Igala token, the ‘mid-tones’ ‘ȯ’ and ‘ė’ with respect to the sounds /eu/ and /ei/ do not always remain the same for some of these sounds could be in context short, intermediate or long in tone, meaning that up to eight varieties of these two sounds can be gotten but in the main time, the author could only come about four glyphs for writing them, that being ė = /ei/, ǡ = /eiei/, ȯ = /eu/, and ȱ = /eueu/. 2.3 Elision and other forms of Vowel Combination

2.3 Élision et autres formes de combinaison de voyelles

Since the Igala language ‘somewhat’ follows the ‘French Lemon Rule’ or the VCV (vowel – consonant – vowel) Rule for it is rare to have situation where vowels follow themselves concurrently during words formation. Even in one instance where this rule is broken as shall be demonstrated below, this is more the result of an elision of a corresponding consonant sound than a naturally fixed format for that word. The only known exception to this rule is in respect to the word ābū which means ‘how’ or ‘what’, or where’, in which case, speakers tend to deliberately elicit the /b/ consonant or both the /b/ and and the succeeding /u/ vowels during communication for only the vowel /a/, or /a/ with /u/ in respect to the word mà (them) is educed so that when succeeded with the suffices ū (I or me), ē (you), ā (we), ī (she/he/it), and mà (them), the word becomes sounded as ā’ū (how or what did I, as in the sentence “ā’ū kā?” (how or what did I say), ā’ē (how or what did you, as in the sentence “ā’ē mā?” (how do you know), ā’ā (how or what did we, as in the statement, “ā’ā chė?” (how or what did we do), ā’ī (how or where did she/he/it, as in the sentence, “ā’ī lė?” (how or where did she/he/it go), and ā’ū mà or ā’mà (how or what do they, as in the sentence “ā’ū mà kō” or “ā’mā kō?” (What or how did they write?). Although, these other forms are used as substitute for ābū, they don’t however constitute a better alternative to ābū which is the actual form of how the word should be used for these are only corrupt versions of that word ābū.

Étant donné que la langue igala suit « en quelque sorte » la « règle du citron français » ou la règle VCV (voyelle – consonne – voyelle), car il est rare d'avoir une situation où des voyelles se suivent simultanément lors de la formation des mots. Même dans un cas où cette règle est brisée, comme cela sera démontré ci-dessous, cela est plus le résultat d'une élision d'une consonne correspondante que d'un format naturellement fixe pour ce mot. La seule exception connue à cette règle concerne le mot ābū qui signifie « comment » ou « quoi », ou « où », auquel cas les locuteurs ont tendance à élider délibérément la consonne /b/ ou à la fois la consonne /b/ et la voyelle /u/ qui suit pendant la communication, car seule la voyelle /a/, ou /a/ avec /u/ pour le mot mà (eux) est produite, de sorte que lorsqu'il est suivi des suffixes ū (je ou moi), ē (tu), ā (nous), ī (elle/il/cela), et mà (eux), le mot se prononce ā'ū (comment ou quoi ai-je, comme dans la phrase « ā'ū kā ? » (comment ou quoi ai-je dit ?)), ā'ē (comment ou quoi as-tu, comme dans la phrase « ā'ē mā ? » (comment sais-tu ?)), ā'ā (comment ou quoi avons-nous, comme dans l'énoncé « ā'ā chė ? » (comment ou quoi avons-nous fait ?)), ā'ī (comment ou où a-t-elle/il/cela, comme dans la phrase « ā'ī lė ? » (comment ou où est-elle/il/cela allé(e) ?)), et ā'ū mà ou ā'mà (comment ou quoi font-ils/elles, comme dans la phrase « ā'ū mà kō » ou « ā'mā kō ? » (Quoi ou comment ont-ils/elles écrit ?)). Bien que ces autres formes soient utilisées comme substitut à ābū, elles ne constituent cependant pas une meilleure alternative à ābū qui est la forme réelle de la manière dont le mot doit être utilisé, car ce ne sont que des versions corrompues de ce mot ābū.

More also, when writing sentences in the Igala language, certain letters are omitted from some words to avoid creepiness, muddiness or jumpiness and in the place of these omitted alphabets the apostrophe is used as a way of showing these elisions. The hyphen can be used to write certain words but for a writer who has a better understanding of the parts of speech, the use of the hyphen becomes almost unnecessary, but rather than committing the error of agglutinating the words or creating ambiguity problems in word usage as was observed by Malema et al. (2020), with a better understanding of inherent speech parts, it would have been preferable to have these words hyphenated. Since vowels are not reechoed, the use of the umlaut or dieresis as is common with some Northern European languages or English is not common since there was no need for any two vowels to be cluttered to be vocalized. More so, since there are currently no homegrown phonemic orthographies at the moment or because the development of a standardized version of the Igala language is still an evolving process, there is the need to check against the practice in the English language sound system where vowels could produce multiple sounds or where certain sounds are repeated even when the repetition is almost nearly unnecessary, as one will have with the repetition of the letter ‘l’ in the word ‘ball’, or the repetition of the ‘g’ in ‘egg’.

De plus, lors de la rédaction de phrases en langue igala, certaines lettres sont omises dans certains mots pour éviter un effet de lourdeur, de confusion ou de saccade, et à la place de ces alphabets omis, l'apostrophe est utilisée comme moyen de montrer ces élisions. Le trait d'union peut être utilisé pour écrire certains mots, mais pour un écrivain qui a une meilleure compréhension des parties du discours, l'utilisation du trait d'union devient presque superflue. Cependant, plutôt que de commettre l'erreur d'agglutination des mots ou de créer des problèmes d'ambiguïté dans l'utilisation des mots, comme l'ont observé Malema et al. (2020), avec une meilleure compréhension des parties inhérentes du discours, il aurait été préférable d'avoir ces mots reliés par un trait d'union. Comme les voyelles ne sont pas redoublées, l'utilisation du tréma ou de la diérèse, comme c'est le cas pour certaines langues d'Europe du Nord ou l'anglais, n'est pas courante, car il n'était pas nécessaire que deux voyelles soient regroupées pour être vocalisées. De plus, puisque, actuellement, il n'existe pas d'orthographes phonémiques indigènes ou parce que le développement d'une version standardisée de la langue igala est encore un processus en évolution, il est nécessaire de se prémunir contre la pratique dans le système sonore de la langue anglaise où les voyelles peuvent produire plusieurs sons ou où certains sons sont répétés même lorsque la répétition est presque inutile, comme on le verrait avec la répétition de la lettre « l » dans le mot « ball », ou la répétition du « g » dans « egg ».

In English, letters as used in certain words do not usually follow a uniform pattern, i.e., letter ‘a’ (which could sound as /ei/ as in ‘cake’ or /æ/ as in ‘can’ /kæn/, or /o:/ as in ball and as in /a:/ in ‘cart’), letter ‘e’ (which could sound as /e/ as in ‘end’ or /i/ as in ‘elastic’ or ‘penis’), letter ‘i’ (which could pronounced as /i/ as in ink or /ai/ as in kite), letter ‘o’ (which could sound like /o/ as in on or /o:/ as in off, and as /u/ or /u:/ as in oops or ooze when repeated or as /Ʌ/, /o/, or /u:/ when letter ‘u’ comes immediately in front of letter ‘o’ in a spelling as we have in the words coupling or thought or coup), letter ‘u’ (which could be pronounced as /u/ as in book or /u:/ as in boom or /Ʌ/ as in umbrella, or as /3:/ as in urban when r is placed after letter u). Like the Hungarian language or other languages that do not depend exclusively on the use of the plain alphabet but employs some measure of diacritic to widen the range of available alphabets so as to accommodate more letters that are produced distinctly, while writing the Igala language, we must not necessarily change the sound of a monophony by that mere rule that some words having similar spelling be pronounced differently, neither are additional phoneme added to a vowel or a consonant as a way of changing the sound that the carrying letter is supposed to produce into another phonetic sound as we have with the English words plough or bought, and fort and resort. This problem of pronunciation could pose some headache to a new learner of the English language.

En anglais, les lettres utilisées dans certains mots ne suivent généralement pas un schéma uniforme, c'est-à-dire que la lettre ‘a’ (qui pourrait se prononcer /ei/ comme dans ‘cake’ ou /æ/ comme dans ‘can’ /kæn/, ou /o:/ comme dans ball et comme dans /a:/ dans ‘cart’), la lettre ‘e’ (qui pourrait se prononcer /e/ comme dans ‘end’ ou /i/ comme dans ‘elastic’ ou ‘penis’), la lettre ‘i’ (qui pourrait se prononcer /i/ comme dans ink ou /ai/ comme dans kite), la lettre ‘o’ (qui pourrait se prononcer comme /o/ comme dans on ou /o:/ comme dans off, et comme /u/ ou /u:/ comme dans oops ou ooze lorsqu'elle est répétée ou comme /Ʌ/, /o/, ou /u:/ lorsque la lettre ‘u’ vient immédiatement avant la lettre ‘o’ dans une orthographe comme nous le voyons dans les mots coupling ou thought ou coup), la lettre ‘u’ (qui pourrait se prononcer /u/ comme dans book ou /u:/ comme dans boom ou /Ʌ/ comme dans umbrella, ou comme /3:/ comme dans urban lorsque r est placé après la lettre u). Comme la langue hongroise ou d'autres langues qui ne dépendent pas exclusivement de l'utilisation de l'alphabet simple mais emploient une certaine mesure de diacritique pour élargir la gamme des alphabets disponibles afin d'accueillir plus de lettres qui sont produites distinctement, lors de l'écriture de la langue Igala, nous ne devons pas nécessairement changer le son d'une monophonie par cette simple règle que certains mots ayant une orthographe similaire soient prononcés différemment, ni ajouter des phonèmes supplémentaires à une voyelle ou à une consonne comme moyen de changer le son que la lettre porteuse est censée produire en un autre son phonétique comme nous le voyons avec les mots anglais plough ou bought, et fort et resort. Ce problème de prononciation pourrait poser quelques maux de tête à un nouvel apprenant de la langue anglaise.

3 Conclusion

3 Conclusion

This paper provides an overview of vowels used in the Igala language. Vowels are central to word formation and without them speech making becomes impossible. These vowels combine with the consonant sound to form words but this is not to say that without the consonant sounds, certain words cannot be verbalized or written as in the case of the Igala language where certain words can be written without the combination of consonant sounds with the consonant sounds as it applies to the four vowels, a, e. i, u which by standing alone means something. This is largely the result of the fact that, the Igala language has a wide ranging tone system.

Cet article présente un aperçu des voyelles utilisées dans la langue Igala. Les voyelles sont centrales dans la formation des mots et sans elles, la production de la parole devient impossible. Ces voyelles se combinent avec le son consonne pour former des mots, mais cela ne veut pas dire que sans les sons consonnes, certains mots ne peuvent pas être verbalisés ou écrits comme dans le cas de la langue Igala où certains mots peuvent être écrits sans la combinaison de sons consonnes avec les sons consonnes comme cela s'applique aux quatre voyelles, a, e. i, u qui, en restant seul, signifie quelque chose. Cela est largement le résultat du fait que la langue Igala possède un système de tons très étendu.

4 Limitation of the Study

4 Limitation de l'étude

As an under-resourced indigenous language whose contextual study as a culture transmitting agent is relatively new (Ojoajogwu, 2017), approaches towards formalization of the Igala language in a more modern sense has been done scantily but also indiscriminately in ways that are devoid of any serious scientific consideration, more so that those who made attempt at writing the language are either not language specialist who understand scientific rudiments regarding the rules of language as it relates with grammar and tone pattern and structure, or were individuals who were too hasty in a way that deep thinking regarding the depth and breadth of both grammar and tones contained in the language were not attended to at great depth. It was therefore not surprising that among some of the available materials that contain words, sentences and meanings, one often see variations in diacritic forms used to demonstrate tones and pronunciation from scholar to scholar and in some cases certain diacritic orthographies that can represent some tones are left out. Added to this is also a variation with respect to accent and tribal differences in tones from one area to the other which has further influence the work output of the various writers. This factor therefore contribute to the first limitation of this very study, because understanding the inherent flaws regarding the inappropriate diacritic orthographies adopted by other writers, the very writer developed separate tools representing several tones, which while being scientifically logical, nonetheless, confine exclusively not to any previous methods used by the earlier writers.

En tant que langue autochtone sous-dotée dont l'étude contextuelle en tant qu'agent de transmission culturelle est relativement nouvelle (Ojoajogwu, 2017), les approches de formalisation de la langue igala dans un sens plus moderne ont été menées de manière parcimonieuse mais aussi indiscriminée, d'une façon dépourvue de toute considération scientifique sérieuse, d'autant plus que ceux qui ont tenté d'écrire la langue ne sont soit pas des spécialistes de la langue qui comprennent les rudiments scientifiques concernant les règles de la langue en ce qui a trait à la grammaire et à la structure et au patron des tons, soit étaient des individus trop pressés, de sorte qu'une réflexion approfondie sur la profondeur et l'étendue de la grammaire et des tons contenus dans la langue n'a pas été menée en grande profondeur. Il n'était donc pas surprenant que parmi certains des matériaux disponibles qui contiennent des mots, des phrases et des significations, on voit souvent des variations dans les formes diacritiques utilisées pour démontrer les tons et la prononciation d'un érudit à l'autre et dans certains cas certaines orthographes diacritiques qui peuvent représenter certains tons sont laissées de côté. S'ajoute à cela également une variation en ce qui concerne l'accent et les différences tribales de tons d'une zone à l'autre qui a encore influencé le travail des différents auteurs. Ce facteur contribue donc à la première limitation de cette étude même, car en comprenant les défauts inhérents concernant les orthographes diacritiques inappropriées adoptées par les autres auteurs, l'auteur lui-même a développé des outils séparés représentant plusieurs tons, qui, bien que scientifiquement logiques, ne se confinent néanmoins exclusivement à aucune des méthodes précédentes utilisées par les auteurs antérieurs.

Although, prior to this current work, no effort was made to study the oral form of the Igala language from the basic in a more comprehensive form, such as is done in this paper, a fact which accounted for the neglect of some vowel forms represented by special symbols as stated above. But vowels were not the only orthographies left out for even consonant orthographies that present a single symbol as a unit of consonant cluster such as the ‘c’ with an over dot, i.e.; ċ, or the caron or hacek as it is at times called; č, both of whom represent the sound /ch/ in other world languages, as with respect to the English word ‘church’, and the symbol ŋ which is supposed to represent a single unit of the cluster /ng/ as in the word ‘ānyīngā’ (finger nail) which ought to have been ‘āɲīŋā’ or the ‘ny’ cluster (ɲ) is hardly used. While the non-use of these orthographies by previous writer posed some limitation with respect to how this very writer would have written syllables that should carry these orthographies, it was also a limitation of this study because this very writer failed to do otherwise from what has been held down for so long owing to English influence on the writing of the Igala language. While the tilde ñ which bear the /nn/ cluster and which is occasionally used by some of the earlier writers, albeit with some misconception here and there, in this work, words boiling around this very sign were not used and for that reason, the sign was bypassed, but in future attempt, it shall be treated with special consideration.

Bien que, avant ce travail actuel, aucun effort n'a été fait pour étudier la forme orale de la langue Igala à partir de la base sous une forme plus complète, comme cela est fait dans cet article, un fait qui explique la négligence de certaines formes voyelles représentées par des symboles spéciaux comme indiqué ci-dessus. Mais les voyelles n’étaient pas les seules orthographes laissées de côté, même pour les orthographes consonantiques qui présentent un seul symbole comme une unité de groupe consonantique, comme le ‘c’ avec un point, i.e.; ċ, ou le caron ou hacek comme il est parfois appelé; č, qui représentent tous deux le son /ch/ dans d’autres langues du monde, comme par rapport au mot anglais ‘church’, et le symbole ŋ qui est censé représenter une seule unité du groupe /ng/ comme dans le mot ‘ānyīngā’ (ongle du doigt) qui aurait dû être ‘āɲīŋā’ ou le groupe ‘ny’ (ɲ) est à peine utilisé. Bien que la non-utilisation de ces orthographes par l'auteur précédent ait posé une certaine limitation quant à la façon dont cet auteur aurait écrit des syllabes qui devraient porter ces orthographes, c'était aussi une limitation de cette étude parce que cet auteur même n'a pas fait autrement de ce qui a été retenu si longtemps en raison de l'influence anglaise sur l'écriture de la langue Igala. Abstract

The aim of this article is to provide some insight into the link between diacritic orthography and the implicit sounds in these orthographies that are applied in writing the Igala Language corpus. 30 vowels were identified (5 short vowels, and 25 mid to long vowels of different variety) plus 8 diphthongs. 2 Vowels and Words in Igala Language

2 Irabu na Maneno katika Lugha ya Kiigala

The Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary of English defined lexicon as; “all the words and phrases used in a particular language or subject; all the words and phrases used and known by a particular person or group of people” (Hornby, 2015). With respect to the Igala as with other languages, lexical formation begins with conjoining strings of phones or alphabets to form morphemes from which words are created. Currently, no known homemade or standard alphabetical or orthographical system exists for writing the Igala language except for the esoteric signs and markings; usually on the floor, used by traditional witchdoctors, which is not known to the vast majority of the people and that also did not serve formal purposes within society. In the midst of this foibles induced by this lack of inbred phonemes, writers beginning from the modern era, are known to either select the Latin phonetic symbols or the Arabic phonemes to replicate or transcribe words of Igala origin, what is at times referred to in West Africa as a’jami or ajami (Mc Laughlin, 2017).

Kamusi ya Oxford Advanced Learner's ya Kiingereza ilifafanua leksimu kama; “maneno na vishazi vyote vinavyotumika katika lugha au somo fulani; maneno na vishazi vyote vinavyotumika na kujulikana na mtu au kikundi fulani cha watu” (Hornby, 2015). Kuhusiana na Kiigala kama ilivyo kwa lugha nyingine, uundaji wa kileksika huanza kwa kuunganisha mifuatano ya sauti au alfabeti ili kuunda mofimu ambazo kutokana nazo maneno huundwa. Kwa sasa, hakuna mfumo wa kialfabeti au wa tahajia wa kujitengenezea au sanifu unaojulikana wa kuandika lugha ya Kiigala isipokuwa ishara na alama za kiesoteriki; kwa kawaida sakafuni, zinazotumiwa na waganga wa kienyeji, ambazo hazijulikani kwa idadi kubwa ya watu na ambazo pia hazikutumikia malengo rasmi ndani ya jamii. Katikati ya udhaifu huu unaosababishwa na ukosefu huu wa fonimu asilia, waandishi kuanzia enzi ya kisasa, wanajulikana kwa kuchagua alama za kifonetiki za Kilatini au fonimu za Kiarabu ili kunakili au kufasili maneno yenye asili ya Kiigala, jambo ambalo wakati mwingine hurejelewa Afrika Magharibi kama a'jami au ajami (Mc Laughlin, 2017).

Notwithstanding the fact that Igala is a tone-based language (Rodriguez, 2014; Dawson et al. 2015; Adeniyi, 2016), while attempting to study the structure of the Igala language, most of the attention of scholars has been on parts of speech, without much effort been dedicated towards tones and sounds with the result that without a prior knowledge of the language words written are hard to pronounce, in some cases not even with the English translation of these provided, and as for those categories of writers who tried to express tones using diacritic orthographies, without a prior knowledge of diacritic by the readers, it is still hard for the readers to decipher what was written, besides, through occasional failures of writers to provide double slash transcription boxes or oral parenthesis of sounds represented by orthographic symbols in their works, the fact that these symbols produce certain sounds from place to place and from one writer to the other makes it very hard for readers to grasp with the sound frame being inferred by these writers.

Pamoja na ukweli kwamba Kiigala ni lugha yenye msingi wa toni (Rodriguez, 2014; Dawson et al. 2015; Adeniyi, 2016), wakati wa kujaribu kuchunguza muundo wa lugha ya Kiigala, umakini mwingi wa wasomi umekuwa kwenye aina za maneno, bila juhudi nyingi kuelekezwa kwenye toni na sauti na matokeo yake ni kwamba bila ujuzi wa awali wa lugha maneno yaliyoandikwa ni magumu kutamka, katika baadhi ya matukio hata kwa tafsiri ya Kiingereza ya haya kutolewa, na kuhusu zile kategoria za waandishi waliojaribu kueleza toni kwa kutumia tahajia za diakritiki, bila ujuzi wa awali wa diakritiki kwa wasomaji, bado ni vigumu kwa wasomaji kufasiri kilichoandikwa, zaidi ya hayo, kupitia kushindwa mara kwa mara kwa waandishi kutoa visanduku vya unukuzi wa mshazari mara mbili au mabano ya mdomo ya sauti zinazowakilishwa na alama za tahajia katika kazi zao, ukweli kwamba alama hizi hutoa sauti fulani kutoka sehemu moja hadi nyingine na kutoka mwandishi mmoja hadi mwingine hufanya iwe vigumu sana kwa wasomaji kufahamu mfumo wa sauti unaodokezwa na waandishi hawa.

Abraham (2017; 2019) as well as, Emah and Ojonugwa (2020) while using diacritic orthography to write words, partly to show how differences in pitch change word meanings as was once observed by Yip (2002), failed to provide double slash transcription of the sounds represented by the orthographic symbols incorporated. Kigalaonline.wordpress.com2 made an attempt that came close to remedying the above stated shortcomings but ended up identifying just three form each for the five main vowels and two supplementary vowels (ó and é to sound /ó:/ and /é:/ [Sic] - or as in the more commonly recognized phonological form /eu/ and /ei/), which gave only seventeen vowels at the expense of the 30 vowels + 8 diphthongs recognized in this very paper. This shortcoming is not limited to the Igala language alone. Other likewise languages that are still plodding around the threshold of traditional education by the start of the 20th Century, mostly in respect of the low-resourced language groups in Africa are also faced with this very problem, a development Ken Lodge, observed thus:

Abraham (2017; 2019) vilevile, Emah na Ojonugwa (2020) wakati wa kutumia tahajia ya diakritiki kuandika maneno, kwa sehemu kuonyesha jinsi tofauti za mwinuko wa sauti hubadilisha maana za maneno kama ilivyowahi kuzingatiwa na Yip (2002), walishindwa kutoa unukuzi wa mshazari mara mbili wa sauti zinazowakilishwa na alama za tahajia zilizojumuishwa. Kigalaonline.wordpress.com2 ilifanya jaribio lililokaribia kurekebisha mapungufu yaliyotajwa hapo juu lakini ikaishia kutambua aina tatu tu kwa kila moja ya vokali kuu tano na vokali mbili za ziada (ó na é kusikika /ó:/ na /é:/ [Sic] - au kama ilivyo katika umbo la kifonolojia linalotambulika zaidi /eu/ na /ei/), jambo ambalo lilitoa vokali kumi na saba tu kwa gharama ya vokali 30 + diphthongu 8 zinazotambuliwa katika makala haya haswa. Upungufu huu haukomei kwa lugha ya Kiigala pekee. Lugha nyingine kama hizo ambazo bado zinakwama kwenye kizingiti cha elimu ya jadi kufikia mwanzo wa Karne ya 20, hasa kuhusiana na makundi ya lugha zenye rasilimali chache barani Afrika pia zinakabiliwa na tatizo hili haswa, jambo ambalo Ken Lodge, alilizingatia hivi:

Traditional education largely ignores spoken languages… little attention is paid to the details of speech in an objective way. We therefore need a method of describing speech in objective, verifiable terms as opposed to the lay approaches which typically describes sound as ‘‘hard’’, ‘‘soft’’, ‘‘sharp’’, and so on which can only be understood by the person using such description (Lodge, 2009).

Elimu ya kimapokeo kwa kiasi kikubwa inapuuza lugha zinazozungumzwa… umakini mdogo hulipwa kwa maelezo ya usemi kwa njia isiyoegemea upande wowote. Kwa hiyo tunahitaji mbinu ya kuelezea usemi kwa maneno yasiyoegemea upande wowote na yanayoweza kuthibitishwa, tofauti na mikabala ya kawaida ambayo kwa kawaida huelezea sauti kama ''ngumu'', ''laini'', ''kali'', na kadhalika, ambayo inaweza tu kueleweka na mtu anayetumia maelezo hayo (Lodge, 2009).

In the context of this particular paper thus, Latin alphabets were adopted for fashioning Igala sounds, words, and phrasal forms using diacritic symbols bearing phonemic modes similar to Blench (2011). All the English phonemes were adopted except the letters, Q, V, S, X, and Z which were discarded consonants for lack of relevance as it is with Yoruba (Kasali et al. 2021) while the letter ‘c’ is applied only in combination with the letter ‘h’ to produce the digraph /t∫/ that is used for writing words such as ūchōnā (somebody’s name meaning ‘creator’ or just ‘the creator’ in a more general sense of the word), as can be had with English words as chicken and kitchen. More also, the letter ‘c’ is not used in writing /k/ sounding words that are often written with the letter ‘c’ such as can /kæn/, neither can it be devoted to writing a sound /s/ bearing word such as ceiling for this would have been sílīni /si:lini/ or actually chílīni /t∫i:lini/ because of the still the more total absence of the ‘s’ consonant within the Igala lexical system. Similarly, rather than write the word quantity with the letter ‘q’, in our own Igala orthography, the spelling of quantity would appear as ‘kwontity’.

Katika muktadha wa makala hii hasa, alfabeti za Kilatini zilipitishwa kwa ajili ya kuunda sauti za Kiigala, maneno, na miundo ya vishazi kwa kutumia alama za diakritiki zenye mifumo ya kifonimu sawa na Blench (2011). Fonimu zote za Kiingereza zilipitishwa isipokuwa herufi, Q, V, S, X, na Z ambazo zilitupiliwa mbali kama konsonanti kwa kukosa umuhimu kama ilivyo kwa Kiyoruba (Kasali et al. 2021) wakati herufi 'c' inatumika tu kwa kuunganishwa na herufi 'h' ili kutoa digrafu /t∫/ ambayo hutumika kuandika maneno kama vile ūchōnā (jina la mtu linalomaanisha 'muumba' au 'muumba' tu kwa maana pana zaidi ya neno), kama inavyoweza kupatikana kwa maneno ya Kiingereza kama chicken na kitchen. Zaidi ya hayo, herufi 'c' haitumiki katika kuandika maneno yenye sauti /k/ ambayo mara nyingi huandikwa kwa herufi 'c' kama vile can /kæn/, wala haiwezi kutumika kuandika neno lenye sauti /s/ kama vile ceiling kwa kuwa hii ingekuwa sílīni /si:lini/ au kwa kweli chílīni /t∫i:lini/ kwa sababu ya kutokuwepo kabisa kwa konsonanti 's' ndani ya mfumo wa kileksika wa Kiigala. Vivyo hivyo, badala ya kuandika neno quantity kwa herufi 'q', katika tahajia yetu ya Kiigala, tahajia ya quantity ingeonekana kama 'kwontity'.

Writing vowels during word formation often involves the embracement of any one out of some three alternative styles. The first is the exclusive use of plain alphabets in which case only vowels that are unadorned with glyphs would be put to use. This first form does not embody the use of diacritic in any way and if vowel are to be crafted, that would depend upon the efficacious amalgam of plain alphabets to produce words that are not delineated in the existing letter ordering as in the spelling of the word Igalaa in wiktionary.org in which case the underlined double ‘a’ in that word Igalaa does not merely suggest a long vowel (i.e.; /a:/) but a repetition of the vowels as /a/ + /a/ to give a sound much like a gliding ‘are are’. The second form of these on-hand modes which this author here assume to be actually impractical depend exclusively on the use of diacritic, if not in writing the plain orthographic characters but in the scrawling of the oral parenthesis of words as in the Webster’s II New College Dictionary. The third style involves the commixing of the other two diametrical forms, i.e.; the adoption of both the plain vowels and the diacritic–based vowels. Any of these three forms is cogent enough provided they goose-step along the conventional morphophonemic norms. It is the third form of these approaches-that of mixing plain Latin alphabet with Latin diacritic symbols, that would be utilized in this particular work.

Kuandika vokali wakati wa uundaji wa maneno mara nyingi huhusisha kupitishwa kwa mojawapo kati ya mitindo mitatu mbadala. Ya kwanza ni matumizi ya kipekee ya alfabeti za kawaida ambapo ni vokali tu ambazo hazijapambwa kwa glifi ndizo zingetumika. Aina hii ya kwanza haijumuishi matumizi ya diakritiki kwa namna yoyote na ikiwa irabu zitatengenezwa, hilo litategemea mchanganyiko mwafaka wa alfabeti za kawaida ili kuzalisha maneno ambayo hayajaainishwa katika mpangilio wa herufi uliopo kama ilivyo katika tahajia ya neno Igalaa katika wiktionary.org ambapo ‘a' mbili zilizopigiwa mstari katika neno hilo Igalaa hazipendekezi tu irabu ndefu (yaani; /a:/) bali urudiaji wa irabu kama /a/ + /a/ kutoa sauti inayofanana sana na 'are are' inayoteleza. Aina ya pili ya njia hizi zilizopo ambazo mwandishi huyu hapa anadhani kuwa kwa kweli haziwezekani inategemea kabisa matumizi ya diakritiki, ikiwa si katika kuandika herufi za kawaida za tahajia basi katika ukwaruzaji wa mabano ya mdomo ya maneno kama ilivyo katika kamusi ya Webster's II New College Dictionary. Mtindo wa tatu unahusisha mchanganyiko wa aina zile nyingine mbili zinazopingana kabisa, yaani; upokeaji wa irabu za kawaida na irabu zenye msingi wa diakritiki. Yoyote kati ya aina hizi tatu inatosha kushawishi mradi tu zinafuata kwa uthabiti kanuni za kawaida za mofofonemia. Ni aina ya tatu ya mbinu hizi-ile ya kuchanganya alfabeti ya Kilatini ya kawaida na alama za matamshi za Kilatini, ndiyo itakayotumika katika kazi hii mahususi.

In the oral parenthesis provided in the Webster’s II New College Dictionary and thesaraus.yourdictionary.com, short vowels were represented with the application of the breve- a form of diacritic that is presented like the lower half of a cycle above the vowels, i.e.; ă, ĕ, ĭ, ŏ, ŭ, but in writing this work, rather than to vent these letters using the breve, they were written just as plain as possible, i.e.; ‘a, e, i, o, u’. Be that said, the other point of departure concern the sounds the other diacritic orthographies used by these esteem dictionaries produced with respect to our own keys here. In the Webster’s II New College Dictionary (1999), the macron was adopted to produce the unique form of the long vowels ō ∼ /eu/ as in the word old /ōld/ in much of a respect used by Dawson et al. (2015) and ā ∼ as in the word said /sād/, but in this work, moved by the peculiarity of the Igala language which partially sets it apart from English and the prima facie claim by Wells (2001) that the macron is ratified for initiating a middle locus between the short vowels and the long vowels.

Katika mabano ya mdomo yaliyotolewa katika kamusi ya Webster's II New College Dictionary na thesaraus.yourdictionary.com, irabu fupi ziliwakilishwa kwa kutumia breve—aina ya diakritiki inayowasilishwa kama nusu ya chini ya duara juu ya irabu, yaani; ă, ĕ, ĭ, ŏ, ŭ, lakini katika kuandika kazi hii, badala ya kutoa herufi hizi kwa kutumia breve, ziliandikwa kwa usahili iwezekanavyo, yaani; ‘a, e, i, o, u'. Hilo likiwa limesemwa, jambo lingine la kutofautiana linahusu sauti ambazo tahajia nyingine za diakritiki zilizotumiwa na kamusi hizi zinazoheshimika zilizalisha kuhusiana na funguo zetu wenyewe hapa. Katika Webster's II New College Dictionary (1999), macron ilipitishwa ili kuzalisha aina ya kipekee ya irabu ndefu ō ∼ /eu/ kama ilivyo katika neno old /ōld/ kwa kiasi kikubwa kama ilivyotumiwa na Dawson et al. (2015) na ā ∼ kama ilivyo katika neno said /sād/, lakini katika kazi hii, kwa kuchochewa na upekee wa lugha ya Igala ambao kwa kiasi fulani unaitenganisha na Kiingereza na dai la awali la Wells (2001) kwamba macron imeidhinishwa kwa kuanzisha eneo la kati kati ya irabu fupi na irabu ndefu.

Macrons were therefore used in this work, not for the production of the ā = /ei/ and ō = /eu/ sounds for these are in this work illustrated by the use of the letters ‘o’ and ‘e’ with dots on top of them (i.e.; ȯ = /eu/ and ė = /ei/). Although, there are a few improvements lately, most of the previous writings, following mainly from the recommendation of the 1984 Igala Orthographic Committee which recognized the under dotted ọ and ẹ for the low tones o ∼ /o˩/ | e ∼ /ee˩˩/ (i.e.; as in the spelling of ọjọ /oo˩˩joo˩˩/ in Okwoli (1996) and Ahiaba (2015), and ẹrẹ /ee˩˩ree˩˩/ in Ayegba et al. (2017) for sounding the falling /o/ and /e/ sounds, while the quartet reserved the plain forms of ‘o’ and ‘e’ alphabets for the rising and falling forms of /eu/ and /ei/ sounds as used for Yoruba in aroadedictionary.com, most of these scholars recognized only 7 vowels plus a so called 24 consonant sounds (Ojoaogwu, 2017) or 32 symbols in whole according to Miachi and Armstrong (1986), which meant they left out a number of vowels and therefore downplaying the use of the macron in the work of succeeding writers with the only exceptions being Omachonu (2011) and to an extent, Dawson et al. (2015) who either used it in much of a respect as done in this paper or as was done in the Webster’s II New College Dictionary.

Kwa hiyo makroni zilitumika katika kazi hii, si kwa ajili ya utoaji wa sauti ā = /ei/ na ō = /eu/ kwa maana hizi katika kazi hii zinaonyeshwa kwa kutumia herufi ‘o' na ‘e' zenye nukta juu yake (yaani; ȯ = /eu/ na ė = /ei/). Ingawa kuna maboresho machache hivi karibuni, maandishi mengi ya awali, yakifuata hasa pendekezo la Kamati ya Orthografia ya Kiigala ya 1984 ambayo ilitambua ọ na ẹ zenye nukta chini kwa toni za chini o ∼ /o˩/ | e ∼ /ee˩˩/ (yaani; kama katika tahajia ya ọjọ /oo˩˩joo˩˩/ katika Okwoli (1996) na Ahiaba (2015), na ẹrẹ /ee˩˩ree˩˩/ katika Ayegba et al. (2017) kwa ajili ya kutamka sauti zinazoshuka za /o/ na /e/, wakati kundi la wanne lilihifadhi maumbo ya kawaida ya alfabeti ‘o' na ‘e' kwa ajili ya maumbo yanayopanda na kushuka ya sauti /eu/ na /ei/ kama inavyotumika kwa Kiyoruba katika aroadedictionary.com, wengi wa wasomi hawa walitambua irabu 7 pekee pamoja na kile kinachoitwa sauti 24 za konsonanti (Ojoaogwu, 2017) au alama 32 kwa jumla kulingana na Miachi na Armstrong (1986), jambo ambalo lilimaanisha waliacha idadi kadhaa ya irabu na hivyo kupunguza matumizi ya makroni katika kazi ya waandishi waliofuata isipokuwa tu Omachonu (2011) na kwa kiasi fulani, Dawson et al. (2015) ambao aidha waliitumia kwa heshima kubwa kama ilivyofanywa katika makala hii au kama ilivyofanywa katika Webster's II New College Dictionary.

Thus, the use of the macron in this article follows a model that is similar to its use by Karshima (2012) in respect to the Tiv language or in respect to the Igala word ìkpā (bag) in Rodriguez (2014); not to be confused with íkpa (knock knees), in which ā = /æ/ as in the American English pronunciation of the words ‘plan’ and ‘stand’ in oxfordlearnersdictionary.com as an intermediate tone between the sound /a/ and /a:/ of the IPA sound system, ē = /ɛ/ as in the English word ‘any’, i.e.; an intermediate tone between the sound /e/ and /e:/ of the IPA sound system) as was used in respect to the word ònēkèlē in Rodriguez (2015) which the author of this very paper would here again rewrite as ōnēkēlē (male), ī = /i:/ > /x/ > /i/, i.e.; an intermediate tone between the sound /i/ and /i:/ of the IPA sound system, ō = /ɔ/ as in the cardinal mid-back rounded ‘o’ used in sounding the English word ‘but’ or the Igala word ōkō /ɔkɔ/ in respect to airplane or ship which is in contrast to òkò /o˩koo˩˩/ (millipede) or okā /okæ/ (style, i.e.; acrobatic) or ókō /o:kɔ/ (money), with ‘ō’ being an intermediate tone between the sound /o/ and /o:/ of the IPA sound system, ū = /u:/ > /x/ > /u/ as in Lithuanian, Livonian, and Maori, i.e.; an intermediate tone between the sound /u/ and /u:/ of the IPA sound system. The x in the oral parentheses represents the indefinite phonemic sound for the diacritic orthographies ī & ū ∼ x. Decision to jettison the breve in this paper has a twofold underling. First was to ensure the design of a coherent orthographic system with pure vowels used in their original orthographic forms as it is in most English language texts, notwithstanding whatever graphical modification or characterization that the addition of impending glyphs to the first letter thenceforth might confer. The second reason propped from the need to eschew the immanent supererogatory burden of having to write them with an added character when they could remain plain or without partial alteration and still maintain their functions as short vowels.

Hivyo, matumizi ya mstari wa juu katika makala haya yanafuata mfano unaofanana na matumizi yake na Karshima (2012) kuhusu lugha ya Tiv au kuhusu neno la Igala ìkpā (begi) katika Rodriguez (2014); lisichanganywe na íkpa (miguu inayopinda ndani), ambapo ā = /æ/ kama katika matamshi ya Kiingereza cha Marekani ya maneno ‘plan' na ‘stand' katika oxfordlearnersdictionary.com kama toni ya kati kati ya sauti /a/ na /a:/ za mfumo wa sauti wa IPA, ē = /ɛ/ kama katika neno la Kiingereza ‘any', yaani; toni ya kati kati ya sauti /e/ na /e:/ za mfumo wa sauti wa IPA) kama ilivyotumika kuhusu neno ònēkèlē katika Rodriguez (2015) ambalo mwandishi wa makala haya yenyewe hapa tena angeandika upya kama ōnēkēlē (mwanamume), ī = /i:/ > /x/ > /i/, yaani; toni ya kati kati ya sauti /i/ na /i:/ za mfumo wa sauti wa IPA, ō = /ɔ/ kama katika ‘o' ya kati ya nyuma iliyozungukwa ya msingi inayotumika katika kutamka neno la Kiingereza ‘but' au neno la Igala ōkō /ɔkɔ/ kuhusu ndege au meli ambalo linatofautiana na òkò /o˩koo˩˩/ (jongoo) au okā /okæ/ (mtindo, yaani; akrobati) au ókō /o:kɔ/ (pesa), na ‘ō' ikiwa ni toni ya kati kati ya sauti /o/ na /o:/ za mfumo wa sauti wa IPA, ū = /u:/ > /x/ > /u/ kama katika Kilithuania, Kilivonia, na Kimaori, yaani; toni ya kati kati ya sauti /u/ na /u:/ za mfumo wa sauti wa IPA. X katika mabano ya mdomo inawakilisha sauti ya kifonemiki isiyojulikana kwa tahajia za diakritiki ī & ū ∼ x. Uamuzi wa kutupilia mbali breve katika makala hii una msingi wa namna mbili. Kwanza ilikuwa kuhakikisha uundaji wa mfumo thabiti wa tahajia wenye irabu safi zinazotumika katika maumbo yao asili ya tahajia kama ilivyo katika maandishi mengi ya lugha ya Kiingereza, bila kujali marekebisho yoyote ya kimaandishi au sifa ambayo nyongeza ya glifi zinazokuja kwa herufi ya kwanza tangu hapo inaweza kutoa. Sababu ya pili ilitokana na hitaji la kuepuka mzigo wa ndani wa ziada usio wa lazima wa kuziandika kwa herufi iliyoongezwa wakati zingeweza kubaki wazi au bila mabadiliko ya sehemu na bado kudumisha kazi zake kama irabu fupi.

The desuetude towards the breve as done here was not a deliberate attempt to undo the merits of the Webster’s II New College Dictionary, their discretion are still very valid, specifically with respect to the study of the English language. This writer is well abreast with the unequivocal fact that left with a vagaries of indicative keys to choose from, it is always incumbent upon each writer to cherry-pick both the phonotactical and the morphotactical forms that are amenable to the language being worked on (Pretorius and Bosch, 2009), it should however be noted that the use of ā = /ei/ and ō = /eu/, despite not being wrong in any sense, doesn’t nonetheless conforms with the original phonological flow pattern enshrined in their root alphabet ‘a’ which produces the sounds /a/ and /a:/, and ‘e’ which produces the sounds /e/ and /e:/. Bearing in mind the fact that the middle tone is sometimes perceived to be a conflation of two vowels (i.e.; /e/ + /i/ = /ei/ and /e/ + /u/ = /eu/ as it is in English), then it would not be totally wrong to say that the editors of the Webster’s II New College Dictionary were right in their own right to have used the macron in the context they employed it, besides, language analyst do agree that, ‘there is no such thing as a standard form when it comes to matters of language’ (Laperre 2020) and as Szczegielniak laconically hinted, “spelling or orthography does not consistently represent the sounds of language”. Thus, in Igala language, middle tone implies is a position between a long vowel and a short vowel such as the middle sound between the high /a:/ and the low /a/, i.e.; an ‘a’ that has a higher tone than /a/ but yet is still less pitched than /a:/ represented as x > /a/ < /a:/.

Kutotumika kuelekea breve kama ilivyofanywa hapa hakukuwa jaribio la makusudi la kubatilisha sifa za Webster's II New College Dictionary, busara zao bado ni halali sana, hasa kuhusiana na utafiti wa lugha ya Kiingereza. Mwandishi huyu anafahamu vyema ukweli usio na shaka kwamba akiachwa na uteuzi wa vifunguo vya kuashiria visivyotabirika, ni wajibu wa kila mwandishi kuchagua kwa uangalifu maumbo ya kifonotaktiki na kimofotaktiki ambayo yanakubaliana na lugha inayoshughulikiwa (Pretorius na Bosch, 2009), hata hivyo inapaswa kuzingatiwa kwamba matumizi ya ā = /ei/ na ō = /eu/, licha ya kutokuwa makosa kwa namna yoyote, hayalingani hata hivyo na mtiririko asilia wa kifonolojia uliowekwa katika alfabeti yao ya mzizi 'a' ambayo hutoa sauti /a/ na /a:/, na 'e' ambayo hutoa sauti /e/ na /e:/. Kwa kuzingatia ukweli kwamba toni ya kati wakati mwingine huchukuliwa kuwa ni muunganiko wa vokali mbili (yaani; /e/ + /i/ = /ei/ na /e/ + /u/ = /eu/ kama ilivyo katika Kiingereza), basi haingekuwa kosa kabisa kusema kwamba wahariri wa Webster's II New College Dictionary walikuwa sahihi kwa haki yao wenyewe kutumia makroni katika muktadha waliyoitumia, zaidi ya hayo, wachambuzi wa lugha wanakubali kwamba, 'hakuna kitu kama umbo sanifu linapokuja suala la mambo ya lugha' (Laperre 2020) na kama Szczegielniak alivyodokeza kwa ufupi, "tahajia haiwakilishi sauti za lugha kwa uthabiti". Hivyo, katika lugha ya Kiigala, toni ya kati ina maana ya nafasi kati ya vokali ndefu na vokali fupi kama vile sauti ya kati kati ya /a:/ ya juu na /a/ ya chini, yaani; 'a' ambayo ina toni ya juu kuliko /a/ lakini bado ina kiwango cha chini cha sauti kuliko /a:/ inayowakilishwa kama x > /a/ < /a:/.

This writer would have loved to have the alphabet ė which is hereby sounded /ei/ written as ȧ, but because most words in the language are already written with the letter ‘e’ to sound /ei/, as in words such as ugbede /ugbeidei/ or ele /eilei/, the author therefore chose to maintain the letter ė which comes closer to the now more popular tradition. The decision to use the alphabet ȧ instead of ė would have matched perfectly with the use of the over dotted ‘o’ (ȯ) to produce the sound /eu/. The use of the macron itself becomes vital considering the fact that, most words in the Igala language are sounded within this mid tone context.

Mwandishi huyu angependa alfabeti ė ambayo kwa hivyo inatamkwa /ei/ iandikwe kama ȧ, lakini kwa sababu maneno mengi katika lugha tayari yameandikwa kwa herufi 'e' kutamka /ei/, kama katika maneno kama vile ugbede /ugbeidei/ au ele /eilei/, mwandishi kwa hiyo alichagua kudumisha herufi ė ambayo inakaribia zaidi mila iliyo maarufu zaidi sasa. Uamuzi wa kutumia alfabeti ȧ badala ya ė ungepatana kikamilifu na matumizi ya 'o' yenye nukta juu (ȯ) kutoa sauti /eu/. Matumizi ya makroni yenyewe yanakuwa muhimu kwa kuzingatia ukweli kwamba, maneno mengi katika lugha ya Kiigala yanatamkwa ndani ya muktadha huu wa toni ya kati.

As in writing the Yoruba language or French (Olufemi, 2022; Nolte et al. 2018), the acute and the grave forms of diacritic were also used although with a contrastive difference in phonation between the Igala words written here using this form that is different from French as regards to the word café, in which case, the é as in the French and in the form used by Rodriguez (2014) in relation to the word ‘éʤú’ ∼ /eiʤu:/ (face), is pronounced /ei/, more like the English word rake /reik/, whereas, the orthography é as it relates with the Igala and sometimes with the Yoruba; as with the word òré (friend) in (Oshodi, 2016) ∼ /oo˩˩re:/, is nonetheless pronounced /e:/ as in English. Thus, we must agree with Cahill, (2019) that, in using diacritic tools, “the challenge is that the tone marks are usually not consistent among scholars”, with the result that, a letter é that is pronounced as /e:/ in one instance or by one scholar is pronounced at another instance or by another scholar as /ei/ as in spelling of wálé ∼ /wa:lei/ (come home) in Dalamu (2019) which is at variance with Oshodi (2016)’s /e:/ above.

Kama ilivyo katika uandishi wa lugha ya Kiyoruba au Kifaransa (Olufemi, 2022; Nolte et al. 2018), aina za akuti na gravu za alama za matamshi pia zilitumika ingawa kwa tofauti ya kinyume katika utamkaji kati ya maneno ya Kiigala yaliyoandikwa hapa kwa kutumia umbo hili ambalo ni tofauti na Kifaransa kuhusiana na neno café, katika hali hiyo, é kama ilivyo katika Kifaransa na katika umbo lililotumiwa na Rodriguez (2014) kuhusiana na neno ‘éʤú' ∼ /eiʤu:/ (uso), linatamkwa /ei/, zaidi kama neno la Kiingereza rake /reik/, ilhali, tahajia é inavyohusiana na Kiigala na wakati mwingine na Kiyoruba; kama ilivyo kwa neno òré (rafiki) katika (Oshodi, 2016) ∼ /oo˩˩re:/, hata hivyo inatamkwa /e:/ kama katika Kiingereza. Hivyo, ni lazima tukubaliane na Cahill, (2019) kwamba, katika kutumia zana za alama za matamshi, "changamoto ni kwamba alama za toni kwa kawaida hazilingani miongoni mwa wasomi", matokeo yake ni kwamba, herufi é ambayo inatamkwa kama /e:/ katika tukio moja au na msomi mmoja inatamkwa katika tukio lingine au na msomi mwingine kama /ei/ kama ilivyo katika tahajia ya wálé ∼ /wa:lei/ (njoo nyumbani) katika Dalamu (2019) ambayo inakinzana na /e:/ ya Oshodi (2016) hapo juu.

Here too, the grave accent (i.e.; à, è, ì, ò, ù) was used to indicate the low tones as was done in Yip (2002). The use of certain diacritic marks in this work might be counted as inappropriate as is now apparent with respect to how the macron is used here which is already in conflict with how it was used in the Webster’s II Dictionary or how any of these marks are represented in other languages such as French, Dutch, Portuguese, Italian, and Mohawk (Finegan, 2012).

Hapa pia, alama ya gravu (yaani; à, è, ì, ò, ù) ilitumika kuashiria toni za chini kama ilivyofanywa katika Yip (2002). Matumizi ya alama fulani za diakritiki katika kazi hii yanaweza kuhesabiwa kuwa yasiyofaa kama inavyoonekana sasa kuhusiana na jinsi makroni inavyotumika hapa ambayo tayari inakinzana na jinsi ilivyotumika katika Kamusi ya Webster's II au jinsi alama hizi zinavyowakilishwa katika lugha nyingine kama vile Kifaransa, Kiholanzi, Kireno, Kiitaliano, na Kimohawk (Finegan, 2012).

A critic might ask to know why ‘this author did not use the breve diacritic in writing the short vowels’, which has already been reflected upon above. To cover any anomaly that emanates from how certain things are represented in this work, enabling tools with instructions regarding how to pronounce the diacritic forms is provided, with the hope that the writer of this piece and the readers might be on a common wavelength in comprehending what has been put down. Hence, words having these marks which as this writer suppose might not follow the existing methods in some languages, can be pronounced; if not so perfectly, it would be ‘nearly perfect’.

Mhakiki anaweza kuuliza kujua kwa nini ‘mwandishi huyu hakutumia alama ya breve katika kuandika vokali fupi', jambo ambalo tayari limetafakariwa hapo juu. Ili kufidia hitilafu yoyote inayotokana na jinsi mambo fulani yanavyowakilishwa katika kazi hii, zana saidizi zenye maelekezo kuhusu jinsi ya kutamka maumbo ya diakritiki zimetolewa, kwa matumaini kwamba mwandishi wa kipande hiki na wasomaji wanaweza kuwa na uelewa sawa katika kufahamu kile kilichoandikwa. Kwa hiyo, maneno yenye alama hizi ambayo kama mwandishi huyu anavyodhani huenda hayafuati mbinu zilizopo katika baadhi ya lugha, yanaweza kutamkwa; ikiwa si kikamilifu kabisa, yatakuwa 'karibu kamilifu'.

The Igala vowels are still yet not limited to the above mentioned accent forms. In this study, it is revealed that some vowels are a combination of double sound of the same vowel either ways raised between short or long vowel and then pitched lowly or highly with the corresponding vowel (like a bend), as was done with respect to that closing tone in the word Igalaa. For this category of vowels, the author came up with the idea of having orthographies with macrons on top of them that are further capped with a grave or the acute diacritic but only two of the short vowels ḕ, ḗ and ṑ, ṓ could meet this requirement, and so the author thought of remedying this problem with the use of the circumflex having either the acute or the grave diacritic mark on top of them, i.e.; ấ, ầ, but only the letter ‘a’ has this feature out of the other three still missing letters conveying the short vowels (a, i. and u). The next that could be done was to check for diacritic graphemes that have the features that can best solve the ‘u’ and the ‘i’ problem (regarding this category of sound), but this author once again stumbled upon the idea of using the ‘u’ dieresis capped with either the grave or the acute mark, i.e.; ǘ and ǜ, but the ‘i’ symbols was still missing, and so, the author thought of using the Greek small letter iota with psili and varia (ἲ) and ἵ small letter iota with psili and oxia which the author found as the only current fitting option for use here.

Vokali za Kiigala bado hazijazuiliwa kwa maumbo ya lafudhi yaliyotajwa hapo juu. Katika utafiti huu, imefunuliwa kwamba baadhi ya vokali ni mchanganyiko wa sauti mbili za vokali ileile zilizoinuliwa kwa namna yoyote kati ya vokali fupi au ndefu na kisha kupandishwa au kushushwa toni kwa vokali inayolingana (kama upinde), kama ilivyofanywa kuhusiana na ile toni ya kufunga katika neno Igalaa. Kwa kategoria hii ya vokali, mwandishi alibuni wazo la kuwa na maandishi yenye makroni juu yake ambayo yamefunikwa zaidi kwa alama ya gravu au akuti lakini ni vokali fupi mbili tu ḕ, ḗ na ṑ, ṓ zilizoweza kukidhi hitaji hili, na kwa hiyo mwandishi alifikiria kurekebisha tatizo hili kwa kutumia sirkumfleksi yenye aidha alama ya akuti au gravu juu yake, yaani; ấ, ầ, lakini ni herufi 'a' pekee ndiyo yenye sifa hii kati ya herufi nyingine tatu ambazo bado hazipo zinazowasilisha vokali fupi (a, i. na u). Jambo lililofuata ambalo lingeweza kufanywa lilikuwa kuangalia grafimu zenye alama za matamshi ambazo zina sifa zinazoweza kutatua vyema tatizo la 'u' na 'i' (kuhusiana na kategoria hii ya sauti), lakini mwandishi huyu alikwama tena kwenye wazo la kutumia dieresisi ya 'u' ikiwa imefunikwa kwa alama ya grave au acute, yaani; ǘ na ǜ, lakini alama ya 'i' bado haikuwepo, na kwa hiyo, mwandishi alifikiria kutumia herufi ndogo ya Kigiriki iota yenye psili na varia (ἲ) na ἵ herufi ndogo ya iota yenye psili na oxia ambayo mwandishi aliiona kama chaguo pekee linalofaa kwa sasa kwa matumizi hapa.

2.1 Contextual Usage of Vowels

2.1 Matumizi ya Kimuktadha ya Vokali

In writing the orthographic forms of Igala vowels, one would thus have 30 of these, with each of the short vowels producing 5 variants each while from the glyph ȯ and ė can be gotten an additional 8 diphthongs. In the Webster’s II New College Dictionary, orthographic characters and phonetic sounds adhering or being inherent in the long vowels of the English language were written in the forms; /â/ or /ä/ = /a: /, /ê/ = /e: /, /ē/ = /i: /, /ô/ = /o: /, /û/ = /u: /, /ā/ = /ei/, and ō = /eu/. In this work the above 5 high tones were replicated as á = /a: /, é = /e: /, í = /i: /, ó = /o: /, ú = /u: /. Added to these long vowels are the 5 short vowels a = /a/, e = /e/, i = /i/, o = /o/, u = /u/. 2.2 Diphthongs

2.2 Irabu Pacha

In writing the Igala token, the ‘mid-tones’ ‘ȯ’ and ‘ė’ with respect to the sounds /eu/ and /ei/ do not always remain the same for some of these sounds could be in context short, intermediate or long in tone, meaning that up to eight varieties of these two sounds can be gotten but in the main time, the author could only come about four glyphs for writing them, that being ė = /ei/, ǡ = /eiei/, ȯ = /eu/, and ȱ = /eueu/. These eight sounds are generated from the first vowels /e/ + /i/ = /ei/ and /e/ + /u/ = /eu/, out of which we have 8 diphthongs, although some of these are not currently portrayed by any orthographic symbol. There would thus be the need for additional symbols, i.e.; dotted ‘ė’ or ‘ȧ’ and ȯ orthographies with a grave or acute accents placed (either ways) on top of the dots on each to sound /eieii˩˩/ and /eueuu˩˩/ or /eiei:/ and /eueu:/.

2.3 Elision and other forms of Vowel Combination

2.3 Elisioni na aina nyingine za mchanganyiko wa vokali

Since the Igala language ‘somewhat’ follows the ‘French Lemon Rule’ or the VCV (vowel – consonant – vowel) Rule for it is rare to have situation where vowels follow themselves concurrently during words formation. Even in one instance where this rule is broken as shall be demonstrated below, this is more the result of an elision of a corresponding consonant sound than a naturally fixed format for that word. The only known exception to this rule is in respect to the word ābū which means ‘how’ or ‘what’, or where’, in which case, speakers tend to deliberately elicit the /b/ consonant or both the /b/ and and the succeeding /u/ vowels during communication for only the vowel /a/, or /a/ with /u/ in respect to the word mà (them) is educed so that when succeeded with the suffices ū (I or me), ē (you), ā (we), ī (she/he/it), and mà (them), the word becomes sounded as ā’ū (how or what did I, as in the sentence “ā’ū kā?” (how or what did I say), ā’ē (how or what did you, as in the sentence “ā’ē mā?” (how do you know), ā’ā (how or what did we, as in the statement, “ā’ā chė?” (how or what did we do), ā’ī (how or where did she/he/it, as in the sentence, “ā’ī lė?” (how or where did she/he/it go), and ā’ū mà or ā’mà (how or what do they, as in the sentence “ā’ū mà kō” or “ā’mā kō?” (What or how did they write?). Although, these other forms are used as substitute for ābū, they don’t however constitute a better alternative to ābū which is the actual form of how the word should be used for these are only corrupt versions of that word ābū.

Kwa kuwa lugha ya Kiigala ‘kwa kiasi fulani' inafuata ‘Kanuni ya Limau ya Kifaransa' au Kanuni ya VCV (irabu – konsonanti – irabu) kwa sababu ni nadra kuwa na hali ambapo irabu hujifuata zenyewe kwa wakati mmoja wakati wa uundaji wa maneno. Hata katika tukio moja ambapo kanuni hii inavunjwa kama itakavyoonyeshwa hapa chini, hii ni zaidi matokeo ya elisioni ya sauti ya konsonanti inayolingana kuliko muundo uliowekwa kiasili kwa neno hilo. Isipokuwa pekee kinachojulikana kwa kanuni hii ni kuhusiana na neno ābū linalomaanisha ‘vipi' au ‘nini', au ‘wapi', ambapo, wazungumzaji huwa na mwelekeo wa kutamka kwa makusudi konsonanti /b/ au zote mbili /b/ na irabu /u/ zinazofuata wakati wa mawasiliano kwa maana irabu /a/ pekee, au /a/ pamoja na /u/ kuhusiana na neno mà (wao) hupunguzwa ili wakati inapofuatiwa na viambishi tamati ū (mimi), ē (wewe), ā (sisi), ī (yeye), na mà (wao), neno hilo linasikika kama ā'ū (vipi au nini nilifanya, kama katika sentensi “ā'ū kā?” (vipi au nini nilisema), ā'ē (vipi au nini ulifanya, kama katika sentensi “ā'ē mā?” (vipi unajua), ā'ā (vipi au nini tulifanya, kama katika kauli, “ā'ā chė?” (vipi au nini tulifanya), ā'ī (vipi au wapi alifanya, kama katika sentensi, “ā'ī lė?” (vipi au wapi alienda), na ā'ū mà au ā'mà (vipi au nini wanafanya, kama katika sentensi “ā'ū mà kō” au “ā'mā kō?” (Nini au vipi waliandika?). Ingawa, aina hizi nyingine hutumika kama mbadala wa ābū, hata hivyo hazijumuishi mbadala bora zaidi wa ābū ambao ndio umbo halisi la jinsi neno hilo linavyopaswa kutumika kwa maana hizi ni matoleo potovu tu ya neno hilo ābū.

More also, when writing sentences in the Igala language, certain letters are omitted from some words to avoid creepiness, muddiness or jumpiness and in the place of these omitted alphabets the apostrophe is used as a way of showing these elisions. The hyphen can be used to write certain words but for a writer who has a better understanding of the parts of speech, the use of the hyphen becomes almost unnecessary, but rather than committing the error of agglutinating the words or creating ambiguity problems in word usage as was observed by Malema et al. (2020), with a better understanding of inherent speech parts, it would have been preferable to have these words hyphenated. Since vowels are not reechoed, the use of the umlaut or dieresis as is common with some Northern European languages or English is not common since there was no need for any two vowels to be cluttered to be vocalized. More so, since there are currently no homegrown phonemic orthographies at the moment or because the development of a standardized version of the Igala language is still an evolving process, there is the need to check against the practice in the English language sound system where vowels could produce multiple sounds or where certain sounds are repeated even when the repetition is almost nearly unnecessary, as one will have with the repetition of the letter ‘l’ in the word ‘ball’, or the repetition of the ‘g’ in ‘egg’.

In English, letters as used in certain words do not usually follow a uniform pattern, i.e., letter ‘a’ (which could sound as /ei/ as in ‘cake’ or /æ/ as in ‘can’ /kæn/, or /o:/ as in ball and as in /a:/ in ‘cart’), letter ‘e’ (which could sound as /e/ as in ‘end’ or /i/ as in ‘elastic’ or ‘penis’), letter ‘i’ (which could pronounced as /i/ as in ink or /ai/ as in kite), letter ‘o’ (which could sound like /o/ as in on or /o:/ as in off, and as /u/ or /u:/ as in oops or ooze when repeated or as /Ʌ/, /o/, or /u:/ when letter ‘u’ comes immediately in front of letter ‘o’ in a spelling as we have in the words coupling or thought or coup), letter ‘u’ (which could be pronounced as /u/ as in book or /u:/ as in boom or /Ʌ/ as in umbrella, or as /3:/ as in urban when r is placed after letter u). Like the Hungarian language or other languages that do not depend exclusively on the use of the plain alphabet but employs some measure of diacritic to widen the range of available alphabets so as to accommodate more letters that are produced distinctly, while writing the Igala language, we must not necessarily change the sound of a monophony by that mere rule that some words having similar spelling be pronounced differently, neither are additional phoneme added to a vowel or a consonant as a way of changing the sound that the carrying letter is supposed to produce into another phonetic sound as we have with the English words plough or bought, and fort and resort. This problem of pronunciation could pose some headache to a new learner of the English language.

3 Conclusion

3 Hitimisho

This paper provides an overview of vowels used in the Igala language. Vowels are central to word formation and without them speech making becomes impossible. These vowels combine with the consonant sound to form words but this is not to say that without the consonant sounds, certain words cannot be verbalized or written as in the case of the Igala language where certain words can be written without the combination of consonant sounds with the consonant sounds as it applies to the four vowels, a, e. i, u which by standing alone means something. This is largely the result of the fact that, the Igala language has a wide ranging tone system.

Makala hii inatoa muhtasari wa vokali zinazotumika katika lugha ya Igala. Vokali ni msingi wa uundaji wa maneno na bila hizo utungaji wa usemi unakuwa hauwezekani. Vokali hizi huchangamana na sauti ya konsonanti kuunda maneno lakini hii haimaanishi kwamba bila sauti za konsonanti, maneno fulani hayawezi kutamkwa au kuandikwa kama ilivyo katika lugha ya Igala ambapo maneno fulani yanaweza kuandikwa bila mchanganyiko wa sauti za konsonanti na sauti za konsonanti kama inavyotumika kwa vokali nne, a, e. i, u ambazo kwa kusimama peke yake humaanisha kitu. Hii kwa kiasi kikubwa ni matokeo ya ukweli kwamba, lugha ya Kiigala ina mfumo mpana wa toni.

4 Limitation of the Study

4 Mipaka ya Utafiti

As an under-resourced indigenous language whose contextual study as a culture transmitting agent is relatively new (Ojoajogwu, 2017), approaches towards formalization of the Igala language in a more modern sense has been done scantily but also indiscriminately in ways that are devoid of any serious scientific consideration, more so that those who made attempt at writing the language are either not language specialist who understand scientific rudiments regarding the rules of language as it relates with grammar and tone pattern and structure, or were individuals who were too hasty in a way that deep thinking regarding the depth and breadth of both grammar and tones contained in the language were not attended to at great depth. It was therefore not surprising that among some of the available materials that contain words, sentences and meanings, one often see variations in diacritic forms used to demonstrate tones and pronunciation from scholar to scholar and in some cases certain diacritic orthographies that can represent some tones are left out. Added to this is also a variation with respect to accent and tribal differences in tones from one area to the other which has further influence the work output of the various writers. This factor therefore contribute to the first limitation of this very study, because understanding the inherent flaws regarding the inappropriate diacritic orthographies adopted by other writers, the very writer developed separate tools representing several tones, which while being scientifically logical, nonetheless, confine exclusively not to any previous methods used by the earlier writers.

Sababu hii ndiyo inayochangia upungufu wa kwanza wa utafiti huu, kwa sababu kwa kuelewa makosa yaliyomo katika matamshi yasiyofaa yaliyofanywa na waandishi wengine, mwandishi mwenyewe alitengeneza zana tofauti zinazowakilisha toni kadhaa, ambazo ingawa ni za kimantiki kisayansi, hata hivyo, hazihusiani kikamilifu na mbinu zozote zilizotumiwa na waandishi wa awali.

Although, prior to this current work, no effort was made to study the oral form of the Igala language from the basic in a more comprehensive form, such as is done in this paper, a fact which accounted for the neglect of some vowel forms represented by special symbols as stated above. But vowels were not the only orthographies left out for even consonant orthographies that present a single symbol as a unit of consonant cluster such as the ‘c’ with an over dot, i.e.; ċ, or the caron or hacek as it is at times called; č, both of whom represent the sound /ch/ in other world languages, as with respect to the English word ‘church’, and the symbol ŋ which is supposed to represent a single unit of the cluster /ng/ as in the word ‘ānyīngā’ (finger nail) which ought to have been ‘āɲīŋā’ or the ‘ny’ cluster (ɲ) is hardly used. While the non-use of these orthographies by previous writer posed some limitation with respect to how this very writer would have written syllables that should carry these orthographies, it was also a limitation of this study because this very writer failed to do otherwise from what has been held down for so long owing to English influence on the writing of the Igala language. While the tilde ñ which bear the /nn/ cluster and which is occasionally used by some of the earlier writers, albeit with some misconception here and there, in this work, words boiling around this very sign were not used and for that reason, the sign was bypassed, but in future attempt, it shall be treated with special consideration.

Added to the above two limitations is the fact that some orthographies representing vowels do not currently exist to exhibit these sounds on the IPA list of symbols on Microsoft word as pointed out in the body of the work, or perhaps these symbols exists but because of the limited knowledge of this author regarding how some of the symbols are pronounced, they could not be accounted for by the author. This fact also poses a limitation of its own. Added to this missing vowels or undiscovered vowels by this author is the absence of a single symbol for the consonant clusters ‘kw’, ‘kp’, nw, and ‘gh’. This writer is aware that the over dotted p (ṗ), is currently used with respect to writing the Yoruba language, a development that some writers of Igala language have become accustomed to with respect to writing the ‘kp’ cluster as one would have with the Yoruba name ‘Tōṗē’ which is pronounced as /topke/ or /tōkpē/, but this symbolization of the /kp/ sound with ‘ṗ’ calls for question since it is not the first phone on the cluster. Could it then be that this symbolization is the result of the fact that, there is no over dotted ‘k’ on the IPA list of symbols or because the ‘p’ in the combination forms a more voiced phoneme than the ‘k’ or it is because the people by choice, chose to write like that? As for the ‘kw’ cluster, should the under lined ḵ (the qaph) merely remain as ق as it is in the Arabic orthography or another use as a single unit for presenting the ‘kw’ cluster be found in it within the Latin orthographic symbols for those languages whose writing systems are still evolving?
