About

Talitha makes open-access research on African language technology readable in French and Swahili, so that the people these papers are about can read them in a language they use every day. Each paper keeps its original text, and the translations sit next to it. The site now holds the papers of the RAIL workshop (Resources for African Indigenous Languages) from 2020 to 2024. More years, and the AfricaNLP workshop, follow as their translations are checked.

How the translations are made

The text is taken from each paper's PDF and split into sentences. Several open models translate every sentence: MADLAD-400, OPUS-MT, Mistral Small (French) and Qwen (Swahili). Automatic quality estimation (AfriCOMET and LaBSE) and a set of checks pick one candidate per sentence. The checks catch lost numbers and links, copied or truncated sentences, and African language names that went missing or changed. Sentences the checks were unsure about are marked on every translated page.

Nobody has reviewed these translations yet. Figures, tables and layout are not reproduced exactly. The original paper is always the reference version, and every page links to it.

The name

Talitha is a star in the front paw of the Great Bear. Its name comes from the Arabic al-qafza al-thālitha, the third leap of the gazelle: Arab astronomers saw three pairs of stars under the Great Bear as the hoof prints of a leaping gazelle, and Talitha marks the third.

A translation is a leap too, from one language into another, and this project makes the jump for research written about African languages so it can land with the people who speak them. The Great Bear is also a quiet nod to home: arktos is Greek for bear, and Talitha is made by Arktos AI Lab.

Corrections and contact

If you are an author, or you read French or Swahili and spot a mistake, write to info@arktosailab.com. Authors who would rather not have their paper translated can ask for it to be removed.
