| **Abstract** | **Muhtasari** |
| Sentiment analysis focuses on the automatic detection and classification of opinions expressed in texts. Emojis can be used to determine the sentiment polarities of the texts (i.e. positive, negative, or neutral). Several studies demonstrated how sentiment analysis is accurate when emojis are used (Kaity and Balakrishnan, 2020). While they have used emojis as features to improve the performance of sentiment analysis systems, in this paper we analyse the use of emojis to reduce the manual effort in labelling text for training those systems. Furthermore, we investigate the manual effort reduction in the sentiment labelling process with the help of sentiment-bearing words as well as the combination of sentiment-bearing words and emojis. In addition to English, we evaluated the approaches with the low-resource African languages Sepedi, Setswana, and Sesotho. The combination of emojis and words sentiment lexicon shows better performance compared to emojis-only lexicons and words-based lexicons. Our results show that our emoji sentiment lexicon approach is effective, with an accuracy of 75% more than other sentiment lexicon approaches, which have an average accuracy of 69.1%. Furthermore, our distant supervision method obtained an accuracy of 77.0%. We anticipate that only 23% of the tweets will need to be changed as a result of our annotation strategies. | Uchambuzi wa hisia hulenga katika utambuzi wa kiotomatiki na uainishaji wa maoni yanayoonyeshwa katika maandishi. Emoji zinaweza kutumika kubainisha mielekeo ya hisia za maandishi (yaani, chanya, hasi, au isiyoegemea upande wowote). Utafiti kadhaa ulionyesha jinsi uchambuzi wa hisia unavyokuwa sahihi wakati emojis zinapotumika (Kaity na Balakrishnan, 2020). Ingawa wametumia emoji kama vipengele ili kuboresha utendaji wa mifumo ya uchambuzi wa hisia, katika makala hii tunachambua matumizi ya emoji ili kupunguza juhudi za kibinadamu katika kuweka lebo kwenye maandishi kwa ajili ya kufunza mifumo hiyo. Zaidi ya hayo, tunachunguza upunguzaji wa juhudi za kibinadamu katika mchakato wa kuweka lebo za hisia kwa msaada wa maneno yenye hisia pamoja na mchanganyiko wa maneno yenye hisia na emoji. Mbali na Kiingereza, tulitathmini mbinu hizo kwa Lugha za Afrika zenye rasilimali chache Sepedi, Setswana, na Sesotho. | Recent efforts have been made to address the challenges of sentiment analysis for under-resourced languages (Mabokela et al., 2022a; Abdullah and Rusli, 2021). Recently, SAfriSenti corpus (Mabokela and Schlippe, 2022b; Mabokela et al., 2022b) was created -it is a multilingual sentiment corpus for South African under-resourced languages. SAfriSenti Corpus is the largest Twitter sentiment corpus to date, with the goal of addressing the challenges of 11 South African languages. | Juhudi za hivi karibuni zimefanywa kushughulikia changamoto za uchambuzi wa hisia kwa lugha zisizo na rasilimali za kutosha (Mabokela et al., 2022a; Abdullah na Rusli, 2021). NaijaSenti Corpus) (Muhammad et al., 2022; Alabi et al., 2022) Kiswahili (Martin et al., 2021), lahaja za Tunisia (Medhaffar et al., 2017) na Bambara (Diallo et al., 2021) zimechunguzwa kwa uchambuzi wa hisia. (Chen et al., 2018) learnt bi-sense emoji embeddings and train an attention-based LSTM for sentiment classification. By considering only the positive and negative descriptions for each Unicode emoji, the fine-tuning of emoji embeddings can be expedited. However, (Singh et al., 2019) proposed a straightforward method for processing emojis by replacing emojis with their descriptions in tweets and using a pre-trained word embedding strategy that is similar to that of the standard words. Furthermore (Liu et al., 2021) examined and evaluated the impact of supplementing emojis as additional features to improve the sentiment analysis performance. They developed an improved emoji-embedding model based on Bi-LSTM which in turn achieved the best sentiment analysis accuracy on online Chinese texts. | Baadhi ya tafiti zilijaribu kujifunza upachikaji wa emoji ili kukamilisha upachikaji wa maneno ya maandishi kwa kazi za uainishaji wa hisia (Grover, 2021). | **4.1 Words-Based Sentiment Lexicon** | **4.1 Maneno-Baseed Sentenment Lexicon** |
| Numerous sentiment lexicons have been produced in various ways, including; manual creation—which is deemed to be a time-consuming and expensive process, and automatic and semi-automatic. Sentiment lexica are typically lists of words with values assigned to them that indicate the word's sentiment. Typically, these are integer values that express the polarity and intensity of the polarity as increasing or decreasing absolute values. For example, values usually range from -5: (very negative) to -1: (weakly negative) and +5: (very positive) to +1: (weakly positive). Sentiment lexicons have been used in many sentiment systems to help determine the semantic orientation of the texts (Nielsen, 2011; Hutto and Gilbert, 2015). | Leksika nyingi za hisia zimetolewa kwa njia mbalimbali, zikiwemo; uundaji kwa mikono—ambao unachukuliwa kuwa mchakato unaotumia muda mwingi na wa gharama kubwa, na uundaji wa kiotomatiki na nusu-otomatiki. Leksika za hisia kwa kawaida ni orodha za maneno yenye thamani zilizopewa ambazo zinaonyesha hisia ya neno. Kwa kawaida, hizi ni thamani kamili zinazoelezea upolariti na ukali wa upolariti kama thamani kamili zinazoongezeka au kupungua. Kwa mfano, thamani kwa kawaida hupatikana kutoka -5: (hasi sana) hadi -1: (hasi dhaifu) na +5: (chanya sana) hadi +1: (chanya dhaifu). Leksika za hisia zimetumika katika mifumo mingi ya hisia kusaidia kubainisha mwelekeo wa semantiki wa maandishi (Nielsen, 2011; Hutto na Gilbert, 2015). |

| **6 Conclusion** | **6 Hitimisho** |
| In this paper, we describe the different sentiment labelling strategies that involve the utilisation of emojis and words to automatically prelabel tweets for low-resource languages. Additionally, we utilised the SentiLexicon<sub>words+morph</sub> plus the sentiment taggers to perform sentiment labelling. We create our SentiEmojiLex<sub>emojis</sub> from the existing manually annotated tweets in the SAfriSenti corpus-a multilingual Twitter sentiment corpus for South African languages (i.e. Sepedi, Setswana, Sesotho and English) which will later be extended to other South African languages. We created our SentiEmojiLex<sub>emojis</sub> by extracting only the tweets that contain emojis and converting the emojis to their corresponding textual descriptions. Furthermore, we leverage the approach by (Gavilanes et al., 2018) to develop our emoji sentiment lexicon. We achieved better accuracy and F<sub>1</sub> score with our DistSuper<sub>emojis+words</sub>. Comparing our SentiLexicon<sub>words+morph</sub> with the sentiment-bearing words lexicon, our results show that the SentiEmojiLex<sub>emojis</sub> strategy is more effective and reliable. In addition, by comparing our labelling strategies to existing emoji sentiment lexicons, we obtained comparable results with an accuracy of 75% for SentiEmojiLex<sub>emojis</sub> and 77% of accuracy for the DistSuper<sub>emojis+words</sub> approach. Furthermore, we used the CombSentiLex<sub>emojis+words</sub> and DistSuper<sub>emojis+words</sub> approaches to label the remaining tweets in the SAfriSenti corpus (i.e. 32% (16,215 tweets)). Therefore, developing an automatic sentiment annotation strategy for tweets with emojis is more likely to reduce human annotation effort. Additionally, these methodologies can be readily adapted to other under-resourced African languages, provided the data gathered contains emojis. Our future endeavors include leveraging emoji embedding to formulate context-sensitive sentiment labeling techniques for specialized systems. Moreover, we aim to enhance sentiment classification by incorporating various active learning approaches that incorporate emojis. | Katika makala haya, tunaeleza mikakati tofauti ya kuweka lebo za hisia ambayo inahusisha matumizi ya emoji na maneno ili kuweka lebo awali kiotomatiki kwenye twiti kwa lugha zenye rasilimali chache. Zaidi ya hayo, tulitumia SentiLexicon<sub>words+morph</sub> pamoja na vitambulishi vya hisia kufanya uwekaji wa lebo za hisia. Tunaunda SentiEmojiLex<sub>emojis</sub> yetu kutoka kwenye twiti zilizopo zilizofafanuliwa kwa mikono katika korasi ya SAfriSenti—korasi ya hisia za Twitter ya lugha nyingi kwa Lugha za Afrika Kusini (yaani Sepedi, Setswana, Sesotho na Kiingereza) ambayo baadaye itapanuliwa kwa Lugha nyingine za Afrika Kusini. Tuliunda SentiEmojiLex<sub>emojis</sub> yetu kwa kutoa tu twiti zilizo na emoji na kubadilisha emoji hizo kuwa maelezo yake ya kimaandishi yanayolingana. Zaidi ya hayo, tunatumia mbinu ya (Gavilanes et al., 2018) kuendeleza kamusi yetu ya hisia za emoji. Tulipata Usahihi bora na alama ya F<sub>1</sub> kwa DistSuper<sub>emojis+words</sub> yetu. Tukilinganisha SentiLexicon<sub>words+morph</sub> yetu na leksikoni ya maneno yenye hisia, matokeo yetu yanaonyesha kuwa mkakati wa SentiEmojiLex<sub>emojis</sub> ni bora zaidi na wa kuaminika. Zaidi ya hayo, kwa kulinganisha mikakati yetu ya kuweka lebo na leksikoni zilizopo za hisia za emoji, tulipata matokeo yanayolingana kwa Usahihi wa 75% kwa SentiEmojiLex<sub>emojis</sub> na Usahihi wa 77% kwa mkabala wa DistSuper<sub>emojis+words</sub>. Zaidi ya hayo, tulitumia mbinu za CombSentiLex<sub>emojis+words</sub> na DistSuper<sub>emojis+words</sub> kuweka lebo kwenye twiti zilizobaki katika korasi ya SAfriSenti (yaani 32% (16,215 twiti)). Kwa hiyo, kuunda mkakati wa kiotomatiki wa uwekaji lebo wa hisia kwa twiti zenye emoji kuna uwezekano mkubwa wa kupunguza juhudi za binadamu za uwekaji lebo. Aidha, mbinu hizi zinaweza kutumika kwa urahisi kwa lugha zingine za Afrika ambazo hazina rasilimali za kutosha, ikizingatiwa kuwa data iliyokusanywa ina emoji. Juhudi zetu za baadaye zinajumuisha kutumia upachikaji wa emoji kuunda mbinu za uwekaji lebo wa hisia zinazozingatia muktadha kwa mifumo maalumu. Zaidi ya hayo, tunalenga kuboresha uainishaji wa hisia kwa kujumuisha mbinu mbalimbali za ujifunzaji tendaji zinazojumuisha emoji. |

| Tswana, known by its indigenous name Setswana, is a Bantu language spoken in Southern Africa by approximately 8.2 million individuals. It belongs to the Bantu language family within the Sotho-Tswana branch of Zone S, and shares close ties with the Northern Sotho, Southern Sotho, Kgalagadi, and Lozi languages. Setswana is an official language in Botswana and South Africa and serves as a lingua franca in Botswana and certain parts of South Africa, particularly in the North West Province. Tswana-speaking ethnic groups can be found across more than two provinces in South Africa, mainly in the North West, where approximately four million people speak the language. | Tswana, inayojulikana kwa jina lake la asili Setswana, ni lugha ya Kibantu inayozungumzwa Afrika Kusini na takriban watu milioni 8.2. | Sesotho, also referred to as Southern Sotho, is a Southern Bantu language belonging to the Sotho-Tswana ("S.30") group. It is primarily spoken in Lesotho, where it serves as both the national and official language, as well as in South Africa (particularly in the Vaal and Free State), where it is one of the 11 official languages. It is also recognized as one of the 16 official languages of Zimbabwe. As with all Bantu languages, Sesotho is an agglutinative language that utilizes numerous affixes, and derivational and inflectional rules to construct complete words. | Sesotho, ambayo pia inajulikana kama Southern Sotho, ni lugha ya Bantu ya Kusini inayotoka kwenye kikundi cha Sotho-Tswana ("S.30").
