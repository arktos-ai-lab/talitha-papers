| **Abstract** | **Muhtasari** |
| The South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) has proven to be a valuable tool for data analysis in the multilingual context of South Africa, particularly in governmental texts. However, its suitability for broader projects has yet to be determined. This paper aims to assess the performance of the SA-LID in identifying isiXhosa in YouTube comments as part of the methodology for research on the expression of cultural identity through linguistic strategies. We curated a selection of 10 videos which focused on the isiXhosa culture in terms of theatre, poetry, language learning, culture, or music. The videos were predominantly in English as were most of the comments but the latter were interspersed with elements of isiXhosa, identifying the commentators as speakers of isiXhosa. The SA-LID was used to identify all instances of the use of isiXhosa to facilitate the analysis of the relevant items. Following the application of the SA-LID to this data, a manual evaluation was conducted to gauge the effectiveness of this tool in selecting all isiXhosa items. Our findings reveal significant limitations in the use of the SA-LID, encompassing the oversight of unconventional spellings in indigenous languages and misclassification of closely related languages within the Nguni group. Although proficient in identifying the use of Nguni languages, differentiating within this language group proved challenging for the SA-LID. These results underscore the necessity for manual checks to complement the use of the SA-LID when other Nguni languages may be present in the comment texts. | Kitambulishi cha Lugha cha Afrika Kusini (SA-LID) kimethibitika kuwa zana muhimu kwa uchanganuzi wa data katika muktadha wa lugha nyingi wa Afrika Kusini, hasa katika maandishi ya kiserikali. | **2.1. The isiXhosa Language** | **2.1. Lugha ya isiXhosa** |
| While there are between 24 and 30 spoken languages in South Africa (Finlayson and Madiba, 2002), the constitution of the Republic of South Africa recognises 12 of these as official languages (Republic of South Africa, 1996, 2023). These official languages are typically grouped into six language groups, including: (i) South African Sign Language, (ii) Sotho-Tswana, which includes Sesotho, Setswana, and Sepedi, (iii) Sotho-Makua-Venda, which includes Tshivenḓa, (iv) West Germanic, which includes Afrikaans and English, (v) NguniTsonga, which includes Xitsonga, and (vi) Nguni, which includes isiZulu, Siswati, siNdebele and isiXhosa. | Ingawa kuna kati ya lugha 24 na 30 zinazozungumzwa Afrika Kusini (Finlayson and Madiba, 2002), katiba ya Jamhuri ya Afrika Kusini inatambua 12 kati ya hizo kama lugha rasmi (Republic of South Africa, 1996, 2023). Lugha hizi rasmi kwa kawaida zimepangwa katika vikundi sita vya lugha, vikijumuisha: (i) Lugha ya Ishara ya Afrika Kusini, (ii) Kisotho-Tswana, kinachojumuisha Kisotho, Kitswana, na Kipedi, (iii) Kisotho-Makua-Venda, kinachojumuisha Tshivenḓa, (iv) Kijerumaniki cha Magharibi, kinachojumuisha Kiafrikana na Kiingereza, (v) KinguniTsonga, kinachojumuisha Xitsonga, na (vi) Kinguni, kinachojumuisha isiZulu, Siswati, siNdebele na isiXhosa. |
| The Nguni language group occupies a significant position as the largest language group in South Africa. IsiXhosa, the second-most prominent Nguni language within South Africa, (Wheeler, 2018), is predominantly spoken in the Eastern Cape and the Western Cape Provinces. Notably, it is also officially recognised in Zimbabwe (Republic of Zimbabwe, 2021). According to Wheeler (2018), isiXhosa has much in common with isiZulu in terms of their linguistic roots. In fact, as discussed later in Section 4.3, isiXhosa demonstrates close linguistic ties and mutual intelligibility with other languages in the Nguni group as well (Dyers, 2000). Additionally, isiXhosa stands out for its use of clicks, a feature present in only about 0.5% of the world's languages (Brenzinger and Shah, 2023), including a few Bantu languages. These clicks are represented by the use of three consonants: /c, q, and x/ (Nogwina et al., 2013; Gxowa-Dlayedwa, 2015, 2018; Wheeler, 2018). | Kundi la lugha la Nguni linashikilia nafasi muhimu kama kundi kubwa zaidi la lugha nchini Afrika Kusini. IsiXhosa, lugha ya pili kwa umaarufu ya Nguni ndani ya Afrika Kusini, (Wheeler, 2018), inazungumzwa zaidi katika Mikoa ya Rasi ya Mashariki na Rasi ya Magharibi. Inafaa kuzingatia, pia inatambuliwa rasmi nchini Zimbabwe (Republic of Zimbabwe, 2021). Kulingana na Wheeler (2018), isiXhosa ina mambo mengi yanayofanana na isiZulu kuhusu mizizi yao ya kilugha. Kwa hakika, kama inavyojadiliwa baadaye katika Sehemu ya 4.3, isiXhosa inaonyesha uhusiano wa karibu wa kilugha na uelewano wa pande zote na lugha nyingine katika kundi la Nguni pia (Dyers, 2000). Zaidi ya hayo, isiXhosa inajitokeza kwa matumizi yake ya milio ya kubofya, sifa inayopatikana katika takriban asilimia 0.5 tu ya lugha za dunia (Brenzinger and Shah, 2023), ikijumuisha Lugha chache za Kibantu. Milio hii ya kubofya inawakilishwa kwa matumizi ya konsonanti tatu: /c, q, and x/ (Nogwina et al., 2013; Gxowa-Dlayedwa, 2015, 2018; Wheeler, 2018). |

| The initial step in comprehending written text is to ascertain the language in which it is written (Babhulgaonkar and Sonavane, 2020). Various language identification tools are developed for this purpose, with the goal of discerning the language(s) present in the text (Jauhiainen et al., 2024). Note that these language identifiers are designed to encompass both speech and written texts. However, due to the inherent differences between written text, composed of discrete characters, and speech, which involves a continuous signal relying on acoustic features, different natural language processing methods are traditionally employed for text and speech, resulting in limited methodological overlap between the two (Murthy and Kumar, 2006; Ambikairajah et al., 2011). | Hatua ya awali katika kuelewa maandishi ni kubaini lugha ambayo yameandikwa (Babhulgaonkar and Sonavane, 2020). Zana mbalimbali za utambuzi wa lugha zimetengenezwa kwa madhumuni haya, kwa lengo la kubaini lugha zilizopo katika maandishi (Jauhiainen et al., 2024). Kumbuka kwamba vitambulishi hivi vya lugha vimeundwa kujumuisha usemi na maandishi. Hata hivyo, kutokana na tofauti za kiasili kati ya maandishi, yanayoundwa na herufi za pekee, na usemi, unaohusisha ishara endelevu inayotegemea sifa za akustika, mbinu tofauti za uchakataji wa lugha asilia hutumika kijadi kwa maandishi na usemi, hivyo kusababisha mwingiliano mdogo wa kimbinu kati ya hivyo viwili (Murthy na Kumar, 2006; Ambikairajah et al., 2011). |
| For the purpose of this discussion, our focus is specifically on language identifiers for written texts. Text language identification involves analyzing written linguistic features, including character n-grams and word frequency patterns. This analytical process often makes use of statistical models and machine learning algorithms (Nezhadi et al., 2017). | Kwa madhumuni ya mjadala huu, lengo letu ni hasa vitambulishi vya lugha kwa maandishi. Utambuzi wa lugha ya maandishi unahusisha kuchanganua sifa za kiisimu za maandishi, ikiwemo n-gramu za herufi na mifumo ya marudio ya maneno. Simply put, humans are unable to detect languages that are outside their current linguistic repertoires. | Kwa kawaida, binadamu huchukuliwa kama watambulishaji sahihi zaidi wa lugha (Deshwal et al., 2020). | **2.3. Language Identification Tools** | **2.3. Zana za Utambuzi wa Lugha** |
| The introduction of automatic language identifiers serves as a valuable advancement in language detection. The primary task of a language identification tool is to analyse a given spoken or written text and generate a prediction of the language in which the text is spoken or written (Navrátil, 2006; Bergsma et al., 2012; Solorio et al., 2014). This process includes assessing the probability of each word in the provided text as belonging to one or more of the languages in the tool's library (Lui and Baldwin, 2012). The identified language is determined by the highest probability, initiating a competition among language models to determine the most likely match for the entire sample. Like humans, automatic language identifiers also rely on libraries (Agarwal et al., 2023). In this way, the automatic tools need to be trained using different languages and will not be able to detect new languages that are not in their existing libraries. | Kuanzishwa kwa vitambuzi vya lugha otomatiki kunatumika kama maendeleo muhimu katika utambuzi wa lugha. Kazi kuu ya zana ya utambuzi wa lugha ni kuchanganua maandishi yaliyotamkwa au kuandikwa na kutoa utabiri wa lugha ambayo maandishi hayo yametamkwa au kuandikwa (Navrátil, 2006; Bergsma et al., 2012; Solorio et al., 2014). Mchakato huu unajumuisha kutathmini uwezekano wa kila neno katika maandishi yaliyotolewa kuwa ni mali ya lugha moja au zaidi katika maktaba ya zana (Lui na Baldwin, 2012). Lugha iliyotambuliwa huamuliwa na uwezekano wa juu zaidi, ikianzisha ushindani kati ya miundo ya lugha ili kubaini ulinganifu unaowezekana zaidi kwa sampuli nzima. Kama binadamu, vitambulishi vya lugha vya kiotomatiki pia hutegemea maktaba (Agarwal et al., 2023). Kwa njia hii, zana za kiotomatiki zinahitaji kufunzwa kwa kutumia lugha tofauti na hazitaweza kutambua lugha mpya ambazo hazipo katika maktaba zao zilizopo. |
| In this paper, as indicated in Section 1, we conduct a qualitative evaluation of the South African Language Identifier (henceforth SA-LID). The SA-LID was designed to classify text into one of the 11 official written languages of South Africa, either at the document or line level (Puttkammer et al., 2018). The SA-LID has been trained using government text corpora obtained during the National Centre for Human Language Technology (NCHLT) Text project and collected through collaboration between the South African Department of Arts and Culture and the Centre for Text Technology (Puttkammer et al., 2018). | Katika makala haya, kama ilivyoonyeshwa katika Sehemu ya 1, tunafanya tathmini ya kimaelezo ya Kitambulishi cha Lugha cha Afrika Kusini (hapa kikijulikana kama SA-LID). SA-LID iliundwa kuainisha maandishi katika mojawapo ya lugha 11 rasmi za maandishi za Afrika Kusini, ama katika kiwango cha hati au cha mstari (Puttkammer et al., 2018). SA-LID imefunzwa kwa kutumia korasi za maandishi za serikali zilizopatikana wakati wa mradi wa Maandishi wa Kituo cha Kitaifa cha Teknolojia ya Lugha ya Binadamu (NCHLT) na kukusanywa kupitia ushirikiano kati ya Idara ya Sanaa na Utamaduni ya Afrika Kusini na Kituo cha Teknolojia ya Maandishi (Puttkammer et al., 2018). |
| The SA-LID uses feature extraction, identifying language-specific patterns through the analysis of character n-grams, ranging from bigrams to 6-grams. The model was trained using the Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier, incorporating labelled training samples and the selected feature extractor. These components collectively enable the model to discern languages effectively based on the extracted features. In the subsequent step of text classification, the trained classifier is applied to text inputs, resulting in a list of probable languages arranged by their respective probabilities. The final language determination is achieved by selecting the language with the highest probability, based on the model's consideration of learned patterns and characteristics during the training process, ensuring accurate identification of the language in the given text. | SA-LID hutumia uchimbaji wa sifa, ikitambua mifumo mahususi ya lugha kupitia uchanganuzi wa n-gramu za herufi, kuanzia bigramu hadi 6-gramu. Mfano huo ulifunzwa kwa kutumia kiainishaji cha Multinomial Naive Bayes, kikijumuisha sampuli za mafunzo zilizo na lebo na kichimbuzi cha vipengele kilichochaguliwa. | As we analyzed the comments, we observed a diverse array of languages employed by commentators, including code-switching between indigenous South African languages as well as the unexpected occurrences of Japanese, Russian, and Arabic. | **5. Conclusion** | **5. Hitimisho** |
| In this paper, we explore the use of automatic language identification using the South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) to discern languages within a YouTube comments corpus. This study forms part of a broader project aiming to uncover linguistic strategies employed by isiXhosa speakers in expressing their language and cultural identities in YouTube comments. As digital platforms continue to shape communication patterns, understanding language identities becomes crucial for fostering inclusive and accurate representation. To facilitate this, there is a need for accurate language identification in multilingual texts. As such, the context of our broader study led us to evaluate the reliability of the SA-LID in identifying any use of isiXhosa language elements in the relevant comments which we mined from YouTube. The question which underpinned our research, as reflected in Section 1, related to whether we could rely on the language identification results generated through the use of the SA-LID to accurately identify all instances of the use of isiXhosa. | Katika makala hii, tunachunguza matumizi ya utambuzi wa lugha kiotomatiki kwa kutumia South African Language Identifier (SA-LID) ili kubaini lugha ndani ya korasi ya maoni ya YouTube.
